# Risankizumab: From Plaque Psoriasis to Dermatitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Risankizumab (Skyrizi) is an IL-23 p19 inhibitor marketed in Canada and approved for psoriasis, among other inflammatory conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dermatitis**, but only **1 completed Phase 2 RCT** (in atopic dermatitis) and a small body of case-level literature support this. Mechanistic fit is weak, so this is a research question rather than a ready candidate.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. The literature describes risankizumab as a humanised IgG monoclonal antibody that targets the p19 subunit of IL-23 (PMID 31098898). IL-23/Th17 signalling is central to psoriasis and plausibly contributes to some other inflammatory skin diseases.

The link to dermatitis is only partial. Atopic dermatitis is mainly Th2-driven, with possible Th22 and Th17 involvement. That is the rationale the Phase 2 study used for IL-23 blockade (PMID 36588137). The label "dermatitis" is also broad and mixes distinct entities. The published reports mostly show activity in IL-23/Th17-type skin disease, such as erythrodermic psoriasis and acrodermatitis continua of Hallopeau, rather than in atopic dermatitis itself.

The very high TxGNN score is a model prediction and does not replace clinical evidence. The Phase 2 atopic dermatitis RCT exists, but its primary endpoint result is not summarised in the Evidence Pack and must be checked against the full publication before any efficacy conclusion is drawn.

## Safety Considerations

- **Paradoxical eczema:** Eczematous eruptions during IL-23 inhibitor treatment, including risankizumab, are reported in the literature, with an estimated incidence of 1-3% in psoriasis patients on biologics. This is relevant when considering use in dermatitis.
- **Drug interactions:** No interactions were found in the queried database.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

---

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
One completed Phase 2 RCT in atopic dermatitis exists, but its efficacy result has not been confirmed here. The mechanistic fit with Th2-driven disease is weak, and paradoxical eczema is a reported risk. The other nine predicted indications are model predictions only (L5), with no trials or literature, and the data suggest several are likely knowledge-graph artifacts. Acrodermatitis chronica atrophicans is a Borrelia infection, and the two pityriasis lichenoides entries are probably duplicate nodes.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review the primary endpoint and safety results of NCT03706040 / PMID 36588137
- Narrow "dermatitis" to a specific subtype (e.g., atopic, or IL-23/Th17-type eczematous disease) before further evaluation
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications
- Canadian approved-indication text and dosage forms for the five DINs
- Detailed MOA data from DrugBank
