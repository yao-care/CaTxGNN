# Efavirenz: From HIV-1 Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Efavirenz is a non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI) used to treat HIV-1 infection.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, backed by **1 clinical trial** (withdrawn, no data) and **15 publications**, all preclinical.
The prediction most likely reflects the RT-SHIV macaque research model rather than a real human therapeutic use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, efavirenz is an NNRTI that targets HIV-1 reverse transcriptase. Its efficacy in HIV-1 infection is established, and mechanistically it might be applicable to lentiviral infections that carry a similar reverse transcriptase.

Wild-type SIV is generally not sensitive to NNRTIs. The macaque literature instead uses **RT-SHIV**, an SIV chimera in which the reverse transcriptase is replaced by the HIV-1 version, which makes it efavirenz-sensitive. Several of the publications listed below use this model, with efavirenz alone or as part of combination antiretroviral therapy. The high TxGNN score probably reflects this heavy presence in the literature.

SIV is a non-human primate virus, so there is no human clinical use case. The evidence only indirectly informs HIV-1 treatment, which is already the approved use. It does not support a new therapeutic indication.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only clinical trial is withdrawn with no participants, and all publications are preclinical macaque or in vitro work. SIV has no human therapeutic use, so the prediction most likely reflects the RT-SHIV research model rather than a viable repurposing opportunity.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A clear statement of the intended use case, since SIV infection has no human indication
- Confirmation of the approved indication text for the Canadian DINs
