# Atorvastatin: From Lipid Lowering to Familial Hypercholesterolemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Atorvastatin is a statin (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor) that is marketed in Canada for lowering cholesterol.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Familial Hypercholesterolemia (FH)**, with **35 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction.
This is closer to confirming an established use than to true repurposing, because statins are already standard therapy for FH.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Atorvastatin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, the key enzyme in cholesterol synthesis. This upregulates LDL receptors on liver cells, which clears more LDL-C from the blood. The Evidence Pack lacks a formal mechanism-of-action entry, but this is the well-established mechanism for the statin class.

FH is a genetic condition of markedly elevated LDL-C, most often caused by faulty LDL receptors. Atorvastatin's mechanism matches this problem directly. Response differs by subtype:

- **Heterozygous FH:** Patients keep some functional LDL receptors, so statins work well.
- **Homozygous FH:** Patients with little or no receptor function respond only partly. They usually need add-on drugs such as ezetimibe or PCSK9 inhibitors, and several of the trials below test exactly these combinations.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3 randomized trials involve atorvastatin as monotherapy or background therapy in FH, and the mechanism directly matches the disease. The evidence mostly tests add-on agents rather than atorvastatin alone, and the torcetrapib combination trials ended for safety reasons. Benefit is clearest in heterozygous FH, and homozygous FH generally needs combination therapy.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada product monograph warnings and contraindications, which are not in the current data
- Confirmation of whether FH is already a labelled indication for the Canadian products
- Formal mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Drug interaction review (none retrieved)
- A dosing and monitoring plan that separates heterozygous from homozygous FH, including children and adolescents

**Other predictions in the Evidence Pack:** Other predicted indications (HIV-associated comorbidities, brain stem infarction, and three low-evidence candidates) have weaker support and were not evaluated in this report.
