## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Atorvastatin belongs to the statin class. It lowers LDL cholesterol and triglycerides. Statins also have anti-inflammatory effects that may reduce monocyte and macrophage activation.

People with HIV often develop high lipids, especially when taking antiretroviral therapy (ART) with protease inhibitors. They also have persistent immune activation and a higher cardiovascular risk. Atorvastatin could plausibly help with all three. Several trials test this, looking at immune activation, monocyte activation and plaque inflammation in people with HIV.

The evidence supports managing HIV-associated dyslipidemia and cardiovascular and inflammatory risk. It does not show that atorvastatin treats HIV infection itself. One small randomized trial in people with HIV not on ART looked at viral load, and another small Phase 4 study (terminated, n=5) also looked at viral replication. Neither gives conclusive results here.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The data compiled for this report list 100 recorded interactions.
  - The most important is **clarithromycin (Major)**.
  - Moderate interactions include aprepitant, omeprazole and other proton pump inhibitors (esomeprazole, lansoprazole, pantoprazole), cimetidine, azole antifungals (clotrimazole, miconazole), dexamethasone, metronidazole, naltrexone, eliglustat, eluxadoline, and other statins (rosuvastatin, simvastatin).
  - Antacids such as aluminum hydroxide and magnesium hydroxide are Minor.
- **HIV-specific interaction concern**: The literature and trials highlight CYP3A4-mediated interactions with ritonavir- or cobicistat-boosted protease inhibitors. These raise atorvastatin exposure and the risk of muscle toxicity such as myopathy, so dose caps and monitoring are needed.

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
- Several trials and reviews study atorvastatin in people with HIV, including one completed Phase 3 randomized trial and a larger Phase 3 trial of unknown status. The studies are small, and the benefit shown relates to lipids, inflammation and cardiovascular risk rather than control of HIV itself.
- Only one completed Phase 3 randomized trial is available, so the evidence level is L2.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results from larger, adequately powered trials with immune activation, cardiovascular or viral endpoints (for example the 320-participant trial NCT03037372).
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada, and mechanism-of-action data.
- A dosing and monitoring plan for use with boosted protease inhibitors or cobicistat (dose caps, muscle symptom and liver monitoring).
