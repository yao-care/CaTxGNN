# Amiodarone: From Antiarrhythmic Therapy to Catecholaminergic Polymorphic Ventricular Tachycardia

## One-Sentence Summary

Amiodarone is a well-known antiarrhythmic drug that is marketed in Canada. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (CPVT)**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and **10 publications** retrieved. None of these publications shows that amiodarone works in CPVT, so this prediction is model-driven only.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source record. Based on known information, amiodarone is a Vaughan Williams class III antiarrhythmic. It also has beta-blocking effects and sodium- and calcium-channel effects. This gives it a plausible rationale for suppressing ventricular arrhythmias.

CPVT is a rare inherited channelopathy. Diastolic calcium leak from the RYR2 or CASQ2 pathways triggers ventricular arrhythmia under adrenergic stress. The published CPVT literature centres on beta-blockers, flecainide and sympathetic denervation, not amiodarone. The retrieved papers include case reports of refractory arrhythmia storms and one general review of ventricular arrhythmia pharmacotherapy. None shows amiodarone efficacy in CPVT.

The high TxGNN score most likely reflects the shared "ventricular arrhythmia" neighbourhood in the knowledge graph, not direct evidence for CPVT. The prediction is therefore a hypothesis to test, not a supported new use.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high (99.78%), but no clinical trials exist and none of the retrieved papers shows amiodarone benefit in CPVT. The CPVT literature favours beta-blockers, flecainide and sympathetic denervation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Direct clinical evidence of amiodarone in CPVT, such as case series, registry data or trials
- Mechanism of action data and a mechanistic comparison against the established CPVT treatments
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications
- The Health Canada approved-indication text, to confirm the original labelled uses

**Note:** The same Evidence Pack lists **ventricular tachycardia** (rank 3) with much stronger support: Phase 3 and Phase 4 trials and RCT publications, including PROCAMIO and OPTIC. That use is likely already established, so it may be a label-verification item rather than true repurposing. It could be assessed in a separate report.
