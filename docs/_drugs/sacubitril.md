# Sacubitril: From Cardiovascular Use to Diabetic Nephropathy

## One-Sentence Summary

Sacubitril is a neprilysin inhibitor, marketed in Canada as a single-ingredient product (for example, ENTRESTO) and used in combination with valsartan. The TxGNN model's top-ranked predictions are rare genetic disorders with no supporting evidence. The best-supported candidate is **diabetic nephropathy** (TxGNN rank 3), backed by **1 registered clinical trial** and **15 publications**, mostly preclinical or indirect. This report evaluates diabetic nephropathy as the most actionable prediction. The two highest-scoring predictions have essentially no supporting evidence.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, sacubitril is a prodrug whose active metabolite inhibits neprilysin, an enzyme that breaks down natriuretic peptides. It is marketed together with valsartan, an angiotensin receptor blocker (ARB).

For diabetic nephropathy, the rationale is biologically plausible:

- **Valsartan** blocks the renin-angiotensin system, which is already a cornerstone of diabetic kidney disease management.
- **Sacubitril** raises natriuretic peptide levels, which may reduce glomerular hypertension, oxidative stress, NF-κB-mediated inflammation, and fibrosis.
- Several rodent studies (db/db mice, KKAy mice, Zucker obese rats, streptozotocin-type models) report improved renal injury markers, and a podocyte cell study points to an Nrf2/HO-1 antioxidant pathway.
- A secondary analysis of the PARADIGM-HF trial examined renal function in patients with type 2 diabetes and heart failure, which provides indirect human data.

The other top predictions have weaker support. For brain small vessel disease 1 and the autosomal dominant hematuria-retinal arteriolar tortuosity-contractures syndrome, both rare COL4A1-related basement membrane disorders, no mechanism has been established. Rheumatoid arthritis and hemoglobinopathy likewise show no clear mechanistic link. These are model predictions only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (diabetic nephropathy may be advanced as a research question)

**Rationale:**
- Diabetic nephropathy has a plausible mechanism and consistent preclinical support, but human evidence is indirect and the only registered Phase 4 trial has unknown status and no posted results. This falls short of Phase 2/3 RCT-level proof (L3).
- The two highest-scoring predictions (COL4A1-related disorders) and the rheumatoid arthritis and hemoglobinopathy predictions rest on model scores alone (L5) and should not be pursued.
- Safety label data (warnings and contraindications) could not be retrieved, so safety screening cannot yet proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada, plus approved indication and dosage form details for each DIN
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Results or a status update for NCT06501651, and verification of its exact population and endpoints
- Full-text review of the clinical studies (PMID 37549515, 40416927, and the PARADIGM-HF secondary analysis) to confirm design and renal outcomes
- Safety considerations specific to diabetic nephropathy patients, such as renal function, potassium, and hypotension monitoring, in light of the identified drug interactions
