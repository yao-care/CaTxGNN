## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, mefenamic acid belongs to the NSAID class. It inhibits the cyclooxygenase enzymes (COX-1 and COX-2) and reduces prostaglandin synthesis, which relieves inflammation and pain.

Rheumatoid arthritis is a chronic inflammatory joint disease in which prostaglandin-driven pain, swelling and stiffness are prominent. Mefenamic acid's anti-inflammatory and analgesic effects are therefore mechanistically applicable to symptom control. Published head-to-head and placebo-controlled crossover trials, versus ibuprofen, sulindac, flurbiprofen and indomethacin, support this.

This is an established NSAID class effect, not a novel repurposing signal. The evidence supports symptom control only, not disease modification, and mefenamic acid is not a preferred agent for rheumatoid arthritis today.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text):** hypertension, kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, peptic ulcer disease (including active), blood coagulation disease, myocardial infarction, coronary thrombosis, cardiovascular and heart disease, and hemoglobinopathy.
- **Literature safety signals:** autoimmune haemolytic anaemia (case reports) and severe enteropathy with villous atrophy in prolonged users. Analgesic-associated nephropathy and medication-overuse headache are also reported.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were found in total. Examples at Moderate level include acetylsalicylic acid, corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide), metformin, chlorpropamide, mesalazine, balsalazide, aprepitant and potassium salts. Examples at Minor level include famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine, dapagliflozin and linaclotide.

Please also refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Several small, dated, double-blind trials show mefenamic acid relieves rheumatoid arthritis symptoms, comparable to other NSAIDs. However, this is a class effect with no disease-modifying benefit, no registered trials exist, and better-established agents are available. Safety signals (gastrointestinal toxicity, haemolytic anaemia, renal and cardiovascular cautions) and the missing Canadian label information add further caution.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indication
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Comparison against current standard-of-care rheumatoid arthritis agents
- Consideration of migraine (particularly menstrually related migraine) as a more promising direction, supported by placebo-controlled evidence
