# Sucralfate: From Duodenal Ulcer to Duodenogastric Reflux

## One-Sentence Summary

Sucralfate is an aluminum sucrose sulfate complex historically used as a mucosal-protective agent for duodenal ulcer disease. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Duodenogastric Reflux**, with **0 registered clinical trials** but **13 supporting publications**, including several small randomized controlled trials from the 1980s–2000s. A second candidate indication (duodenal obstruction) was also flagged by the model but rejected internally as mechanistically implausible.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, sucralfate is a topically acting agent that forms a protective, viscous complex over damaged mucosa and adsorbs bile acids and pepsin; its efficacy in duodenal ulcer healing has been well established, and mechanistically this bile-acid-binding and mucosa-coating action may also be applicable to duodenogastric reflux, where retrograde bile and duodenal contents injure the gastric mucosa.

Duodenal ulcer and duodenogastric (alkaline/bile) reflux are pathophysiologically related — both involve mucosal injury within the same anatomical region, and bile reflux is a recognized cause of gastritis often treated empirically with the same mucosal-protective strategies used for ulcer disease. Several of the identified publications directly test sucralfate in alkaline/bile reflux gastritis (post-surgical and primary), which supports the biological plausibility of the TxGNN prediction rather than it being a purely incidental knowledge-graph co-occurrence.

A second candidate, **duodenal obstruction**, was also generated by the model (score 99.30%) but the mechanistic review found no plausible link — sucralfate has no pharmacological action capable of relieving mechanical obstruction, and the associated literature consists mainly of unrelated ulcer/case-report material. This candidate has already been scored **Hold / L5** internally and is not carried forward in this report.
