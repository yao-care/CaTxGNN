## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Ciprofloxacin belongs to the fluoroquinolone class, which kills bacteria by inhibiting DNA gyrase and topoisomerase IV. Its efficacy in bacterial infections is well established. Its mechanistic fit for scleroderma has not been shown.

Two indirect leads link ciprofloxacin to scleroderma:

1. **Possible antifibrotic effect.** A 2010 paper reports that oral ciprofloxacin reduced the severity of scleroderma skin disease. Scleroderma is an autoimmune connective tissue disorder marked by microvascular injury and excessive skin fibrosis, and no effective pharmacological treatment currently exists. The Evidence Pack could not confirm the study's design or size from the title alone.
2. **Gut bacterial overgrowth.** Small bowel bacterial overgrowth is common in systemic sclerosis. An antibacterial drug could plausibly relieve these symptoms. That would be symptom management, not modification of the underlying disease.

The very high TxGNN score is a model prediction only and should not be read as clinical evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Support for scleroderma is limited to two publications, one of them a 1995 cohort about gut bacterial overgrowth, and no registered trials. The evidence level is L4 (Research Question stage), and the high model score alone does not justify moving forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full-text review of the 2010 study (PMID 20507401) to confirm design, sample size, endpoints and whether the antifibrotic result is reproducible
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, which are currently a blocking gap for safety screening
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank to support an antifibrotic hypothesis
- Confirmation of the approved indication text on the Canadian licences

**Note on other candidates:** Among the model's predicted indications, **septicemic plague** (rank 10) has the strongest evidence, at L2. It has a completed Phase 2 randomized trial ([NCT01243437](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01243437), n=200) and a 2025 NEJM-published randomized trial in bubonic plague. Ciprofloxacin is likely already labeled for plague, so that candidate should be checked against the label and assessed separately.
