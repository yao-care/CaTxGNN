# Cyproterone Acetate: From Androgen-Dependent Conditions to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Cyproterone acetate (CPA) is a progestogen with antiandrogen activity. It is marketed in Canada as a single-ingredient product and in combination with ethinyl estradiol, and it is used for androgen-dependent conditions such as acne, hirsutism and prostate cancer.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **3 indirect publications**.
The evidence is weak, and the literature suggests caution because hormonal exposure can trigger or worsen migraine.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, cyproterone acetate is a synthetic progestogen that blocks the androgen receptor (AR). Its efficacy in androgen-dependent conditions and in combined contraceptive use is established, and it may mechanistically be applicable to migraine through hormonal effects on the brain.

Migraine is sensitive to fluctuations in sex hormones. A 2003 review reports that progestins interact with the GABA-A receptor and with dopamine and opioid systems. It also notes that CPA increases dopaminergic responses and binds opiate receptors independently of its classical antiandrogen effect. This gives a plausible but indirect biological link.

None of the retrieved literature shows CPA treating migraine. Hormone exposure can also trigger or worsen migraine, so the direction of effect is uncertain. The high model score may partly reflect shared hormonal and vascular associations rather than therapeutic benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The only interaction record is pharmacological. CPA acts on the androgen receptor (AR), which is its known target and not a drug-drug interaction.
- **Related safety signals from the migraine literature**: Reviews of hormonal contraception describe increased venous thromboembolism and arterial ischemic risk with combined hormonal contraceptives. Migraine with aura is a recognized contraindication for combined hormonal contraceptives. CPA-containing products have been associated with higher thrombotic risk.

For labelled warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score and indirect literature. No trial or paper shows CPA benefiting migraine, and the thrombotic and hormonal risks are a particular concern in migraine, especially migraine with aura.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada, to complete safety screening
- Direct clinical or preclinical evidence of CPA effect on migraine, including whether the effect is beneficial or harmful
- A vascular and thromboembolic risk assessment in migraine populations, especially migraine with aura
