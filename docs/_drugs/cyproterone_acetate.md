# Cyproterone Acetate: From Antiandrogen/Hormonal Therapy to Migraine Disorder

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. CPA is known as an antiandrogen and progestin that suppresses gonadotropins and androgen action. Its efficacy in hormone-driven conditions is established, but its mechanistic link to migraine is weak.

One 2003 review describes some biological plausibility. Progestins interact with the GABA-A receptor, and CPA increases dopaminergic responses and binds opiate receptors independently of its classical hormonal effects. These are neuromodulatory effects that could in theory touch on migraine pathways.

The rest of the literature points the other way. Studies on hormone therapy and migraine show that hormonal treatment affects migraine differently in different women, and it may worsen migraine in some. The high TxGNN score therefore looks like a hormone-related association in the knowledge graph, not evidence that CPA treats migraine.

## Safety Considerations

- **Vascular and thrombotic risk (from literature)**: In the broader literature retrieved for this drug, CPA/ethinylestradiol contraceptives are associated with higher venous thromboembolism risk. Changes such as activated protein C resistance and altered thrombin generation have been reported, and the risk is amplified by factor V Leiden. Migraine with aura is a recognized risk modifier for stroke and thrombosis with hormonal contraception. This matters directly for a migraine population.
- **Long-term safety**: Literature discusses the mutagenic potential of CPA, but a large multicentre study did not find an association.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings, contraindications and drug interaction information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has no clinical trials, no mechanistic support and no CPA-specific efficacy data in migraine. The related literature suggests hormonal therapy may worsen migraine in some women, and CPA-containing products carry a thrombotic risk that is especially relevant to migraine with aura.

Other predicted indications for this drug look more promising. Amenorrhea (L3, 4 trials) is supported by cycle-regulation trials in PCOS and irregular menses, though it may reflect established use rather than new repurposing. Thrombophilia and the other coagulation-related predictions are safety and contraindication signals, not treatment candidates.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, which are a blocking gap for safety screening
- Mechanism of action data, for example from DrugBank
- Drug-specific studies of CPA in migraine, such as case series or controlled trials
- A safety assessment covering thrombotic and vascular risk in patients with migraine, especially migraine with aura
