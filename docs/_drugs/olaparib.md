# Olaparib: From Ovarian Cancer to Female Breast Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Olaparib is a PARP inhibitor originally used to treat ovarian cancer, including BRCA-associated disease.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **female breast carcinoma**,
with **30 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction, including three Phase 3 randomized trials (OlympiA, OlympiAD and its follow-ups) in BRCA-mutated breast cancer.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the known pharmacology of PARP inhibitors, olaparib blocks PARP1/2 and traps PARP on DNA. In tumours with homologous recombination deficiency (HRD), such as those with germline BRCA1/2 mutations, unrepaired single-strand breaks turn into double-strand breaks that the cell cannot fix. This "synthetic lethality" leads to cancer cell death.

The same mechanism underlies olaparib's use in BRCA-associated ovarian cancer. BRCA1/2 mutations also drive a subset of breast cancers, so the biology carries over directly. The strongest support is in germline BRCA1/2-mutated, HER2-negative breast cancer. Support for non-BRCA HRD-related mutations (for example PALB2) is weaker and comes from Phase 2 data only.

The very high TxGNN score is consistent with the clinical evidence, but it does not add independent support beyond it.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Large Phase 3 randomized trials (OlympiA in early breast cancer and OlympiAD in metastatic disease) support olaparib in germline BRCA1/2-mutated, HER2-negative breast cancer, and the mechanism matches its ovarian cancer use. The evidence is strongest for this specific population; for other groups it is weaker. The package insert warnings and contraindications were not available, so the safety review is incomplete.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Obtain and review the Health Canada package insert (warnings, contraindications, approved indications for each DIN)
- Confirm the official mechanism-of-action data
- Limit use to germline BRCA1/2-mutated, HER2-negative breast cancer with confirmed testing
- Review interactions with strong CYP3A inhibitors (e.g. clarithromycin) and with the other major interacting drugs listed above
- Confirm whether the Canadian label already covers breast cancer, and the dosage forms of the two DINs
