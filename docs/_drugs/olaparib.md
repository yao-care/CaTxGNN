# Olaparib: From Ovarian Cancer to Female Breast Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Olaparib is an oral PARP inhibitor, originally used to treat ovarian cancer, particularly BRCA-mutated disease.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **female breast carcinoma**, with **30 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this direction, including several Phase 3 randomized trials (OlympiAD, OlympiA) in germline BRCA-mutated breast cancer.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data was not available in the structured drug record. Based on the literature compiled for this report, olaparib is a PARP inhibitor. It exploits synthetic lethality in tumours with homologous recombination deficiency, particularly those with germline BRCA1/2 mutations.

In BRCA-mutated ovarian cancer, olaparib's efficacy is well established. BRCA-mutated breast cancer shares the same defect in double-strand DNA break repair. These tumour cells depend on PARP-mediated single-strand break repair, so blocking PARP is lethal to them. The mechanism that supports the ovarian indication therefore applies directly to this breast cancer population.

Benefit is biomarker-dependent. In the pivotal trials it was shown in patients with a germline BRCA1/2 mutation and HER2-negative disease, and it should not be assumed for unselected breast cancer. Exploratory Phase 2 data (TBCRC 048) suggest activity in some non-BRCA homologous recombination repair gene mutations, such as PALB2. The very high TxGNN score is consistent with this mechanistic and clinical picture.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screening returned 100 interacting drugs. The most clinically important are those rated **Major**: aprepitant, dexamethasone and clarithromycin. These are likely related to CYP3A-mediated metabolism, so concomitant use needs review. Many others are rated Moderate, including metformin, bupropion, cimetidine, miconazole, clotrimazole, glyburide, ondansetron, repaglinide, rosuvastatin and simvastatin. Prucalopride is rated Minor.

For other safety information, such as warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple Phase 3 randomized trials (OlympiAD and OlympiA) support olaparib in germline BRCA1/2-mutated, HER2-negative breast cancer, and the mechanism matches its established ovarian cancer use. The benefit is biomarker-restricted, so use must be limited to the appropriate genetic population.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation of the approved breast cancer indication in the Canadian product monograph, since indication text for the two DINs was not available
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada
- Detailed mechanism of action data from a structured source
- A germline BRCA1/2 testing pathway to select eligible patients
- A haematological monitoring and drug interaction management plan, particularly for strong CYP3A inhibitors and inducers
