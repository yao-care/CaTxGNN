## Rizatriptan: From Migraine to Migraine with Brainstem Aura
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Rizatriptan is a triptan originally used to treat migraine, including migraine with or without aura.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**,
but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** and only **general migraine literature** (19 publications, none specific to brainstem aura) supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, rizatriptan is a selective 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist (a triptan) with proven efficacy in acute migraine, and it may be mechanistically applicable to related migraine subtypes.

Migraine with brainstem aura is a subtype of migraine with aura. The two share underlying biology, including trigeminovascular activation, CGRP release and cortical spreading depression. This close disease similarity is consistent with the very high TxGNN score.

Two caveats apply. First, the literature found covers general migraine, migraine with aura, menstrual, vestibular and pediatric migraine, but not brainstem aura specifically. Second, triptans have historically carried cautionary labelling in hemiplegic and basilar-type migraine because of a theoretical vasoconstrictive risk. Safety in this subtype needs explicit review before any recommendation.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (these come from disease-drug links, not label text): hypertension, liver disease, gallbladder disease, stroke and cerebrovascular disorders, cerebral infarction, myocardial infarction and coronary thrombosis, myocardial ischaemia, coronary artery disease, Prinzmetal angina, peripheral vascular disease and arterial disease, hemoglobinopathy, chronic kidney disease and end-stage renal failure, and familial or sporadic hemiplegic migraine.
- **Drug Interactions**: 80 interactions were identified. The **Major** ones include:
  - Serotonergic and weight-loss agents: lorcaserin, fenfluramine, dexfenfluramine, sibutramine.
  - 5-HT3 antagonists: ondansetron, granisetron, dolasetron, palonosetron.

  Morphine, including the liposomal form, is rated **Moderate**.
- Several cardiovascular and cerebrovascular contraindications, plus hemiplegic migraine, overlap with the clinical profile of brainstem-aura migraine. This needs careful review.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic link to a closely related migraine subtype is plausible and the TxGNN score is very high. However, no trials or subtype-specific literature support this indication, so it remains a research question. Cautionary labelling and knowledge-graph contraindications around hemiplegic migraine and cerebrovascular disease add safety concerns that have not been resolved.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, which is a blocking gap for safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Subtype-specific evidence (case series, observational studies or trials) in migraine with brainstem aura
- Explicit safety assessment of triptan use in brainstem-aura and basilar-type migraine, including vascular risk screening
