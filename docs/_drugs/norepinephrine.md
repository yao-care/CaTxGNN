## Norepinephrine: Obstructive lung disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Norepinephrine is an injectable adrenergic drug whose original indications include hypotensive disorder, asthma, bronchitis, emphysema and bronchiectasis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **obstructive lung disease**, but the support is thin: **1 loosely related clinical trial** and **about 20 publications**, mostly reviews and physiology studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for norepinephrine: "Norepinephrine is a peripheral vasoconstrictor (alpha-adrenergic action) and an inotropic stimulator of the heart and dilator of coronary arteries (beta-adrenergic action)." ([DailyMed: Norepinephrine Bitartrate In Dextrose (norepinephrine bitartrate) prescribing information, Sagent Pharmaceuticals](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=be3c6f4f-54fc-4cb3-a299-8a022515d396), label version 2026-10-27; accessed 2026-11-19.) Based on general pharmacology, norepinephrine acts on adrenergic receptors (mainly alpha-adrenergic, with some beta effects) and is a potent vasopressor. The reviews retrieved describe adrenergic signalling and sympathetic innervation as part of how airway tone and inflammation are regulated in asthma and COPD, which gives a plausible but indirect biological link.

The predicted indication is also close to the drug's original indications. Asthma, bronchitis, emphysema and bronchiectasis are all obstructive airway conditions, so this is nearer to an on-label-adjacent use than a true repurposing.

The benefit-risk balance is the main concern. Systemic norepinephrine is a strong vasoconstrictor, and non-selective use for airway disease carries risks of hypertension, arrhythmia and tissue ischaemia. A favourable profile for this use has not been established.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (not label text): hypertensive disorder, hypertension and thrombotic disease.
- **Drug Interactions**: 70 interactions were identified in total, all of the 20 listed being Moderate. The main groups are:
  - Antidiabetic drugs: acarbose, metformin, pioglitazone, repaglinide, chlorpropamide, and the DPP-4 inhibitors (sitagliptin, saxagliptin, linagliptin, alogliptin).
  - SGLT2 inhibitors: canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin.
  - GLP-1 agonists: semaglutide, dulaglutide, albiglutide.
  - Insulins: aspart, degludec, detemir.
  - Sympathomimetic anorectics: phentermine, diethylpropion.
- Systemic use may cause hypertension, arrhythmia and tissue ischaemia, as noted in the mechanistic assessment.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but no clinical study tests norepinephrine for obstructive lung disease. The only registered trial is unrelated, and the literature consists of indirect reviews and physiology studies. The cardiovascular risks of systemic use also mean a favourable benefit-risk profile is not established.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada, plus the approved indication text for each DIN
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Clinical evidence (at least a well-designed Phase 2 study) showing benefit in obstructive airway disease
- A safety and route assessment for non-vasopressor, airway-targeted use, including cardiovascular risk mitigation
