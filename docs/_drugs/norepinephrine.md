## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Norepinephrine is a vasopressor with weak beta-2 activity. Endogenous noradrenaline signalling is clearly involved in airway control. Preclinical work links brainstem Dbh+ noradrenergic neurons to allergen-induced airway hyperreactivity (PMID 38987587). Older physiology papers describe elevated plasma noradrenaline and autonomic control of airway calibre in COPD and asthma (PMIDs 6777857, 9009625, 2048831).

These findings describe the body's own noradrenergic activity in lung disease. They do not show that giving exogenous norepinephrine would help. A vasopressor would be expected to raise safety concerns in patients with obstructive lung disease, such as tachyarrhythmia and vasoconstriction. The very high TxGNN score reflects proximity in the knowledge graph, not clinical data. It should be read as a hypothesis-generating signal only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

The TxGNN score is high, but the supporting evidence is limited to indirect mechanistic and physiology literature on endogenous noradrenaline. No retrieved trial tests norepinephrine for obstructive lung disease, and safety data are missing.
