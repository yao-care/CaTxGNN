## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source record. Based on general knowledge, heparin acts by potentiating antithrombin, which inhibits thrombin and factor Xa. This makes an anticoagulant rationale for a prothrombotic condition biologically plausible.

Severe protein C deficiency is an inherited loss of a natural anticoagulant, which leaves patients prone to clotting. In principle, an anticoagulant could offset that tendency. But nothing in the supplied data shows heparin has been tested or shown to help in this disease. The approved indications of the Canadian products are also not listed, so the link to the original use cannot be checked against the source record.

The high model score reflects a network-based association, not clinical proof, and should not be used for decisions on its own.
