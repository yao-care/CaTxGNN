## One-Sentence Summary

Flecainide is a class 1C sodium channel blocker originally used for rhythm control in atrial fibrillation.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant to **stroke disorder**, but the support is indirect: **1 clinical trial** (with an arrhythmia endpoint, not a stroke endpoint) and **20 publications**, mostly about atrial fibrillation rather than stroke itself.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, flecainide is a class 1C antiarrhythmic that blocks sodium channels, and its efficacy in maintaining or restoring normal rhythm in atrial fibrillation is established.

The link to stroke is indirect. Atrial fibrillation is a major cause of cardioembolic stroke, so keeping the heart in sinus rhythm may lower arrhythmia-related stroke risk. No study provided shows that flecainide directly reduces stroke.

There is an important limitation. Class 1C agents are contraindicated in structural or ischaemic heart disease, which describes many stroke patients. This restricts how widely the drug could be applied.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (drug-disease contraindication links, not package insert text): kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, potassium deficiency, myocardial infarction, coronary thrombosis, haemoglobinopathy, atrioventricular block, sick sinus syndrome (including the familial form), and pyoureter.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Major interactions include **dolasetron** and **eliglustat**. Moderate interactions include famotidine, cimetidine, loperamide, bupropion, clarithromycin, levofloxacin, lorcaserin, palonosetron, trospium and several laxatives or bowel preparations. Minor interactions include ascorbic acid, potassium citrate and metronidazole.
- **Safety signals from the literature**: proarrhythmia, particularly in structural or ischaemic heart disease; overdose toxicity, including unmasking of a Brugada pattern; and the need for co-management with anticoagulants in AF patients.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only trial measures arrhythmia after PFO closure rather than stroke, and no study shows that flecainide reduces stroke. The pathway runs indirectly through AF rhythm control. In addition, the contraindication in structural and ischaemic heart disease, which is common in stroke patients, limits real-world applicability. Evidence stands at L3 and the item is best treated as a research question.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results from NCT05213104, and ideally a trial with stroke or cerebrovascular events as the endpoint
- Mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- A defined target population (for example, patients with AF and no structural heart disease) with an anticoagulation co-management plan
