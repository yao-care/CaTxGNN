# Selumetinib: From Neurofibromatosis to Peripheral Nerve Schwannoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Selumetinib is a MEK1/2 inhibitor originally used for neurofibromatosis. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **peripheral nerve schwannoma**, the best-supported of its top 10 predictions. The evidence is early-stage: **3 clinical trials** (one terminated, two not yet recruiting) and **6 publications**, mostly reviews, preclinical work and case reports.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not currently available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, selumetinib is a MEK1/2 inhibitor that blocks the RAS/RAF/MEK/ERK signalling pathway.

In neurofibromatosis, loss of NF1 or NF2 leads to hyperactivation of this pathway in Schwann cells, driving nerve sheath tumour growth. Selumetinib is already approved for NF1 plexiform neurofibromas, a closely related nerve sheath tumour. Schwannomas arise from the same cell lineage and share this signalling dependence, particularly in NF2-related schwannomatosis (NF2-SWN). This gives a plausible mechanistic basis for the prediction.

Reviews and preclinical work in the compiled literature are consistent with this idea. However, efficacy in schwannoma itself has not been demonstrated in a completed study.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic rationale is strong, and selumetinib is already marketed in Canada for a related nerve sheath tumour. However, evidence in schwannoma consists of one terminated 10-patient Phase 2 trial, two trials not yet recruiting, and a case report in ependymoma. No completed efficacy dataset exists. The other nine top predictions have little or no supporting evidence. Rhabdoid tumour has only indirect preclinical and umbrella-trial support.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results or data from the terminated NCT03095248 trial
- Confirmation of whether selumetinib is included in PRIME-NF2, and follow-up on NCT07707947 once it recruits
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada (needed for safety screening)
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A review of Major drug interactions (e.g., strong CYP3A4 inhibitors) for the target population
