## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for Rivaroxaban in this evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap, DG002). Based on the supporting literature and trial descriptions collected for this candidate, Rivaroxaban is understood to act as a direct oral Factor Xa inhibitor, primarily used for thromboembolic conditions such as DVT, PE, and stroke prevention in atrial fibrillation.

The rationale linking Rivaroxaban to rheumatoid arthritis is not a direct pharmacological connection. RA is a chronic inflammatory disease in which systemic inflammation can activate the coagulation cascade (elevated thrombin generation), increasing patients' VTE risk. This explains why RA patients sometimes require anticoagulant therapy as **comorbidity management** — it does not indicate that Factor Xa inhibition modifies RA's underlying autoimmune or joint-inflammation pathways. No literature in this evidence pack demonstrates a direct anti-inflammatory or disease-modifying effect of Rivaroxaban on RA.

Given this, the TxGNN prediction likely reflects a **confounded association** in the knowledge graph — Rivaroxaban and RA co-occur through shared "thromboembolic risk" nodes rather than a therapeutic mechanism. This should be treated as a low-confidence signal requiring mechanistic validation before further investment.
