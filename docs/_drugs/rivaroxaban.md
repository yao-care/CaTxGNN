## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the supplied data. Rivaroxaban is known as a direct Factor Xa inhibitor, so it reduces thrombin generation and clot formation. Its efficacy in thrombosis is well established, but the supplied data do not show a mechanism for rheumatoid arthritis.

The only plausible link is indirect. Autoimmune inflammation is associated with abnormal coagulation and thrombin generation. A 2021 review (PMID 34175144) discusses thrombin generation assays in autoimmune disease and the cardiovascular and thrombotic risk that comes with it. That would make rivaroxaban relevant to the thrombotic complications of autoimmune disease. It would not make it a treatment for the disease itself.

No evidence shows that anticoagulation changes RA disease activity or progression. The high score (0.996) may reflect knowledge-graph connectivity rather than a true therapeutic signal. It should be read as a hypothesis, not a finding.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but there are no registered trials and no RA-specific studies. The only mechanistic link, coagulation abnormalities in autoimmune inflammation, is indirect and does not show that rivaroxaban affects RA itself. The evidence supports a hypothesis only.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (for example, from DrugBank), to analyze the link to RA pathways
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, to allow safety screening
- Preclinical or observational evidence that Factor Xa or thrombin inhibition modifies RA disease activity
- A safety assessment for RA patients, who often take NSAIDs, corticosteroids and methotrexate, because combined bleeding risk and interactions are a concern

**Other predictions in this pack:** Gout, HIV infectious disease and brachydactyly-syndactyly syndrome are also at Hold. Gout and the rare syndrome have no supporting mechanism. The HIV literature concerns anticoagulant safety and drug interactions, not treatment of HIV.
