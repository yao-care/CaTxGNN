## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Thiamine is an essential vitamin (B1), and its role in correcting thiamine deficiency is well established. Mechanistically, it may be applicable to complications of hyperthyroidism.

Thyrotoxicosis raises metabolic rate and thiamine turnover, which can cause relative thiamine deficiency. Two serious complications are described in the literature:

- Beriberi-like high-output heart failure.
- Wernicke's encephalopathy, often reported in gestational hyperthyroidism with hyperemesis gravidarum.

In these settings, thiamine acts as supportive therapy for the deficiency-related complications. It does not treat the underlying thyroid disease. The link is plausible, but it is not disease-modifying.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only direct trial is a small pilot (n=12) without available results. The supporting literature is almost entirely case reports of thiamine-deficiency complications in hyperthyroidism, so the benefit is supportive rather than disease-modifying. Health Canada safety data is also missing. This is a research question, not a ready candidate.

Other TxGNN predictions for this drug (thyroid hormone resistance, primary hereditary glaucoma, open-angle glaucoma) have no plausible mechanism or only indirect, combination-supplement evidence. They should remain on hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (currently a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Results and control design of NCT02767245
- Controlled studies of thiamine in hyperthyroid patients, such as those with heart failure or hyperemesis
- Clear positioning of thiamine as adjunctive therapy for deficiency complications rather than a thyroid treatment
