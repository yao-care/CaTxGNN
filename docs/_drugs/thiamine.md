# Thiamine: From Vitamin B1 Supplementation to Hyperthyroidism

## One-Sentence Summary

Thiamine (vitamin B1) is marketed in Canada as a single-ingredient vitamin product, and the data compiled for this report do not list a specific approved indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **hyperthyroidism**, specifically as supportive repletion of the relative thiamine deficiency that hyperthyroidism can cause, not as an antithyroid treatment.
Evidence consists of **1 small completed trial** and **20 publications**, mostly case reports, so this remains a research question.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, thiamine is an essential vitamin (B1) and a cofactor in energy metabolism, and its role in correcting thiamine deficiency is well established. It may be applicable to hyperthyroidism through a secondary route.

Hyperthyroidism raises metabolic rate and thiamine turnover, which can lead to relative thiamine deficiency. That deficiency may contribute to high-output heart failure (beriberi-like), Wernicke's encephalopathy, and other neurological complications. The risk is higher with hyperemesis, bariatric surgery, or poor nutrition.

The rationale is therefore supportive: thiamine would replenish a secondary deficiency, not treat the thyroid disease itself. The high TxGNN score is consistent with this biological link but is a model prediction only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only direct trial is a 12-person non-phased pilot with no reported results, and the literature is mostly case reports. Thiamine would act as supportive repletion of a secondary deficiency, not as treatment of hyperthyroidism itself, so the evidence supports a research question rather than a development decision.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results or publication of NCT02767245, and larger controlled trials of thiamine in hyperthyroidism with cardiovascular or neurological outcomes
- Clarification of the intended use: adjunctive repletion in patients with confirmed thiamine deficiency, not an antithyroid indication
- Approved indication text, warnings, and contraindications from the Health Canada package inserts
- Mechanism of action data
- Assessment of route and formulation (e.g., injectable vs. oral) for the target population
