## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for polyvinyl alcohol is not available. Based on known pharmaceutical usage, PVA is a synthetic film-forming polymer commonly used as an excipient in artificial tears, ophthalmic lubricants, and topical gels/dressings, where it provides occlusive moisture retention and film-forming properties rather than acting as a pharmacologically active therapeutic agent.

Congenital ichthyosiform erythroderma is a keratinization/skin-barrier defect disorder. The theoretical link proposed is that PVA's occlusive, moisture-retaining film could offer symptomatic skin hydration benefit — but this is a physical/excipient-level inference, not a pharmacological mechanism targeting the disease's underlying pathophysiology (e.g., lipid metabolism or keratinization abnormalities in the stratum corneum).

Notably, three additional TxGNN-predicted indications for this drug — self-healing collodion baby, lamellar ichthyosis, and bathing suit ichthyosis — all belong to the same ichthyosis/keratinization-defect disease family, with similarly high scores (99.5–99.8%) but no supporting evidence of any kind. This pattern suggests the model may be capturing a general "skin barrier/moisture" association rather than a specific, validated pharmacological signal, and raises the possibility that the high score is a false positive driven by structural or usage-context similarity rather than genuine disease-modifying mechanism.
