# Idelalisib: From B-Cell Malignancies (CLL/FL/SLL) to Mantle Cell Lymphoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Idelalisib is a first-in-class oral PI3Kδ inhibitor originally approved (outside Canada) for relapsed chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), follicular lymphoma (FL), and small lymphocytic lymphoma (SLL). The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Mantle Cell Lymphoma**, with **9 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction — though the evidence remains early-stage, and a directly relevant Phase 2 combination trial was terminated.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on known information from the supplied literature, idelalisib is a first-in-class, orally administered, PI3Kδ (phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase delta)-selective inhibitor. It blocks B-cell receptor (BCR) downstream signaling that maintains B-cell survival and proliferation, and this mechanism underlies its approval for relapsed CLL, FL, and SLL.

Mantle cell lymphoma (MCL) is, like CLL/FL/SLL, a B-cell derived non-Hodgkin lymphoma in which the PI3K/BCR signaling pathway is also implicated in pathogenesis, providing a plausible mechanistic rationale for extending idelalisib to MCL. However, the evidence pack's own rationale flags an important caveat: *"idelalisib has not received approval in MCL as it has in CLL/FL/SLL; this remains an off-label exploratory population, and the related Phase 2 trial (NCT01796470) was terminated early."* Supporting preclinical literature also describes intrinsic and acquired resistance mechanisms to idelalisib specifically in MCL (e.g., via CBX5 loss or epigenetic regulators), suggesting the biology is more complex than in the approved indications and that resistance is a recognized clinical challenge.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Research Question**

**Rationale:**
Idelalisib's mechanistic link to MCL (shared B-cell/PI3Kδ biology with its approved indications) is plausible, and early-phase clinical activity has been reported (e.g., NCT01838434, PMID 24615778/24795031). However, the directly relevant Phase 2 combination trial (NCT01796470) was terminated, resistance mechanisms specific to MCL are well documented in preclinical literature, the drug is not marketed in Canada, and core safety/label data are missing (Blocking gap). Evidence level L3 (early-phase/preclinical-dominant) does not yet support a Go or Guardrails-based proceed decision.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/Canadian product label — key warnings and contraindications (Blocking data gap)
- DrugBank/detailed mechanism-of-action data (High-severity data gap)
- Outcome data from terminated/completed early-phase MCL trials (NCT01796470, NCT01838434, NCT01088048) to assess a consistent efficacy signal
- Assessment of Canadian market-entry feasibility given current non-marketed status
