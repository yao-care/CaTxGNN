## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the database. Alitretinoin is known to activate the RAR and RXR nuclear receptors. These receptors take part in reproductive axis and endometrial signaling, which gives a loose biological rationale for a link to menstrual function.

The data do not support a direct link to amenorrhea, and the direction of effect is unclear. Systemic retinoids are teratogenic and can disturb menstrual function, so a retinoid could worsen menstrual problems rather than treat them. The high TxGNN score reflects graph-based association only. It is not evidence of clinical benefit.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The amenorrhea prediction rests only on a graph-based model score. There are no trials or publications, and the plausible direction of effect may be harmful because retinoids can disturb menstrual function and are teratogenic.

Among the other predictions in the pack, acne is the only one with any supporting evidence, at L3 and a "Research Question" recommendation. It has one 1996 human study of oral 9-cis-retinoic acid versus 13-cis-retinoic acid (PMID 8884148) plus several reviews. The single registered trial (NCT04663906) concerns isotretinoin and COVID-19 risk, not alitretinoin efficacy. The remaining predictions are L5 with no supporting evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- The approved indication and dosage form for the two HANZEMA DINs
- Any preclinical or clinical evidence linking alitretinoin to menstrual function
- A teratogenicity and reproductive-safety assessment before any further step
