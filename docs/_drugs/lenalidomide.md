# Lenalidomide: From Multiple Myeloma / Myelodysplastic Syndrome to Myeloid Leukemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Lenalidomide is an oral immunomodulatory drug (IMiD) publicly known to be approved for multiple myeloma and transfusion-dependent anemia in low-risk myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS) with del(5q) — this original-indication detail is not present in the evidence pack itself. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **myeloid leukemia**, with **50 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently retrieved in support of this direction, though only a subset are graded as directly relevant.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism-of-action data for lenalidomide is not available in this evidence pack. Based on known pharmacology, lenalidomide is an immunomodulatory drug (IMiD) that binds cereblon (CRBN), a substrate receptor of the CRL4-CRBN E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, and thereby promotes degradation of the transcription factors IKZF1 and IKZF3. This produces antiproliferative, anti-angiogenic, and immune-activating effects.

This mechanism is already clinically validated in del(5q)-associated MDS, a disease that lies on a biological continuum with myeloid leukemia — MDS can transform into acute myeloid leukemia (AML), and both conditions share overlapping clonal hematopoietic stem-cell abnormalities. In AML and high-risk MDS, lenalidomide has most often been studied in combination with hypomethylating agents such as azacitidine, where it is thought to enhance the cytotoxic and immune-activating effects of demethylation therapy.

One important caveat surfaced in the literature evidence: lenalidomide has also been reported to promote the development of TP53-mutated therapy-related myeloid neoplasms (PMID 35512188), indicating the mechanistic relationship with myeloid leukemia is double-edged — potentially both a treatment and, in specific genetic contexts, a contributing risk factor.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 1/2 studies (several graded as directly relevant, including a 320-patient randomized Phase 2 trial) support lenalidomide's biological activity in AML and high-risk MDS, largely in combination with azacitidine — consistent with an L2 evidence level. However, the drug is not currently marketed in Canada, and critical safety data (TFDA/Health Canada warnings, contraindications, MOA) are missing, so this cannot proceed without guardrails.
