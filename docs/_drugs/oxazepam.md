## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Oxazepam is a short-acting benzodiazepine that acts at the benzodiazepine binding site on the GABA-A receptor, producing positive allosteric modulation that enhances inhibitory GABAergic neurotransmission. This mechanism underlies its sedative, anxiolytic, muscle-relaxant, and sleep-inducing/maintaining effects.

Because sedation and sleep induction are direct pharmacological consequences of GABA-A potentiation, insomnia is not a mechanistically distant indication from oxazepam's established anxiolytic/sedative use — the two effects share the same receptor-level mechanism. Clinical literature (e.g., PMID 6691478) confirms oxazepam improves polysomnographically measured sleep parameters in chronic insomnia without the pronounced daytime sedation seen with longer-acting agents like flurazepam.

Notably, the underlying evidence pack itself flags this as **not a novel repurposing hypothesis** but rather an indication already well supported by existing pharmacology — oxazepam has long been used clinically as a hypnotic/sedative in practice, even where formal regulatory indication wording may not explicitly list "insomnia." This should be read as confirmatory rather than exploratory evidence.
