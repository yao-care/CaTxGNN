# Alteplase: From Thrombolytic Therapy to Posteroinferior Myocardial Infarction

## One-Sentence Summary

Alteplase (recombinant tissue plasminogen activator) is a clot-dissolving drug. The Evidence Pack does not list its approved indications.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **posteroinferior myocardial infarction**,
but only **0 clinical trials** and **1 publication** (a cohort/case series that did not test alteplase itself) currently support this specific prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the input. Based on the pack's rationale, alteplase converts plasminogen to plasmin, and plasmin breaks down the fibrin-rich clot. That is the same kind of clot that blocks a coronary artery during an acute heart attack.

Posteroinferior myocardial infarction is a heart attack in a specific region of the heart wall, and it is caused by the same type of coronary thrombosis. The mechanism is therefore plausible and well established. However, the available data address myocardial infarction (MI) in general rather than this infarct territory, so the prediction has not been confirmed for this specific site.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible, but the prediction has no registered trials and only one indirect publication, so the evidence remains at L4 (mechanistic and preclinical level only). Safety data are also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data and original approved indications from DrugBank
- Alteplase-specific clinical evidence in MI stratified by infarct territory
- Route and dosage form details for the three Canadian authorizations

**Other predicted indications:** Septal myocardial infarction and coronary stenosis have slightly stronger support (L3). This rests on older alteplase studies such as TAMI-1 and intracoronary alteplase work. They may be better candidates to prioritize than posteroinferior MI.
