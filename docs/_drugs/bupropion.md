## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacological knowledge, bupropion inhibits the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine. It has no meaningful serotonergic activity.

These are the same catecholamine pathways targeted by established ADHD treatments such as methylphenidate, amphetamines and atomoxetine. Enhancing noradrenergic and dopaminergic signalling is thought to improve attention and executive function. This gives a plausible mechanistic link between bupropion and ADHD. Reviews in the evidence set also list bupropion among the non-stimulant options for adults who do not respond to, or cannot tolerate, stimulants.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
One completed Phase 3 placebo-controlled trial in adults with ADHD (NCT00048360) and a Phase 2/3 trial (NCT00061087) directly address the predicted disease. A Cochrane review and network meta-analyses add supporting context, and the NDRI mechanism is plausible. Only one Phase 3 trial is completed, so the evidence level is L2 rather than L1. The trials' results are not summarised in the data, and the populations are often limited to substance-use comorbidity.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results of NCT00048360 and the Cochrane review's efficacy and safety conclusions
- Package insert warnings and contraindications, in particular seizure risk, blood pressure effects and the interactions listed above
- Confirmed mechanism-of-action data
- Local indication, dosage form and manufacturer details for the Canadian licences
- A patient-selection and monitoring plan that excludes people with seizure disorders, eating disorders or uncontrolled hypertension
