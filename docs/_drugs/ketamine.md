## One-Sentence Summary

Ketamine is a long-established injectable anesthetic and analgesic, sold in Canada under 7 licences.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **headache disorder** (score 99.33%). The retrieved evidence includes **39 registered trials**, of which about **9 test ketamine directly in headache**, and **19 publications**, of which only **one is a headache-specific ketamine study** (a retrospective cohort).
Most of these trials have no posted results, so the evidence supports a research question rather than a treatment decision.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Based on general pharmacology, ketamine is an NMDA receptor antagonist. Ketamine is already used clinically as an analgesic, including at sub-dissociative doses in emergency departments.

Headache disorders such as migraine, cluster headache and chronic daily headache involve central sensitization, glutamatergic signaling and trigeminovascular pain pathways. Blocking NMDA receptors could plausibly dampen these processes. This is a mechanistic hypothesis and is not confirmed by the supplied data.

Several investigators have registered trials of intravenous and intranasal ketamine in acute and chronic headache, which suggests that the model's prediction matches ongoing clinical interest. The registered trials are small, and most have no reported results.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Ketamine is marketed in Canada, and there is a plausible NMDA-related mechanism and a set of small headache trials, including one completed Phase 3 RCT (THINK). However, no posted results were supplied for that trial or any other headache trial, and the literature is mostly indirect. Safety information from the package insert is also missing, which blocks safety screening. The prediction is best treated as a research question until these gaps are filled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking gap)
- Published results from the THINK, Check and KetHead trials
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the approved indications and dosage forms for the 7 Canadian licences
- An assessment of whether the injectable-only Canadian products are suitable for the routes tested in headache trials (intranasal, oral and IV)
