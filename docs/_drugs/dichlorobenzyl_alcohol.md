## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, dichlorobenzyl alcohol is a topical antiseptic used in throat lozenges, with local antibacterial and antiviral activity and a weak local anaesthetic effect.

These properties could plausibly relieve upper respiratory symptoms such as sore throat. However, bronchitis involves inflammation of the lower airways, which a lozenge-delivered local antiseptic is not expected to reach or act upon. No mechanistic link to bronchitis has been established.

The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction only. The data compiled for this report contain no clinical or mechanistic evidence to support it.

A second predicted indication, migraine disorder (score 99.02%), has no clinical trials, no literature, and no plausible mechanistic link. It is also rated L5 / Hold.
