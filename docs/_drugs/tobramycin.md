## Tobramycin: Exposure keratitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Tobramycin is an aminoglycoside antibiotic, originally used for bacterial infections such as staphylococcal infection, conjunctivitis, and uveitis-related eye conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **exposure keratitis**,
but **no clinical trials** and only **8 publications** (none on exposure keratitis itself) are available, so the evidence is weak.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, tobramycin is a broad-spectrum aminoglycoside that inhibits bacterial protein synthesis. It is already used topically for ocular bacterial infections, and its efficacy in those infections is established.

Exposure keratitis occurs when the cornea is not adequately protected (for example, when the eyelids cannot close), leaving it dry and vulnerable to secondary bacterial infection. A topical antibacterial could therefore plausibly help prevent or treat that infection.

The caveat is that none of the retrieved literature addresses exposure keratitis directly. It covers bacterial keratitis in general and in vitro data. One in vitro study also found that aminoglycosides, including tobramycin, are toxic to corneal epithelial cells, which is a concern for an already compromised corneal surface.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from the TxGNN knowledge graph, not label text)**: kidney disease, myasthenia gravis and other neuromuscular junction disorders, Parkinson disease and parkinsonian disorders, herpes simplex virus keratitis, viral, fungal, and tuberculous eye infections, ophthalmic herpes zoster, glaucoma and ocular hypertension, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, hypercalcemia, nephrocalcinosis, *Clostridium difficile* colitis, perinatal necrotizing enterocolitis, and others. These should be confirmed against the Canadian product labelling.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Major interactions are with **magnesium sulfate** and **mannitol**. Moderate interactions include other nephrotoxic or aminoglycoside agents (amphotericin B, vancomycin, kanamycin, neomycin), aspirin, and several proton pump inhibitors and aminosalicylates.
- **Prediction-specific concern**: in vitro corneal epithelial toxicity of aminoglycosides, which is relevant to a damaged cornea.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report. Please refer to the Canadian package insert for the full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but there are no clinical trials and no study of exposure keratitis itself. The evidence is limited to general bacterial keratitis, case reports, and in vitro data, including a corneal epithelial toxicity signal. The case for this indication currently rests on model prediction and plausible mechanism alone.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data
- Clinical data specific to exposure keratitis, such as the rate of secondary bacterial infection and comparison with other topical antibiotics
- Assessment of corneal epithelial toxicity risk in a compromised corneal surface
- Confirmation of the approved indications and dosage forms of the Canadian ophthalmic products (TOBREX, TOBRADEX)

**Otitis externa** has the most supporting literature of the alternatives. *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *S. aureus*, both within tobramycin's spectrum, are common causes, and tobramycin has been used in malignant external otitis. Most of the literature is observational or veterinary, so it is indirect evidence, and ototoxicity is a concern if the tympanic membrane is not intact.

The remaining predictions (non-human animal disease, postinfectious vasculitis, post-infectious syndrome, Chagas cardiomyopathy, infection-related hemolytic uremic syndrome) have no trials or literature and are model predictions only (L5, Hold). Tobramycin's nephrotoxicity is an additional concern for hemolytic uremic syndrome.
