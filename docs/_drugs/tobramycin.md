## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, tobramycin is an aminoglycoside antibiotic whose efficacy against Gram-negative bacterial infections — particularly *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* — is well established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to exposure keratitis as prophylaxis against secondary bacterial infection.

Exposure keratitis arises from incomplete eyelid closure, leading to corneal drying and epithelial breakdown. This compromised epithelial barrier predisposes patients to secondary bacterial corneal infection — a setting where a broad-spectrum topical antibiotic like tobramycin could plausibly play a protective, adjunctive role.

However, the underlying pathology of exposure keratitis (corneal desiccation and epithelial defect) is not itself an infectious process, and tobramycin has no direct effect on epithelial healing or tear film restoration. The repurposing signal therefore reflects a prophylactic/adjunctive use case rather than a primary treatment mechanism, which is consistent with the L4 (preclinical/mechanism-only) evidence classification assigned to this candidate.

## Safety Considerations

Health Canada product-label warnings, contraindications, and formal drug–drug interaction data for tobramycin are not yet available in this evidence pack (flagged as a **blocking data gap** — required before safety review can proceed). As general background, aminoglycosides as a class carry well-recognized risks of nephrotoxicity and ototoxicity with systemic exposure; several of the literature entries above (e.g., aminoglycoside corneal epithelial toxicity studies) also point to local epithelial toxicity as a consideration for ophthalmic use.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence specific to exposure keratitis is weak — both identified clinical trials are only tangentially related (Grade C relevance, not evaluating tobramycin directly), and the supporting literature is dominated by case reports and in vitro toxicity studies rather than direct efficacy data. Critical safety label information is also missing, which blocks formal safety evaluation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada product monograph / official label warnings, contraindications, and DDI data (currently blocking)
- Detailed mechanism of action documentation from DrugBank
- A targeted literature search specifically evaluating topical tobramycin as prophylaxis in exposure keratitis (current evidence is indirect)
- Clarification of whether existing approved tobramycin ophthalmic/otic formulations already cover this prophylactic use case
