# Darunavir: From HIV Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Darunavir is an HIV-1 protease inhibitor, originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection**, a primate model of HIV, with **no registered clinical trials** and **4 publications**, all preclinical macaque studies that are only indirect evidence.
This is a model-based prediction with limited support and is not a true repurposing opportunity for human disease.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this report. Based on known information, darunavir is an HIV-1 protease inhibitor. Its efficacy in HIV infection is established, and it is typically given with a pharmacokinetic booster (ritonavir or cobicistat).

SIV is a close lentiviral relative of HIV, and its protease is sensitive to HIV-1 protease inhibitors, so a mechanistic link is plausible. The four supporting publications are macaque studies of combination antiretroviral therapy (ART). Darunavir is likely one component of the multi-drug regimens studied, so its individual contribution cannot be isolated.

SIV infection is not a human clinical indication. These studies mainly serve as a model of HIV, which is already darunavir's approved use. The prediction therefore largely restates the original indication rather than pointing to a new therapeutic area.

Other predictions are weaker:
- **Feline AIDS (FIV):** FIV protease differs from HIV-1 protease in substrate specificity and inhibitor sensitivity, and HIV-1 protease inhibitors appear less active against it. It is also a veterinary indication, outside the scope of human drug repurposing.
- **Neurodevelopmental disorder with ataxic gait, absent speech, and decreased cortical white matter:** No plausible mechanistic link was identified. Limited CNS penetration and strong CYP3A4 interactions would complicate use in a paediatric neurological population.
- **Obsolete familial combined hyperlipidaemia:** The disease term is obsolete in the ontology. Protease inhibitors are associated with dyslipidaemia, so the drug would more likely worsen than treat it. This is likely a knowledge-graph artifact.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only evidence for the predicted indication is four indirect preclinical macaque studies of multi-drug ART regimens. SIV infection is an animal model of HIV, not a new human indication, so this is not a true repurposing opportunity. The remaining predictions have no supporting evidence or a weak or counter-indicated mechanism.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, which block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Evidence that darunavir specifically (not the whole regimen) contributes to the effect in SIV models, if this direction is pursued
- A clinically meaningful human indication, since the current predictions are veterinary, animal-model or unsupported
