# Omalizumab: From Allergic Asthma to Bronchitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Omalizumab is an anti-IgE antibody, and the published literature in this pack describes it as approved for moderate-to-severe persistent allergic asthma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis**, but only **2 clinical trials** and **8 publications** were retrieved, and none shows direct benefit in bronchitis.
The evidence is weak, so this is a hypothesis rather than a supported repurposing candidate.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the drug record. The literature in this pack describes omalizumab as a humanized monoclonal antibody that binds the constant region of IgE. This lowers free IgE and FcεRI expression on mast cells, basophils and dendritic cells, and reduces allergic airway inflammation.

Its efficacy in allergic asthma is well documented, and bronchitis is also an airway inflammatory condition. The link is indirect, however. It applies mainly to patients whose bronchitis is eosinophilic or allergic, or who have asthma-COPD overlap. Nothing in the retrieved evidence shows that anti-IgE therapy helps ordinary chronic bronchitis.

The TxGNN score is very high, but the two retrieved trials do not test bronchitis directly. The prediction is better read as "airway type-2 inflammation" than as bronchitis in general.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only trial near this indication has 11 participants and does not target bronchitis, and the retrieved literature is mostly asthma-focused reviews. The mechanism is plausible but indirect, so the evidence cannot support a repurposing decision.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Health Canada product monograph, to confirm approved indications and obtain warnings and contraindications.
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.
- Results of NCT02049294, and any larger controlled study in eosinophilic or chronic bronchitis.
- A decision on whether to pursue bronchitis at all. In this pack, "obstructive lung disease" (L1, Proceed with Guardrails) is supported mainly by allergic asthma, which is already an approved indication, and "atopic eczema" (L3) remains a research question.
