## Omalizumab: From Asthma to Bronchitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Omalizumab is an anti-IgE monoclonal antibody, originally used to treat allergic asthma and chronic idiopathic urticaria.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis**, but the supporting evidence is weak and indirect:
**2 clinical trials** (both graded as low relevance) and **7 publications**, mostly reviews on asthma and asthma-COPD overlap.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Pharmacology data show that omalizumab is an antibody that targets human immunoglobulin E (IgE, gene IGHE). It binds free IgE and lowers expression of the high-affinity IgE receptor (FcεRI) on mast cells, basophils and dendritic cells. This dampens allergic airway inflammation.

The link between asthma and bronchitis is mostly indirect. Chronic bronchitis features often coexist with allergic asthma and asthma-COPD overlap, and omalizumab is already used in allergic asthma. No trial in the data tests bronchitis as the primary condition. The one trial that mentions bronchitis in its title enrolled patients with asthma and eosinophilic bronchitis. The TxGNN prediction therefore probably reflects the close association between airway diseases rather than direct evidence in bronchitis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No trial tests omalizumab in bronchitis as the primary condition. The only related trial is a very small study (11 patients) in asthma with eosinophilic bronchitis, and the literature consists mainly of asthma and COPD reviews. Although the TxGNN score is very high, the evidence is indirect, so this is a research question and not a candidate ready to advance.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results and a clear efficacy readout from NCT02049294, or a larger trial in a defined bronchitis population (for example eosinophilic or allergic bronchitis)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, which are required before safety screening
- Approved indication text and dosage forms for the Canadian DINs
- A comparison with other type 2 biologics and existing standard treatment for bronchitis

**Other predictions in the data (for context):**
- **Atopic eczema and dermatitis** (L2): Several small omalizumab trials exist, including a completed Phase 4 study in children, but results are mixed and guidelines favour IL-4/IL-13 blockers.
- **Obstructive lung disease** (L1): This is largely an on-label overlap with allergic asthma. The genuinely new question is COPD with allergen sensitization, which a Phase 2 trial (NCT07059091) is testing and is still recruiting.
- **Bronchial neoplasm and six other predictions** (acne keloid, acrodermatitis chronica atrophicans, familial hydroa vacciniforme, neonatal dermatomyositis, childhood connective-tissue-disease interstitial lung disease): These are model predictions only (L5), with no mechanistic or clinical support, so Hold.
