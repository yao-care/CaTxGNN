## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available from DrugBank in this Evidence Pack (data gap, High severity). Based on the extensive literature collected here, topiramate is a broad-spectrum AED acting through multiple mechanisms — blockade of voltage-gated sodium and calcium channels, potentiation of GABA-A receptor activity, and antagonism of glutamate (AMPA/kainate) receptors. This polypharmacology is repeatedly cited (e.g., PMID 9360061, PMID 18193927) as the reason topiramate has broad efficacy across seizure types, including reflex/triggered epilepsies.

Visual (photosensitive) epilepsy is a subtype of idiopathic generalized epilepsy in which seizures are triggered by flickering light or visual patterns (PMID 16302877). Because topiramate is already established for idiopathic generalized epilepsy and juvenile myoclonic epilepsy — syndromes in which photosensitivity is a common feature — the mechanistic extension to visual epilepsy is biologically plausible. However, every trial and review identified for this candidate treats epilepsy broadly (SANAD trial, JME Cochrane reviews, general monotherapy studies); none specifically enrolled or analyzed photosensitive/visually-triggered seizures as a distinct endpoint.
