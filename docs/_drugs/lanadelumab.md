## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data was flagged as a data gap in the regulatory record (DG002), but the pack's own literature evidence fills this in: lanadelumab is a fully human monoclonal antibody that inhibits plasma kallikrein within the kallikrein-kinin pathway (PMID 30267321). Loss-of-function mutations in *SERPING1* cause C1-inhibitor deficiency or dysfunction, leading to unopposed plasma kallikrein activity and excess bradykinin generation — the direct cause of hereditary angioedema swelling attacks.

Because the predicted indication (C1 inhibitor deficiency) is the precise disease process lanadelumab's mechanism was designed to interrupt, this is not really a case of mechanistic extrapolation to a distant disease. It is much closer to a **confirmation of the drug's known global indication**, surfaced here because Canadian licensing data is absent from this dataset.

Given this, the "why is it reasonable" question is best reframed as: is the underlying efficacy evidence solid, and is the drug simply unlicensed in Canada, or genuinely unstudied? The clinical trial and literature evidence below indicate the former — extensive Phase 3 and real-world evidence already exists worldwide.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Efficacy evidence is strong (L1: multiple completed Phase 3 trials across US, EU, Japanese, and Chinese populations) and the mechanistic link to C1 inhibitor deficiency/HAE is direct rather than speculative. However, the drug is not licensed in Canada, and safety labeling data (warnings, contraindications, DDI) required to clear the S1 safety gate is completely absent (DG001, Blocking severity) — this alone prevents proceeding past Hold regardless of efficacy strength.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada or manufacturer product monograph (warnings, contraindications, DDI) to close DG001
- Confirmed DrugBank/API mechanism-of-action record to close DG002 (literature-derived MOA used above is a stand-in, not a formal source)
- Clarification of whether this candidate should be tracked as "novel repurposing" or reclassified as "known indication pending Canadian market entry," since predicted and established global indications appear to coincide
