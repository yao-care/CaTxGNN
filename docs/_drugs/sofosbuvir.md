## Sofosbuvir: Hepatitis B virus infection
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Sofosbuvir is a nucleotide analogue antiviral that is currently marketed in Canada.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hepatitis B virus infection**.
Direct support is thin: one small, single-arm Phase 2 trial of ledipasvir/sofosbuvir in people with hepatitis B (NCT03312023, published as PMID 36045503). Most other studies are in hepatitis C patients and report on hepatitis B reactivation during treatment.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Sofosbuvir is known as a prodrug of a nucleotide analogue that inhibits a viral RNA-dependent RNA polymerase.

The link to hepatitis B is indirect. Hepatitis B virus is a DNA virus that replicates through reverse transcription, so sofosbuvir has no direct target in it. The rationale rests on clinical observations. In patients with both hepatitis B and C, ledipasvir/sofosbuvir was associated with a modest drop in hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg). This led investigators to test the combination in people with hepatitis B alone (PMID 36045503). That study was small, open-label and single-arm, and the effect cannot be attributed to sofosbuvir alone, because ledipasvir was given together with it.

The remaining literature shows that antiviral treatment for hepatitis C can trigger hepatitis B reactivation in co-infected patients. That is a safety issue, not evidence of anti-hepatitis B benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Hepatitis B reactivation:** Several publications describe hepatitis B reactivation in co-infected patients during sofosbuvir-based therapy, including a case with an immune-escape mutant. Screening and monitoring or prophylaxis are therefore important.
- **Drug Interactions** (8 recorded interactions):
  - **Major:** apalutamide, enzalutamide, lorlatinib
  - **Moderate:** fostamatinib, warfarin, dicoumarol, encorafenib
  - **Minor:** cyclosporine

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only direct evidence in hepatitis B is a small, single-arm Phase 2 study of a ledipasvir/sofosbuvir combination, so any effect cannot be attributed to sofosbuvir. There is no direct mechanistic target in the hepatitis B virus, and the broader literature mainly documents reactivation risk. The high prediction score is a model signal, not clinical proof.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full results of the Phase 2 study (PMID 36045503) and a sofosbuvir-alone comparison with a control arm
- Mechanism of action data, to explain any effect on HBsAg
- A safety plan for hepatitis B reactivation, including screening and monitoring
- Review of the package insert warnings and contraindications, which are not in the data compiled for this report
