## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Chlordiazepoxide is a benzodiazepine, a class that enhances GABA-A receptor signalling and is widely used for sedative-hypnotic effects. Its efficacy in anxiety is reflected in the literature, and mechanistically it may be applicable to insomnia.

Anxiety and insomnia commonly co-occur, and both respond to GABA-A modulation. That makes the model's prediction biologically plausible at the class level. However, the linked literature is indirect: reviews of benzodiazepine hypnotics and anxiolytic pharmacokinetics, plus older anxiety studies. Without the label, this class-level inference cannot be checked against approved uses.

The same literature also raises safety concerns for sedative use, especially in older adults (falls, cognitive impairment, dependence).

Two related predictions rank lower: "sleep disorder, initiating and maintaining sleep" has no linked evidence of its own. Any evidence for it should be assessed under the insomnia entry to avoid double-counting.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high, but the evidence is class-level only (L4). The one linked trial is unrelated, there is no insomnia RCT of chlordiazepoxide, and the Canadian label safety data is missing, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**

- Mechanism of action and original approved indications (for example from DrugBank)

- A safety plan for older adults and for dependence risk
