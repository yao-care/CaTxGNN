## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Chlordiazepoxide is a benzodiazepine, a class that enhances GABA-A receptor signalling and produces sedative-hypnotic effects. This is a plausible mechanism for insomnia.

Anxiety and insomnia commonly occur together, and benzodiazepine hypnotics are an established class for sleep problems. However, the evidence here is indirect: class-level reviews and anxiety-focused studies. No chlordiazepoxide-specific insomnia trial was found. Its long half-life and active metabolites, together with safety concerns in older adults (Beers-type potentially inappropriate medication), limit its suitability.

A related prediction, sleep disorder (initiating and maintaining sleep), has more literature, including a double-blind comparison of chlordiazepoxide-amitriptyline (Limbitrol) with amitriptyline on sleep efficiency and architecture. It is a combination product, so it does not isolate chlordiazepoxide.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph (not from label text):** hypertension, orthostatic hypotension, long QT syndrome and torsades de pointes, epilepsy, gastric ulcer, kidney disease, liver disease, porphyria, hypothyroidism, open-angle glaucoma, systemic lupus erythematosus, psychotic disorders, and manic bipolar disorder, among others.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were found in total. The main **Major** ones are with **morphine** and **liposomal morphine** (additive central nervous system and respiratory depression). **Moderate** ones include bupropion, omeprazole, cimetidine, clarithromycin, dronabinol, nabilone, metoclopramide, opium, sibutramine and teduglutide. Antacids such as aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate and magnesium salts are **Minor**.
- **Class-level concerns noted in the literature:** dependence, next-day sedation, and falls in older adults.

Please also refer to the package insert, as label warnings were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The high TxGNN score is supported only by indirect, class-level literature. There are no clinical trials and no chlordiazepoxide-specific insomnia study. Safety concerns (dependence, sedation, age-related risk, and a long list of knowledge-graph contraindications) further limit its suitability against established alternatives.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data
- Direct evidence of chlordiazepoxide for insomnia (for example, a randomized trial or a systematic review against other benzodiazepine hypnotics)
- A risk-benefit assessment for older adults and for long-term use
