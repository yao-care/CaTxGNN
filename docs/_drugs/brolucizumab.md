## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Based on general knowledge, brolucizumab is a single-chain antibody fragment that blocks VEGF-A and is given by injection into the eye.

No credible mechanistic link to the predicted disease has been identified. The condition involves defects in nuclear-encoded genes needed for cellular energy production (oxidative phosphorylation). VEGF-A blockade has no known role in this process. The high TxGNN score (0.997) is a graph-based prediction, not backed by trials or literature. It should be treated as a hypothesis-generating signal only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has no clinical or literature support and no plausible mechanism. An ocular-route anti-VEGF biologic has no evident relevance to a nuclear-DNA-driven mitochondrial disorder.

Three other predictions for this drug (esophageal varices with and without bleeding, and exocrine pancreatic insufficiency) also sit at L5 with no trials or literature. Esophageal varices has only a weak preclinical VEGF/portal-hypertension rationale. Systemic anti-VEGF exposure would be a bleeding-risk concern there, and standard care is already well established. No mechanism was identified for exocrine pancreatic insufficiency.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank) and a documented biological rationale linking VEGF-A blockade to the predicted disease
- Any preclinical or clinical evidence, including a systematic literature and trial registry search
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, and confirmation of the approved indication and dosage form
- Assessment of route compatibility, since the current product is an intravitreal formulation
