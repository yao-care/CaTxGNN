# Insulin Degludec: From Diabetes Mellitus to Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

## One-Sentence Summary

Insulin degludec is an ultra-long-acting basal insulin analogue, originally used to treat diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **type 1 diabetes mellitus**, with **40 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction.
Note that type 1 diabetes falls within the drug's existing diabetes indication, so this prediction mainly confirms known use rather than opening a truly new therapeutic area.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, insulin degludec is a basal insulin analogue that binds the insulin receptor and replaces the insulin that people with type 1 diabetes can no longer produce. Published reviews describe it as having a long half-life (about 25 hours) and a duration of action beyond 42 hours, giving a flat and stable glucose-lowering profile.

Type 1 diabetes is a form of diabetes mellitus, so the prediction lines up with the drug's established use. The high TxGNN score is consistent with this. It should be read as confirmation of an existing indication, not as true repurposing.

Five other predicted conditions (autoimmune oophoritis, opsismodysplasia, thiamine-responsive dysfunction syndrome, focal stiff limb syndrome, classic stiff person syndrome) have no clinical trials or literature. They rest on computational prediction alone, and several are likely driven by comorbid diabetes rather than a true treatment effect. They are not recommended for further evaluation at this point (Hold).

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs, mostly of Moderate or Minor severity. Examples of moderate interactions:
  - Glucose-lowering agents (metformin, pioglitazone, alogliptin, albiglutide), which may add to the glucose-lowering effect
  - Sympathomimetics and bronchodilators (epinephrine, phenylephrine, pseudoephedrine, salbutamol, formoterol)
  - Beta-blockers (acebutolol)
  - Hormones and corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, estradiol, levonorgestrel, norethisterone)
  - Others (hydrochlorothiazide, doxycycline, ethanol, alpelisib)
- Topical corticosteroids (hydrocortisone topical, alclometasone topical) were rated Minor.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3 trials, plus randomised trials, meta-analyses and a Cochrane review, support degludec in type 1 diabetes. This is an extension of an existing indication and not a new use. Package insert safety data and mechanism data are still missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Approved indication text, dosage forms and manufacturers for the four Canadian DINs
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A safety monitoring plan, especially for hypoglycaemia, and attention to the interacting drugs listed above
- For the five other predicted conditions: no action unless supporting clinical or mechanistic evidence emerges
