# Imatinib: From Chronic Myeloid Leukaemia/GIST to Heart Fibrosarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Imatinib is a BCR-ABL/KIT/PDGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor originally developed for chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML) and gastrointestinal stromal tumours (GIST). This evidence pack screens **10 TxGNN-predicted indications**, almost all in the sarcoma/fibrosarcoma-liposarcoma spectrum, with the top-ranked candidate being **Heart Fibrosarcoma** (TxGNN score 99.94%). However, direct evidence for heart fibrosarcoma specifically is minimal — **0 clinical trials** and only **1 commentary-level publication**, which itself concludes "new indications, but not robust evidence." Several lower-ranked candidates in the same pack (notably *conventional fibrosarcoma* and *liposarcoma*) have real, if still limited, clinical trial support and warrant more attention than the headline candidate.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed structured mechanism-of-action data is not available for this drug in the evidence pack (`original_moa: [Data Gap]`). However, the literature cited within this same pack consistently describes imatinib as a small-molecule tyrosine kinase inhibitor that blocks BCR-ABL, KIT, and platelet-derived growth factor receptor (PDGFR) signalling (PMID 18230575, 15794712, 17687201). This is the pharmacological basis for imatinib's known approved use in CML (BCR-ABL–driven) and GIST (KIT/PDGFRA-driven).

The TxGNN model's sarcoma/fibrosarcoma cluster of predictions is mechanistically plausible in principle, because several soft-tissue sarcomas — most notably dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP), which carries a COL1A1-PDGFB fusion — are directly PDGFR-driven and imatinib already has real clinical activity there (PMID 25852058, 19635106, 27806849). The problem is that **"heart fibrosarcoma" itself has no disease-specific evidence** in this pack: the single matching publication (PMID 18623899) is a 2008 commentary titled *"Imatinib: new indication. New indications, but not robust evidence,"* which is explicitly skeptical rather than supportive. The mechanistic link for this specific indication is therefore an extrapolated PDGFR/KIT hypothesis, not a validated one.

By contrast, conventional fibrosarcoma and related fibroblastic neoplasms (rank 2–3 in this pack) sit much closer to the DFSP biology where imatinib has genuine, if modest, clinical evidence — the caveat being that "conventional fibrosarcoma" as a diagnostic entity (unlike DFSP) usually lacks the COL1A1-PDGFB fusion, so target expression is heterogeneous.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The headline candidate — heart fibrosarcoma — has zero clinical trials and a single non-robust commentary as its only literature support, falling short of anything actionable. Within the same pack, conventional fibrosarcoma (L2, "Research Question") and liposarcoma (L3, "Hold" but with 5 trials) have more real-world evidence, though still not sufficient for a Go decision, and the negative finding in KIT+ liposarcoma (PMID 17708241) tempers optimism even there.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada product monograph / TFDA-equivalent label warnings and contraindications (currently a Blocking data gap, DG001)
- Structured mechanism-of-action confirmation from DrugBank (currently a data gap, DG002)
- If pursuing further, redirect priority from heart fibrosarcoma toward conventional fibrosarcoma/DFSP and liposarcoma, where actual trial data exists, and re-run PubMed/ClinicalTrials queries restricted to imatinib (excluding sunitinib/regorafenib comparator trials)
- Disease-specific biomarker data (e.g., COL1A1-PDGFB fusion, KIT/PDGFR expression) for any candidate indication before advancing past S1
