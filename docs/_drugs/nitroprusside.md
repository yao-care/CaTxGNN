## Nitroprusside: From Hypertensive Emergencies (Intravenous Vasodilator) to Migraine Disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Nitroprusside is an intravenous nitric oxide (NO) donor and vasodilator. Its original indication is not listed in the Canadian licence data compiled for this report.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, but the supporting evidence consists of **0 clinical trials** and **20 publications**, almost all preclinical or mechanistic.
The literature suggests nitroprusside and other NO donors **provoke** migraine-like symptoms rather than relieve them, so the direction of effect is unfavourable for therapy.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the database. Based on general pharmacology, nitroprusside releases nitric oxide, which activates soluble guanylate cyclase and raises cGMP, relaxing vascular smooth muscle.

This NO-cGMP pathway, together with CGRP-mediated vasodilation, is clearly involved in migraine biology. Human physiology studies have compared NO and CGRP responses in the peripheral microcirculation of migraine patients and controls, and found endothelial and vascular smooth muscle differences. This is probably why the model links the drug to migraine.

However, the link points the wrong way for treatment. NO donors, including sodium nitroprusside, are used as migraine triggers in mouse models, and the related donor glyceryl trinitrate provokes migraine attacks in patients. Nitroprusside is therefore better viewed as a **research or challenge agent** than a candidate therapy. The high model score likely reflects pathway association, not therapeutic benefit.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 58 interactions were identified. The 20 listed in the compiled data are all moderate severity. They include corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, prednisone, prednisolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, triamcinolone), SGLT2 inhibitors (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin), opioids (morphine, opium), cannabinoids (dronabinol, nabilone), and others such as bupropion, promethazine, amifostine, sapropterin and iodide I-123.
- **Other concerns noted in the evidence**: Systemic hypotension and cyanide toxicity are key concerns with IV nitroprusside.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There are no clinical trials for migraine, and the literature is preclinical or mechanistic. It indicates that nitroprusside triggers migraine-like symptoms, so a therapeutic benefit is not supported. The drug is also an intravenous agent for acute hemodynamic control, which is poorly suited to a chronic or episodic outpatient condition such as migraine.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada
- Approved indication text and dosage forms for the Canadian licences
- Mechanism of action data
- Evidence of therapeutic benefit, not provocation, in migraine
- If a new indication is pursued, consider a closer review of pulmonary hypertension, with a safety plan covering hypotension and cyanide toxicity
