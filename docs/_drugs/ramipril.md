## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, ramipril is an ACE inhibitor that acts on the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS). It is used to lower blood pressure, and mechanistically it may be applicable to other vascular conditions.

Pulmonary hypertension is a vascular disease, so blocking RAAS is plausible in principle. However, no trials or literature were retrieved, and the data provided do not support any specific pulmonary vascular mechanism. The prediction is a model output and not evidence of benefit.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has a high model score but no clinical trials, no literature and no supported mechanism for pulmonary hypertension (Evidence Level L5). Ramipril's market status in Canada does not by itself support this new use.

Among the other predictions for ramipril, cerebral artery occlusion has the most indirect support: one small Phase 2 vascular study (n=25), a 1996 human cerebral blood flow study and several rodent stroke studies. Those results are mixed, so it is a research question, not a recommendation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank) to assess any pulmonary vascular link
- A targeted search for ramipril or ACE inhibitor studies in pulmonary hypertension
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, to allow safety screening
- Approved indication text from the Canadian licence records, to confirm the original indication
