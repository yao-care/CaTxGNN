# Levonorgestrel: From Osteoporosis to Acne

## One-Sentence Summary

Levonorgestrel is a progestin, listed in the knowledge graph for postmenopausal osteoporosis and osteoporosis, and in Canada it is marketed mainly in contraceptive and intrauterine products.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **acne (disease)**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and the **20 publications** retrieved are mostly general reviews on contraception and progestins.
The evidence is weak, and the available mechanistic reasoning points in the opposite direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Levonorgestrel is a progestin used in oral contraceptives, emergency contraception, intrauterine systems and implants. It acts largely by suppressing ovulation, thickening cervical mucus and thinning the endometrium.

The prediction is only partly plausible. Acne is driven in part by androgen activity in the sebaceous glands, so hormonal drugs are a reasonable area to explore. However, levonorgestrel has some residual androgenic activity. Combined contraceptives improve acne mainly through their estrogen component (higher SHBG, lower free testosterone). Anti-androgenic progestins such as chlormadinone acetate and cyproterone are the ones credited with skin benefits. Levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine systems and implants are reported to be associated with acne in some users.

The very high TxGNN score probably reflects network proximity through hormonal and androgen-pathway links rather than a true therapeutic effect. The link between osteoporosis and acne is also indirect.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report, so please refer to the package insert for label information.

- **Possible contraindications (knowledge graph, not label text)**: hypertension, epilepsy, anxiety and neurotic disorders, porphyria, kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, hyperglycaemia and diabetes (including diabetic ketoacidosis), hypothyroidism, systemic lupus erythematosus, asthma, blood coagulation disease, hypertriglyceridaemia, and hypercalcaemia-related conditions.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. The 20 listed are all of Moderate level. Most involve antidiabetic agents (acarbose, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, ertugliflozin, exenatide, liraglutide, linagliptin, nateglinide and various insulins), which fits the blood glucose effects noted above. Others include aprepitant, clotrimazole, glycerol phenylbutyrate and linaclotide.
- **Literature signals**: venous thromboembolism risk with combined oral contraceptives, and acne reported as a side effect with some levonorgestrel implants and intrauterine systems.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
There are no clinical trials for acne, and the only controlled study is a combination contraceptive trial in which the estrogen component is the likely driver. Levonorgestrel's androgenic activity makes benefit unlikely, and the high model score probably reflects network proximity rather than a therapeutic effect. The evidence level is L4, and the safety data are incomplete.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for levonorgestrel
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications
- Full results of the 2002 placebo-controlled acne trial, and a comparison against anti-androgenic progestins
- Evidence on levonorgestrel alone, separated from the estrogen component of combination products
- A review of the safety signals, including thromboembolism, metabolic effects and acne as a reported side effect
