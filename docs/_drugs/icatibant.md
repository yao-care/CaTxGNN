# Icatibant: From Acute Hereditary Angioedema Attacks to C1 Inhibitor Deficiency

## One-Sentence Summary

Icatibant is a bradykinin B2 receptor antagonist that the trial data show is used for acute attacks of hereditary angioedema (HAE).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **C1 inhibitor deficiency**, the condition that causes HAE, and the model's other 6 predictions have no supporting evidence.
This top prediction is backed by **23 registered clinical trials** (16 involve icatibant directly, including 3 completed Phase 3 randomized trials) and **20 publications**. It largely overlaps an already-established use rather than a true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Icatibant is a selective bradykinin B2 receptor antagonist. In C1 inhibitor deficiency (hereditary or acquired), the kallikrein-kinin system is not held in check and bradykinin builds up. Bradykinin drives the swelling attacks, so blocking its receptor targets the cause of an attack directly.

The original and predicted indications are closely linked. C1 inhibitor deficiency is the underlying cause of type I/II HAE, the setting in which icatibant is already used. This is therefore not a true repurposing candidate. It is better read as the model rediscovering an established use. It may also extend to acquired C1 inhibitor deficiency, where a small real-world series (8 patients) reported use of icatibant for attacks.

The other six predictions (serpinopathy with toxic serpin polymerization, pseudo-von Willebrand disease, primary release disorder of platelets, immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy, antisynthetase syndrome, Glanzmann thrombasthenia) have no trials or literature. Each involves a mechanism unrelated to bradykinin signalling, so their high scores likely reflect proximity in the knowledge graph rather than biology.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Three completed Phase 3 randomized trials, a large real-world registry and many publications support icatibant in HAE due to C1 inhibitor deficiency, which meets the L1 criterion. The prediction largely overlaps an established use, so the real open question is extension to acquired C1 inhibitor deficiency, where evidence is limited to small observational series. The other six predicted indications should stay on Hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, which are required before any safety screening
- Confirmation of the approved indication text and dosage forms for DINs 2425696 and 2547562
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Dedicated evidence (ideally a prospective study) for acquired C1 inhibitor deficiency, if that is the intended new use
