# Mechlorethamine: From Mycosis Fungoides (Topical Use) to Lymph Node Cancer

## One-Sentence Summary

Mechlorethamine (chlormethine) is a nitrogen mustard alkylating agent. In Canada it is marketed as the topical product LEDAGA, and the literature in the pack links topical use to mycosis fungoides, a cutaneous T-cell lymphoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **lymph node cancer (lymphoma)**.
The pack lists about **50 clinical trials** and **20 publications**, but **none of the trials tests mechlorethamine directly**. The drug-specific support is older randomized trials of mechlorethamine-containing regimens (MOPP) in Hodgkin lymphoma and topical-use reports in cutaneous lymphoma.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Mechlorethamine is a bifunctional nitrogen mustard alkylator. It cross-links DNA and kills rapidly dividing lymphoid cells. Historically it was a backbone of MOPP-type regimens (mechlorethamine, vincristine, procarbazine, prednisone) for Hodgkin lymphoma. Topical chlormethine gel is used in mycosis fungoides.

Both the original and predicted uses are lymphoid malignancies that are sensitive to DNA-damaging chemotherapy. The high TxGNN score is consistent with this established use.

**Caution:** the prediction largely reflects an existing or historical indication rather than a true repurposing signal. The trials in the pack mostly test other agents, such as the related alkylator bendamustine, and provide only class-level context. Also, the licence record has no approved indication text, so the label status should be confirmed against the regulatory record before treating this as a new indication.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Mechlorethamine has a plausible and historically established role in lymphoid malignancies, supported by older randomized MOPP trials in Hodgkin lymphoma and topical-use reports in cutaneous lymphoma. However, none of the registered trials tests mechlorethamine, and safer alkylators such as bendamustine and cyclophosphamide have largely displaced it in systemic lymphoma therapy. This is largely an existing indication rather than a new one, so any further work should be limited to a clearly defined question.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking for safety screening)
- The approved indication and dosage form for the LEDAGA licence, to confirm whether lymph node cancer is already covered or would be a genuinely new use
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A defined research question, such as a specific route or niche use, that justifies mechlorethamine over current alkylators
- A cytotoxic handling and safety monitoring plan
