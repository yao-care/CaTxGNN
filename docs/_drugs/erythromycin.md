# Erythromycin: From Bacterial Infections (Including Conjunctivitis) to Punctate Epithelial Keratoconjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Erythromycin is a macrolide antibiotic originally used for a range of bacterial infections, including acne, otitis media, pneumonia, pertussis, gonorrhea and conjunctivitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **1 indirect publication** supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, erythromycin is a macrolide antibiotic with antibacterial activity and anti-inflammatory/immunomodulatory effects. Its efficacy in bacterial conjunctivitis and blepharitis is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis.

Conjunctivitis is already among erythromycin's original indications, and it is already used topically on the eye. This makes it plausible that the drug acts on the bacterial and inflammatory components of related surface eye diseases such as blepharokeratoconjunctivitis.

The very high TxGNN score probably reflects how close this disease is to the approved conjunctivitis indication, not independent evidence of benefit. No trials address punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis itself.

Other predicted indications in the data compiled for this report are acute contagious conjunctivitis (99.55%, model prediction only and close to an existing indication), exposure keratitis (99.50%), lymphogranuloma venereum (99.05%) and necrotizing ulcerative gingivitis (99.00%). Lymphogranuloma venereum has the strongest mechanistic rationale. Erythromycin is active against chlamydiae and is an established alternative to doxycycline, which remains first-line.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications and cautions from the knowledge graph (not label text):** the model links erythromycin to the following conditions:
- Cardiac: long QT syndrome (including familial), torsades de pointes
- Metabolic: porphyria (inherited and other porphyrin metabolism disease)
- Organ disease: kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, hematologic disease, folic acid deficiency anemia
- Gastrointestinal: *Clostridium difficile* colitis, perinatal necrotizing enterocolitis
- Skin: dermatitis, exfoliative dermatitis
- Neuromuscular: myasthenia gravis, adult-onset myasthenia gravis, neuromuscular junction disease
- Hearing: hearing disorder, auditory system disease
- Other: pyoureter

**Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found. The first 20 returned include the following:
- **Major:** clarithromycin, loperamide
- **Moderate:** famotidine, cimetidine, hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide (including nasal), pioglitazone, chlorpropamide, alosetron, aprepitant, balsalazide, bisacodyl, picosulfuric acid, polyethylene glycol (3350 with electrolytes)
- **Minor:** amoxicillin

For label warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests mainly on model proximity to the approved conjunctivitis indication. There are no trials, and the only publication is an indirect pediatric review. Evidence is at L4, and the Health Canada label safety review has not been completed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Indication text for the Canadian licences, particularly the ophthalmic ointment, to see whether punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis is already covered
- Direct clinical evidence, such as a pilot or comparative study of topical erythromycin in punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis
- Review of the knowledge-graph contraindications (QT prolongation, myasthenia gravis, liver disease) against the intended route of use
