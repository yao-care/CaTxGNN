## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action (MOA) data for Ivermectin is not currently available (data gap). Based on established pharmacology, Ivermectin binds to glutamate-gated chloride channels in invertebrate nerve and muscle cells, causing paralysis and death of parasites. It has no established mechanism of action against fungal pathogens such as *Candida* species.

Vulvovaginal candidiasis is a fungal infection with a pathophysiology entirely distinct from parasitic infestation. The evidence pack's own repurposing rationale states explicitly that there is no known mechanistic link between Ivermectin's antiparasitic action and *Candida* pathogenesis — this candidate arises purely from TxGNN knowledge-graph embedding similarity (score 99.95%), without any supporting mechanistic, preclinical, or clinical rationale.

Given the absence of MOA data, clinical trials, and literature support, mechanistic plausibility for this indication should be considered low at this stage.
