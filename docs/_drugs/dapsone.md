## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled record. Based on established pharmacology, dapsone is a sulfone that inhibits dihydropteroate synthase in the folate synthesis pathway, the same target as sulfonamides such as sulfamethoxazole.

Pneumocystis jirovecii depends on this folate pathway, so a drug acting there is mechanistically plausible. This fits the very high prediction score and the clinical activity of dapsone in Pneumocystis pneumonia prophylaxis and treatment.

This is effectively an already-established use, since dapsone is a guideline-recognized alternative to trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, rather than a novel repurposing signal. The mechanistic link comes from established pharmacology, not from the supplied record.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions of concern:** Listed as contraindication-related conditions in the compiled data (not label text): liver disease, gallbladder disease, anemia, macrocytic anemia, G6PD deficiency, glycogen storage disease, cystinuria, galactosemia, pyruvate dehydrogenase E3 deficiency, and methemoglobinemia due to methemoglobin reductase deficiency.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found. Moderate-level ones include aprepitant, metronidazole, picosulfuric acid, nateglinide, nitisinone, rosuvastatin, simvastatin, tinidazole, activated charcoal and troglitazone.
- **Reported adverse events in the literature:** methemoglobinemia with hypoxia, hepatic complications, acute pancreatitis, and hypersensitivity syndrome.

Please also refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3 trials (about 2,000 participants combined) directly study dapsone in Pneumocystis pneumonia prevention and treatment, and network meta-analyses include dapsone-based regimens. Guidelines favour TMP-SMX first, so dapsone is best viewed as an alternative for patients who cannot take it.
