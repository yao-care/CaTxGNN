# Baclofen: From Spasticity to Attention Deficit-Hyperactivity Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Baclofen is a GABA-B receptor agonist that is marketed in Canada as oral and intrathecal products. The data compiled for this report did not include approved indication text.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)**, but there are **0 clinical trials** and only **9 publications** (none testing baclofen in ADHD) supporting this direction.
A second prediction, **nicotine dependence**, has **3 Phase 2 trials** and is noted in the sections below as the better-supported alternative.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Baclofen is a GABA-B receptor agonist. A plausible link to ADHD is that it modulates GABAergic inhibitory tone, which may influence impulsivity and prefrontal-striatal circuits.

However, this link is hypothetical. The supplied literature does not test baclofen in ADHD. The papers cover off-label methylphenidate use in adults, Tourette syndrome and tic treatment, and rodent studies of noradrenergic and neural-circuit mechanisms in impulsivity and social behaviour. The very high TxGNN score (0.993) is a computational prediction only. No clinical or direct preclinical evidence for baclofen in ADHD is supported by the data compiled.

**Alternative candidate: nicotine dependence (TxGNN score 99.19%).** Baclofen activates GABA-B receptors, which inhibit mesolimbic dopamine release and may reduce nicotine reward, craving and withdrawal. Rodent studies (PMID 24553576, 18682277) report that baclofen attenuates nicotine's rewarding effects and withdrawal signs. This indication has Phase 2 trial evidence (evidence level L2, stage S1, "Research Question"), although the evidence is limited, as described below.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for ADHD)

**Rationale:**
The ADHD prediction rests on a model score alone (L5). There are no clinical trials, and none of the supplied publications tests baclofen in ADHD. In contrast, nicotine dependence has three Phase 2 trials, but two were terminated early (one with only 6 participants) and efficacy for smoking cessation has not been established. It is better treated as a research question than as a Go.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada, and the approved indication text for each DIN
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- For ADHD: direct preclinical or clinical studies of baclofen in ADHD before further investment
- For nicotine dependence: adequately powered randomized trials, plus review of the psychiatric and other knowledge-graph contraindication signals in target populations
