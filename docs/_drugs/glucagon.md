## One-Sentence Summary

Glucagon is a peptide hormone that acts on the gut and is marketed in Canada as a nasal formulation (BAQSIMI).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **irritable bowel syndrome**, but no study has tested glucagon itself in this condition.
The linked **2 clinical trials** and the published literature concern GLP-1 and GLP-1 receptor agonists, which are different drugs, so the evidence level is **L5**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for glucagon is not currently available. Glucagon is a proglucagon-derived peptide closely related to GLP-1, and it acts on the gut. It is also used clinically as a gastrointestinal smooth-muscle relaxant during diagnostic imaging, which offers a plausible link to reduced gut motility and visceral hypersensitivity in irritable bowel syndrome.

The supporting studies, however, address GLP-1 and its analogues (such as ROSE-010), not glucagon. Even though the two share a pathway, this evidence is indirect for glucagon. The TxGNN score is a computational prediction and does not replace clinical evidence. Gut-brain axis reviews add mechanistic context only.
