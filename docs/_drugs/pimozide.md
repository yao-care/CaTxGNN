# Pimozide: From Tourette Syndrome to Trichotillomania

## One-Sentence Summary

Pimozide is a dopamine D2 antagonist antipsychotic. The literature describes its approved use as a backup treatment for Tourette syndrome. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **trichotillomania** (compulsive hair pulling). Support is thin: **0 registered clinical trials** and **10 publications**, mostly reviews. The only pimozide-specific clinical report is a 1992 open-label study.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not currently available in the record. Pimozide is known to be a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist. Dopamine dysregulation in habit and impulse-control circuits is a plausible target in trichotillomania.

Tourette syndrome, obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD) and trichotillomania share overlapping features and treatment responses. Antipsychotic augmentation of serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SRIs) is an established, if weakly supported, strategy for SRI-refractory cases. A 1992 report (PMID 1532960) described low-dose pimozide added to SRIs in trichotillomania. Dermatology reviews list pimozide as a possible option.

However, newer agents (N-acetylcysteine, olanzapine) and behavioural treatment (habit reversal) are preferred. Pimozide's QT prolongation risk and CYP2D6/CYP3A4 interactions limit its safe use.

## Safety Considerations

- **Cardiac risk**: QT prolongation limits safe use, according to the evidence assessment.
- **Drug Interactions**: CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 interactions are flagged as limiting factors. A case report describes a paroxetine–pimozide interaction (PMID 7961347). Interaction data for this record were not retrieved.

Please refer to the package insert for complete warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The high TxGNN score is supported only by reviews and a single 1992 open-label report. No registered trials exist. Safer, better-supported options are available (N-acetylcysteine, olanzapine, habit reversal), and pimozide carries QT and interaction risks. This remains a research question, not a development candidate.

The other nine predicted indications are also on Hold. They have even less support: indirect evidence for mania, major affective disorder and ADHD, and prediction-only signals for the rest.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Health Canada product monograph (warnings and contraindications). This is a blocking gap.
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.
- Controlled trial evidence of pimozide in trichotillomania, or at least confirmation of the 1992 findings.
- A comparison against current options (N-acetylcysteine, olanzapine, habit reversal).
- An ECG/QT and CYP2D6/3A4 interaction management plan for any off-label use.
