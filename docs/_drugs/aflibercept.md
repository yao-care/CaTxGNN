## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Aflibercept acts as a decoy receptor ("trap") that binds VEGF-A, VEGF-B and placental growth factor (PlGF). This description relies on the drug's known class. According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for aflibercept: "Vascular endothelial growth factor-A (VEGF-A) and placental growth factor (PlGF) are members of the VEGF family of angiogenic factors that can act as mitogenic, chemotactic, and vascular permeability factors for endothelial cells. VEGF acts via two receptor tyrosine kinases, VEGFR-1 and VEGFR-2, present on the surface of endothelial cells. […] Aflibercept products act as a soluble decoy receptor that binds VEGF - A and PlGF, and thereby can inhibit the binding and activation of these cognate VEGF receptors." ([DailyMed: Pavblu (aflibercept-ayyh) prescribing information, Amgen, Inc](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=c61062f3-80ec-4a20-8c28-46e468592a06), label version 2026-10-29; accessed 2026-11-19.)

Esotropia is a form of strabismus (inward turning of the eye) driven mainly by neuromuscular and sensorimotor factors. No clear mechanistic route from VEGF/PlGF blockade to esotropia is evident. The high score (0.9938) is a model output only; no trial or publication in the compiled data backs it. The prediction should be treated as a hypothesis, not as a supported repurposing opportunity.
