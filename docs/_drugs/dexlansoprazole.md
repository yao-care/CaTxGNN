# Dexlansoprazole: From Erosive Esophagitis/GERD to Active Peptic Ulcer Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Dexlansoprazole is a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) approved for healing erosive esophagitis, maintaining healing, and treating heartburn and non-erosive GERD (per the adolescent safety publication PMID 30390234; the Canadian licence records carry no indication text).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **active peptic ulcer disease**, but **none of the 19 linked clinical trials and 4 publications is confirmed to test dexlansoprazole in ulcer disease**. Most concern the parent racemate lansoprazole, other acid blockers, or erosive esophagitis.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available from DrugBank. Based on class knowledge, dexlansoprazole is a PPI and the R-enantiomer of lansoprazole. PPIs irreversibly inhibit the H+/K+-ATPase in gastric parietal cells, which suppresses acid secretion.

Acid suppression is the basis of peptic ulcer healing, and the racemate lansoprazole is established for ulcer treatment. Erosive esophagitis and peptic ulcer are both acid-related upper GI conditions, so the mechanistic link is plausible.

Ulcer healing is not a labeled dexlansoprazole indication. The trial and literature set contains no dexlansoprazole-specific ulcer efficacy data, so this rests on class extrapolation. Some matched trials are also unrelated, which suggests keyword-level matching.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and the TxGNN score is very high. However, no dexlansoprazole-specific ulcer trial exists in the evidence set, and the supporting Phase 3 data come from lansoprazole and other acid blockers. The Health Canada safety data is also missing, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the drug arms in the ulcer trials, especially NCT05448001, since the title is truncated
- Dexlansoprazole-specific ulcer efficacy evidence, or a formal bridging argument from lansoprazole
- Approved indication text and dosage forms for the four Canadian DINs
- A drug-interaction review; NCT00942175 (dexlansoprazole with clopidogrel, Phase 1) may be a starting point
