# Pertuzumab: From HER2-Positive Breast Carcinoma to Progesterone-Receptor Positive Breast Cancer

## One-Sentence Summary

Pertuzumab is an anti-HER2 antibody originally used to treat HER2-positive breast carcinoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **progesterone-receptor positive breast cancer**,
with **1 clinical trial** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.
Most of this evidence reflects use in the already-approved HER2-positive population, so it is better described as a subgroup of the current indication than a true new indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the rationale compiled, pertuzumab binds a specific region (subdomain II) of HER2 and prevents HER2 from pairing with HER3. This pairing normally drives the PI3K/AKT and MAPK growth signalling pathways, so blocking it dampens tumour growth signals.

In breast cancer, many HER2-positive tumours also carry hormone receptors (ER and/or PR). Laboratory and clinical work suggests that the HER2 and hormone-receptor pathways communicate with each other. This is why pertuzumab is combined with trastuzumab and endocrine therapy in studies such as WSG-TP-II and PERTAIN.

An important caveat: the benefit applies to the HER2-positive subset of PR-positive tumours. Having PR-positive status alone does not make a tumour a pertuzumab target. This prediction is therefore largely a subpopulation of the approved HER2-positive indication, not a true repurposing opportunity.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence is strong for pertuzumab in HER2-positive breast cancer (Phase 3 and Phase 2 randomized data), but that is the already-approved indication. For PR-positive disease specifically, the benefit comes from the HER2-positive subset, and the only registered trial is a biosimilar equivalence study in PR-negative patients. This is a subgroup of the existing indication, not a distinct repurposing opportunity, and the score of 99.93% reflects model prediction rather than new clinical proof. The data pack also lacks label safety information and mechanism data, which blocks formal safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (currently missing; this blocks safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Analysis of whether PR status changes pertuzumab benefit within HER2-positive disease, for example the retrospective finding that PR status may be a stronger determinant of pertuzumab benefit than ER status (PMID 37723497)
- Confirmation of Canadian approved indication text and dosage forms for each DIN
- Clarification of whether this prediction represents any use beyond the current HER2-positive label
