# Ifosfamide: From Cancer Chemotherapy to Female Breast Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Ifosfamide is an alkylating chemotherapy drug that has been marketed in Canada as IFEX for cancer treatment.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **female breast carcinoma**, with **6 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** in this direction.
None of the trials is a completed Phase 2/3 randomised study, so the evidence is encouraging but not conclusive.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Ifosfamide is a prodrug. Liver cytochrome P450 enzymes convert it to isophosphoramide mustard, which cross-links DNA and triggers cell death in rapidly dividing cells. Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the compiled drug record, but this activation pathway is well described in the literature.

Breast cancer cells divide rapidly, so DNA-damaging agents are mechanistically applicable. Ifosfamide is an analogue of cyclophosphamide, which is already used in breast cancer regimens. Several older Phase 2 studies tested ifosfamide alone or combined with vinorelbine, mitoxantrone, etoposide or paclitaxel in metastatic breast cancer. A review reports single-agent response rates of 7–30% in pretreated patients, with higher rates in combinations.

Ifosfamide is not a standard breast cancer drug, and no Phase 3 trial supports it in this setting. The prediction is biologically plausible, but the evidence is early-phase and mostly dated.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-related cautions (knowledge graph, not label text):** The knowledge graph flags ifosfamide as contraindicated or to be used with caution in kidney disease, Fanconi renotubular syndrome, renal tubular acidosis, liver disease, hyperbilirubinaemia (including Gilbert syndrome), gallbladder disease, pyoureter, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, anaemia (including macrocytic anaemia) and potassium deficiency. These come from knowledge-graph edges and should be verified against the official label.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded. The 20 reviewed are mostly Moderate, with one Minor (levofloxacin). The main ones are:
  - Nephrotoxic or aminoglycoside agents: amphotericin B (including the lipid complex), kanamycin, neomycin
  - Sulfonylureas and glinides: chlorpropamide, glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, tolazamide, acetohexamide, nateglinide, repaglinide
  - Antiemetic and CNS-active agents: aprepitant, metoclopramide, dronabinol, nabilone, scopolamine, bupropion, morphine

Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications were not captured in the compiled data. Please refer to the package insert for the authoritative safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The mechanism is plausible and there is a long history of Phase 2 activity in metastatic breast cancer. However, no completed Phase 2/3 randomised trial isolates ifosfamide's benefit, the trials are old or have unknown or terminated status, and ifosfamide is not a standard breast cancer agent.
- The safety burden is significant: marrow suppression, nephrotoxicity, neurotoxicity and urotoxicity.
- Among the other TxGNN predictions for ifosfamide, **rhabdomyosarcoma** has much stronger support. It has multiple completed Phase 3 trials of ifosfamide-containing regimens in soft tissue sarcoma and a consistent literature, and was graded L1 and "Proceed with Guardrails" in the compiled data. That Phase 3 evidence is in the broader sarcoma population, not rhabdomyosarcoma alone. It may be a higher-priority repurposing direction than breast carcinoma.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, to complete the safety screen
- Mechanism-of-action data in a structured source (DrugBank)
- A systematic review or pooled analysis of ifosfamide combinations in metastatic breast cancer, especially metaplastic and anthracycline-resistant subtypes
- Updated status of the Phase 2 trial NCT00026078 and any newer comparative trials
- A monitoring and guardrail plan covering mesna, renal, neurological and marrow monitoring, and cumulative-dose and secondary-malignancy limits
