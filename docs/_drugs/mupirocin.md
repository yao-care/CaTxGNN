## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From general pharmacology, mupirocin is a topical antibiotic that inhibits bacterial isoleucyl-tRNA synthetase, which blocks bacterial protein synthesis. It is mainly active against Gram-positive bacteria, especially *Staphylococcus aureus*.

SSSS is caused by *S. aureus* strains that produce exfoliative toxins. Bullous impetigo, one of mupirocin's original indications, is the localized form of the same toxin-mediated spectrum. SSSS is the severe, widespread end of that spectrum. Because mupirocin already targets the causative organism in the localized disease, it is reasonable to consider it for skin care in SSSS.

Mupirocin would be an add-on, not a replacement for systemic antibiotics, which remain the core therapy. A 2023 retrospective study (PMID 37404367) examined intravenous antibiotics combined with 2% mupirocin ointment in children with SSSS. Resistance is a concern. Two Greek reports describe a mupirocin- and fusidic acid-resistant *S. aureus* clone (ST121) carrying exfoliative toxin genes.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge graph, not label text)**: The TxGNN knowledge graph lists a drug-disease contraindication link with dermatitis. This should be checked against the package insert.
- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction database returned 100 records. The 20 reviewed here (including calcitriol, clotrimazole, morphine, metformin, pantoprazole, omeprazole, vancomycin and prednisone) all have an unclassified severity level, so no clinical significance can be assigned from the data compiled.
- **Ocular use**: An in vitro study (PMID 8222720) reported corneal epithelial toxicity for several antimicrobials including mupirocin, which is a concern for any eye-related use.
- **Neonates**: In neonates with SSSS, the compromised skin barrier raises the risk of systemic absorption, so the area treated should be limited.

Please refer to the Health Canada package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
- For SSSS, mupirocin has a plausible mechanism, shares the pathophysiology of its approved bullous impetigo indication, and has supporting retrospective and observational literature, including a study of mupirocin combined with intravenous antibiotics. There are no RCTs or registered trials, so it should be used only as a topical adjunct to systemic antibiotics.
- The other nine predictions are not recommended for advancement. Pleural empyema, punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis, neurotrophic keratopathy, vaginal discharge, leukoplakia of vagina and non-human animal disease have only model predictions and no supporting studies (Hold).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, which are currently missing and block formal safety screening
- Approved indication text and dosage form details for both Canadian products
- Mechanism of action data confirmed from DrugBank
- Full-text review of PMID 37404367 to confirm the design, outcomes and the contribution of mupirocin
- Local mupirocin resistance surveillance data, given the reported resistant ST121 clone
- A guardrail protocol for neonates and infants covering application area limits and absorption monitoring
- Ideally a prospective study or registry comparing systemic antibiotics with and without topical mupirocin in SSSS
