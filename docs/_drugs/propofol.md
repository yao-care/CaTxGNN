## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action (MOA) data is not available in this evidence pack. Based on known information, propofol is a general anaesthetic/IV sedative-hypnotic agent whose efficacy in anaesthesia induction and procedural sedation is well established; mechanistically it may also be applicable to migraine.

Propofol is a GABA-A receptor positive allosteric modulator that produces dose-dependent central nervous system depression. Preclinical work suggests it may also suppress cortical spreading depression (CSD) — a phenomenon believed to underlie migraine, particularly migraine with aura. This gives a plausible pharmacological bridge between propofol's established sedative/anaesthetic action and a possible abortive effect on migraine attacks.

Consistent with this rationale, subanesthetic (low)-dose IV propofol has an existing off-label clinical practice history as rescue/abortive therapy for refractory migraine in emergency department settings, in both adult and pediatric populations. Because propofol is already administered IV in monitored clinical environments for anaesthesia and sedation, the infrastructure and delivery route required for its use as a migraine abortive agent largely already exist — supporting the biological and practical plausibility of the TxGNN prediction, even though this would remain an off-label, monitored-setting use.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Propofol has meaningful L2-level evidence for migraine — a completed Phase 2/3 trial plus multiple RCTs and society-level reviews supporting abortive use — but safety warnings and contraindications are a **blocking data gap** (cannot complete initial S1 safety screening), and the drug has no current Canadian market authorization for any indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/Health Canada product monograph safety warnings and contraindications (blocking gap)
- Detailed mechanism of action documentation from DrugBank
- Drug-drug interaction data (current query returned no results)
- Confirmation of route/dosing protocol for subanesthetic IV use in migraine (currently unassessed)
- Assessment of the regulatory pathway for market authorization given the drug is not currently marketed in Canada
