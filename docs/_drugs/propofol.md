## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Propofol is a GABA-A positive allosteric modulator. Detailed mechanism data from the Evidence Pack are not available, so this description rests on the pharmacology summarized in the repurposing rationale. Its anesthetic effect comes from enhancing inhibitory GABAergic signaling in the brain.

Preclinical work (PMID 22390898) found that propofol hemisuccinate suppresses cortical spreading depression. This phenomenon is thought to underlie migraine aura and may also trigger migraine pain. This offers a plausible link between an anesthetic and migraine.

The clinical rationale is more direct. Low, sub-anesthetic doses of IV propofol have been used as abortive therapy for refractory migraine, mainly in monitored emergency department (ED) settings. Because propofol is an IV anesthetic requiring monitored administration, any use should stay within that setting. A commentary (PMID 32705803) also questions whether it should be used routinely.

## Safety Considerations

- **Administration setting**: Propofol is an IV anesthetic that requires monitored administration. Use for migraine should be limited to the ED or similarly monitored settings.
- **Cardiac signal from case reports**: Two case reports (PMIDs 19364017 and 20339883) describe coronary artery spasm or vasospastic angina unmasked during general anesthesia that included propofol. They concern anesthesia in general and do not clearly implicate propofol, but they are a possible safety signal.
- **Drug interactions**: No interaction records were found in the Evidence Pack.

For warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
One completed Phase 2/3 trial in pediatric migraine and several RCTs of propofol in the ED (adult and pediatric) support the indication, and it appears in Canadian and American ED guidance documents. Even so, the trials are small, one was terminated early, and a commentary questions routine use. Any further work should be framed as a research question in monitored ED settings only.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Published results and effect sizes from NCT01604785 and NCT02485418, plus the efficacy and safety outcomes of the RCTs listed above
- Defined sub-anesthetic dosing, monitoring requirements and patient selection criteria for ED use
- Review of the coronary vasospasm signal in patients with cardiac risk
