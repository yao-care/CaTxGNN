## Propofol: From General Anaesthesia and Sedation to Migraine Disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Propofol is an intravenous anaesthetic agent marketed in Canada under four active licences. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**. Currently **3 clinical trials** and **20 publications** support this direction, including several randomized trials in the emergency department.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, propofol is a positive allosteric modulator of the GABA-A receptor. This is the basis of its sedative-hypnotic effect.

For migraine, there are two plausible links. Preclinical work shows that propofol hemisuccinate suppresses cortical spreading depression (PMID 22390898), the neural correlate of migraine aura and a possible trigger of migraine pain. Propofol may also modulate trigeminovascular nociceptive signalling.

At sub-anaesthetic doses, propofol has been used as an abortive therapy for refractory migraine in the emergency department. Its sedation and respiratory-depression risk limits use to monitored settings.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations from the knowledge graph (not label text)**: The TxGNN knowledge graph lists contraindication links with epilepsy, hypotensive disorder, respiratory failure, intracranial arteriosclerosis, inherited lipid metabolism disorder and familial hyperlipidemia.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Most are Moderate, and one (metronidazole) is Minor. Examples include:
  - Opioids: morphine, opium.
  - QT-related agents: clarithromycin, levofloxacin, ondansetron, granisetron, dolasetron, cisapride.
  - Others: famotidine, loperamide, and laxatives such as bisacodyl, lactulose and magnesium citrate.
- **Clinical use**: Sedation and respiratory depression require a monitored setting for migraine use.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 2/3 trial in pediatric migraine, several randomized ED trials, and a systematic review support the use of propofol in acute migraine. The evidence is limited by small sample sizes and by the need for monitored administration, so use should stay within controlled settings.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of the design (randomization) of NCT01604785 and the reason NCT02492295 was terminated
- Approved indications and dosage forms for each Canadian licence
- A monitoring protocol for sedation and respiratory depression, and a review of the knowledge-graph contraindications (epilepsy, hypotension, respiratory failure)
