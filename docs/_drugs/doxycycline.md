# Doxycycline: From Bacterial Infections to Post-Bacterial Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Doxycycline is a tetracycline antibiotic originally used for infections such as bronchitis, bacterial pneumonia, acne, anthrax, gonorrhea and chlamydia-type infections.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **post-bacterial disorder** (the highest-ranked prediction that has registered clinical trials), with **11 clinical trials** supporting this direction, although most of them test infection prevention rather than a post-bacterial disorder as such.
The top-scoring prediction, punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis, has only a single 1992 case report behind it.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Based on what is known, doxycycline is a broad-spectrum tetracycline antibiotic with proven efficacy against many bacterial infections, including chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis-related organisms, rickettsial infections and brucellosis. It is also known to have anti-inflammatory and matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)-inhibiting effects.

For **punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis**, the link is that doxycycline treats chlamydial conjunctivitis and may calm ocular-surface inflammation. The only supporting paper, however, is a 1992 report of two patients whose punctate keratitis persisted after chlamydial conjunctivitis had resolved. It offers no controlled evidence that doxycycline treats the keratitis.

For **post-bacterial disorder**, the registered trials mainly test doxycycline as post-exposure prophylaxis (doxy-PEP) to prevent bacterial sexually transmitted infections, plus prevention of relapsing fever and treatment of chlamydia-induced reactive arthritis. This is prevention of bacterial infection rather than treatment of a post-bacterial condition, so the disease label is only loosely mapped to the trials and the indication fit should be confirmed.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: A screening query returned 100 interacting drugs. The first 20 reviewed include these:
  - **Major**: isotretinoin, acitretin, aminolevulinic acid
  - **Moderate**: aluminum hydroxide, aminophylline, amoxicillin, ampicillin, bedaquiline, balsalazide, attapulgite and others
  - **Minor**: acetazolamide, amiloride, bendroflumethiazide
- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (not label text): *Clostridium difficile* colitis, perinatal necrotizing enterocolitis, and dyskinesia of the esophagus.
- **Prediction-specific concerns**:
  - Otitis externa: an in vitro study shows tetracycline cytotoxicity in human tympanic membrane fibroblasts, a concern for ear use.
  - Doxy-PEP: tetracycline resistance (for example in gonococci and *S. aureus*) and microbiome effects.
  - Infection-related hemolytic uremic syndrome: antibiotics in Shiga toxin-producing *E. coli* infection may raise HUS risk.

Please refer to the package insert for the official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold for punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis (rank 1); Proceed with Guardrails for post-bacterial disorder (rank 4)**

**Rationale:**
- The top-scoring prediction rests on a single 1992 case report with no trials, so it cannot support action yet.
- Post-bacterial disorder has the strongest trial base (a completed Phase 2/3 trial, a completed Phase 3 trial and several Phase 4 trials). These trials mainly show prevention of bacterial infection, so the fit to a "post-bacterial disorder" is loose.
- Other predictions are weaker: chronic gingivitis has many periodontitis studies but they concern a different condition (L3), while Chagas cardiomyopathy has a negative preclinical result and infection-related hemolytic uremic syndrome has no evidence at all.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirmation that the post-bacterial disorder label matches the conditions studied in the trials
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Mechanism of action data
- A guardrail plan covering antimicrobial resistance, microbiome effects and drug interactions (especially retinoids)
- For gingivitis, verification of whether the periodontitis evidence applies, and for rank 1, controlled evidence in keratoconjunctivitis
