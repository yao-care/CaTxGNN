## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Doxycycline inhibits the bacterial 30S ribosomal subunit and is active against *Chlamydia trachomatis*. It also has anti-inflammatory and matrix metalloproteinase (MMP) inhibiting effects. Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the source record, so this description comes from the candidate's mechanistic rationale.

Punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis is an inflammatory disease of the ocular surface. It can follow infections such as chlamydial conjunctivitis. Doxycycline could plausibly help through two routes: treating a chlamydial trigger, and dampening the inflammation and tissue breakdown on the ocular surface through MMP inhibition.

This link is plausible but indirect. The only publication describes punctate keratitis appearing after chlamydial conjunctivitis had resolved. It does not evaluate doxycycline as a treatment for the keratitis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The model score is very high, but no clinical trials support this indication. The single paper is a two-patient case series from 1992 that does not test doxycycline for this condition, so the candidate rests on model prediction and an indirect mechanistic argument.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A focused search for ophthalmic studies of doxycycline in punctate keratitis and keratoconjunctivitis, including oral low-dose and topical use
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, which are needed before any safety screening
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- A route-compatibility check, since the Canadian licences do not show whether an ophthalmic-suitable formulation exists
- A comparison against other doxycycline predictions in the same run. "Post-infectious syndrome" and "chronic gingivitis" are currently at L3 with a "Research Question" status, which is stronger than this candidate.
