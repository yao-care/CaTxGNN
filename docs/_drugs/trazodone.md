# Trazodone: From Depression to Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Trazodone was originally used to treat depressive disorders (unipolar depression, major depressive disorder) and anxiety disorder.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD)**,
but **no clinical trials are registered**, and support comes from **20 publications**, mostly small, older studies (one small double-blind placebo-controlled study from 1992, with mixed results).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, trazodone acts on serotonergic pathways (5-HT2A antagonism, weak serotonin reuptake inhibition, and its active metabolite m-CPP). Its efficacy in depression is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to OCD.

OCD responds mainly to serotonin reuptake inhibitors, which is why a serotonergic antidepressant is a plausible candidate. Published reports include a small double-blind placebo-controlled study, plus open-label and case-based reports of trazodone as an add-on in clomipramine-resistant OCD.

The results are mixed and the studies are small. m-CPP has been reported to worsen OCD symptoms in some studies, which creates mechanistic uncertainty.

## Safety Considerations

**Knowledge-graph contraindications** (from the TxGNN knowledge graph, not label text):
- Cardiac rhythm and cardiovascular: long QT syndrome (including familial), torsades de pointes, heart disease, cardiovascular disease
- Blood pressure and autonomic: orthostatic hypotension, postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, multiple system atrophy
- Other: priapism, benign prostatic hyperplasia, blood coagulation disease, potassium deficiency disease, inappropriate ADH syndrome, nephrogenic syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis

**Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. The ones listed in the data compiled for this report include the following.
- **Major**: bupropion, lorcaserin, dolasetron, palonosetron, cisapride, dexfenfluramine, fenfluramine
- **Moderate**: famotidine, loperamide, aprepitant, morphine, clarithromycin, bisacodyl, dronabinol, eliglustat, and others

Several Major interactions (cisapride, dolasetron, palonosetron) are consistent with QT-prolongation concerns, and others (lorcaserin, fenfluramines) with serotonergic effects.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence for OCD is limited to one small double-blind study and several small open-label studies and case reports, mostly from the 1980s and 1990s, with mixed results. No registered clinical trials exist, and the m-CPP metabolite adds mechanistic uncertainty. Safety data from the package insert is also missing, so safety screening cannot proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Full review of the 1992 double-blind study and a systematic assessment of trazodone in OCD, including the m-CPP concern
- A well-designed randomized trial, for example as augmentation in serotonin reuptake inhibitor-resistant OCD
- A safety plan covering QT prolongation, cardiovascular risk, and serotonergic drug interactions
