# Cyclobenzaprine: From Muscle Spasm (Musculoskeletal Conditions) to Myofascial Pain Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Cyclobenzaprine is a centrally acting skeletal muscle relaxant. It is marketed in Canada under 10 licences, and the data compiled for this report do not include approved indication text.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **myofascial pain syndrome**, with **10 clinical trials** and **5 publications** listed for this direction.
Most of the trials, however, test a sublingual formulation in fibromyalgia, so direct evidence for myofascial pain is limited to a few small or older randomized studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, cyclobenzaprine is a centrally acting muscle relaxant structurally related to tricyclic antidepressants. It is thought to act mainly in the brainstem to reduce tonic somatic motor activity. It also has 5-HT2 antagonist, antihistaminic and anticholinergic effects.

Myofascial pain syndrome involves painful muscle trigger points and muscle tension. A drug that reduces muscle hypertonicity and spasm is therefore biologically plausible for this condition. The high TxGNN score (0.99) is consistent with this link, but it reflects network proximity in a knowledge graph and is not clinical validation.

Important caveat: most of the Phase 3 trials listed are for TNX-102 SL, a low-dose sublingual cyclobenzaprine, and they appear to be in fibromyalgia, a related but different chronic pain condition. They support the drug's analgesic activity in a related setting, but they do not directly show benefit in myofascial pain.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph (not label text):** liver failure, glaucoma (including open-angle glaucoma), sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, atrioventricular block and heart block, hyperthyroidism (including Graves disease and thyrotoxicosis). These should be confirmed against the Canadian product monograph.
- **Drug interactions (100 recorded; examples):**
  - Major: bupropion, morphine, potassium citrate, fenfluramine, dexfenfluramine.
  - Moderate: anticholinergic agents such as atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium, dicyclomine and clidinium, plus loperamide, aprepitant, dronabinol and eluxadoline.
  - Additive anticholinergic and central nervous system effects are the main concern.

For label warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (treat as a research question)**

**Rationale:**
- The mechanism is plausible, and a few small randomized studies (an acute myofascial strain trial and jaw myofascial pain trials) point in the right direction. However, the large Phase 3 programme is for a different formulation in fibromyalgia, and none of the evidence is decisive for myofascial pain syndrome. The evidence therefore falls short of L1 or L2.
- Label safety data are missing, so safety screening cannot yet be completed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Canadian product monograph warnings and contraindications.
- Detailed mechanism of action data.
- Verification of the intervention and population in the trigger point injection trial (NCT04704297).
- A controlled trial of cyclobenzaprine specifically in myofascial pain syndrome, with attention to anticholinergic effects and interactions.
