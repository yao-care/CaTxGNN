# Cysteamine: From Cystinosis to Ocular Cystinosis

## One-Sentence Summary

Cysteamine is a cystine-depleting drug originally used to treat cystinosis, including nephropathic cystinosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **ocular cystinosis** (corneal cystine crystal accumulation), with **3 clinical trials** and **16 publications** supporting this direction.
This prediction is largely a manifestation of the approved indication, and topical cysteamine eye drops are already part of standard care, so it is not a true repurposing finding.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, cysteamine is a cystine-depleting agent. It converts lysosomal cystine into a mixed disulfide that can leave the lysosome through the PQLC2 transporter, bypassing the defective cystinosin transporter.

Cystinosis is caused by a defect in cystinosin (the CTNS gene), which lets cystine build up inside lysosomes. In the cornea, this buildup forms crystals that cause light sensitivity, erosions and loss of visual acuity. Oral cysteamine does not reach the cornea because the cornea has no blood vessels, so topical eye drops are needed.

Ocular cystinosis is therefore the same underlying disease expressed in the eye. The trial and literature record is consistent with the mechanism. The evidence level is capped at L2 because the best trial supplied is Phase 2 and no Phase 3 RCT was provided, although the practical evidence is stronger given regulatory approval and multiple reviews.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Three completed trials (Phase 1, Phase 2 and one unclassified randomized study) and a substantial literature base support topical cysteamine for corneal cystine crystals, and a cysteamine eye-drop product is authorized in Canada. The indication is a manifestation of the approved cystinosis indication rather than a new one.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada product monograph warnings and contraindications (not available in the compiled data)
- Confirmation of the approved indication text for each DIN, particularly the ocular product
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Phase 3 or comparative trial data, if the evidence level is to be raised above L2
- Review of the knowledge-graph contraindication signals against the label

**Other predictions (not recommended to advance):** the remaining nine predictions are all on Hold or are research questions. Inborn disorder of lysosomal amino acid transport overlaps with the original indication and has only indirect evidence (L4). Exocrine pancreatic insufficiency is a recognized complication of cystinosis, supported only by a review (L4). The other seven (mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation disorder, free sialic acid storage disease, Hurler syndrome, Steel syndrome, Gaucher disease, autosomal ichthyosis syndrome and GLUT2 glycogen storage disease) rest on model prediction alone (L5) and lack a supported mechanistic link or therapeutic evidence.
