## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, ISDN is an organic nitrate that releases nitric oxide, raising cGMP and relaxing vascular smooth muscle. Its established uses are in cardiovascular conditions such as angina and heart failure.

For **alopecia**, the only conceivable link is improved scalp perfusion through vasodilation. This is speculative: no trials or publications were retrieved, and the related hair-disorder predictions (congenital hypotrichosis milia, hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp, diffuse alopecia areata, hypertrichosis) are likewise unsupported. Alopecia areata is autoimmune and hereditary hair-loss disorders are genetic, so a vasodilator has no established rationale for either.

For **pulmonary hypertension**, the mechanism is more plausible. Vasodilation can lower pulmonary vascular resistance and pressure. Small hemodynamic studies (1979–1996), an intratracheal rat study (2017) and a 2025 bolus-ISDN study support this. Concerns include ventilation/perfusion mismatch and hypoxaemia in lung disease, nitrate tolerance, reduced cardiac output and blood pressure in pre-capillary disease, and the lack of outcome data.

For **vascular disease**, the term is broad and mixes already-approved cardiovascular uses with possible new ones, so it is not a clean repurposing signal.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

Additional signals from the data compiled:

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (from drug–disease relationships in the TxGNN knowledge graph, not from label text): hypotensive disorder, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, stroke and cerebrovascular disorders, cerebral infarction, intracerebral hemorrhage, intracranial hypertension, pseudotumor cerebri, anaemia and haemoglobinopathy, hyperthyroidism (including Graves disease and thyrotoxicosis), slow acetylation status, and Rowell syndrome. These should be verified against the official label.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Those listed are mostly moderate: bupropion, morphine, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin, dronabinol, nabilone, opium, rosiglitazone and sapropterin. Omeprazole is minor. Several others (e.g. calcitriol, glimepiride, doxycycline, atropine, epinephrine) have unknown severity.
- **Disease-specific cautions from the literature**: blood pressure and cardiac output reductions (especially in pre-capillary pulmonary hypertension), possible hypoxaemia in lung disease, nitrate tolerance, and hypotension risk in vulnerable populations such as dialysis patients.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for the rank 1 prediction, alopecia)

**Rationale:**
- The alopecia prediction rests on model score alone (L5). There are no trials or publications and no plausible mechanism. The other hair-related predictions are similarly unsupported.
- Pulmonary hypertension is the most credible lead for further study (L3, "research question"), but evidence is small, old and haemodynamic, with no registered trials or outcome data. The "vascular disease" L1 label reflects completed Phase 3 trials that largely concern existing cardiovascular uses; its only direct trial (diabetic foot ulcers, n=68) has no results in the data compiled, so it should not be treated as confirmed repurposing evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Approved indication text and dosage forms for the two DINs
- Published results of NCT02789033, and a narrowed definition of "vascular disease"
- For pulmonary hypertension: controlled trials with clinical outcomes, plus a plan addressing nitrate tolerance, hypoxaemia and haemodynamic safety
