# Pralatrexate: From Peripheral T-Cell Lymphoma to Pleural Mesothelioma

## One-Sentence Summary

Pralatrexate is an antifolate originally used to treat peripheral T-cell lymphoma and related mature T-cell and NK-cell lymphomas.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pleural mesothelioma** (the top prediction, a benign pleural adenomatoid tumour, has no supporting evidence, so this report focuses on the pleural mesothelioma prediction, which has the most literature), with **0 registered clinical trials** and **2 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, pralatrexate is an antifolate that inhibits dihydrofolate reductase (DHFR). It enters cells through the reduced folate carrier (RFC-1) and is efficiently polyglutamylated, which helps it stay inside tumour cells.

This is mechanistically close to pemetrexed, the standard antifolate backbone for pleural mesothelioma. Its efficacy in T-cell lymphoma is established, and antifolate activity in mesothelioma is a recognised class effect. A 2007 phase II study of pralatrexate in unresectable malignant pleural mesothelioma and a review of antifolate chemotherapy support the class rationale. Preclinical work cited in the study abstract also reports significant antitumour activity in mesothelioma cell lines and xenografts.

Predictions for other mesothelioma subtypes (epithelioid, biphasic, sarcomatoid, peritoneal, pericardial) are extrapolated from the same class rationale, with no subtype-specific pralatrexate data. The two highest-scoring predictions are weak. The pleural adenomatoid tumour is a benign lesion with no clear rationale for a cytotoxic antifolate and is likely a graph-proximity artefact. The relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis prediction has no supporting data, and pralatrexate has no known CNS penetration or neuroinflammation evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence consists of one single-arm phase II study from 2007 with no reported efficacy outcomes in the available data, plus a general review, and there are no registered trials. The class rationale versus pemetrexed is credible, but pemetrexed-based regimens and immunotherapy combinations remain the established comparators. The highest-scoring predictions (adenomatoid tumour, multiple sclerosis) lack any supporting rationale.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Retrieve the efficacy results (response rate, survival) from the phase II mesothelioma study, with histology-specific data if available
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Comparison against pemetrexed-based and immunotherapy regimens
- A safety monitoring plan covering myelosuppression, mucositis, renal function and folate/B12 supplementation
