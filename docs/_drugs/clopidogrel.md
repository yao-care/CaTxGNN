# Clopidogrel: From Antiplatelet Therapy to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Clopidogrel is a P2Y12 platelet inhibitor, used to prevent atherothrombotic events.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**, but the data contain **no registered clinical trials** and **16 publications** for this exact subtype.
Most of those publications are small studies, case reports or cohorts in migraine with aura or migraine with patent foramen ovale (PFO) in general, so the support is indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the Evidence Pack. Clopidogrel is generally known to irreversibly block the platelet P2Y12 receptor. Its efficacy as an antiplatelet agent is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to migraine with aura.

The proposed link runs through platelets and right-to-left shunts such as PFO. Paradoxical microemboli, or platelet-derived vasoactive mediators crossing the shunt, are thought to trigger aura in some patients. Several case series and retrospective reviews report fewer migraine attacks when clopidogrel or related thienopyridines are added. This is especially so in people with PFO, or after transcatheter septal closure.

Two caveats apply. First, nearly all of this evidence covers migraine with aura in general, or migraine with PFO, and does not isolate the brainstem-aura subtype. Applying it here is an extrapolation. Second, the effect direction and size in the pilot RCT are not visible in the retrieved abstract and must be checked in the full publication.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered for migraine with brainstem aura.

For context, trials under the broader "migraine disorder" prediction include a completed Phase 4 RCT of clopidogrel plus aspirin after atrial septal defect closure ([NCT00799045](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00799045), n=220). They also include a Phase 4 trial of clopidogrel in migraine with right-to-left shunt ([NCT02938182](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02938182), n=50, status unknown). These do not test the brainstem-aura subtype.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The brainstorming-level link (P2Y12 inhibition and shunt-related microemboli) is plausible, but there are no trials and no direct evidence for the brainstem-aura subtype. The existing support comes from PFO or post-closure migraine populations. Health Canada safety information is also missing, which blocks progression to safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, obtained and parsed
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Full-text review of the pilot RCT (PMID 24836213) and the systematic review (PMID 39989443) to confirm effect direction and size
- Evidence specific to brainstem aura, or a decision on whether to fold this into a broader "migraine with aura / PFO-associated migraine" question
- Results from the ongoing or unknown-status Phase 4 trials (NCT02938182, NCT05546320)
