## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. From the published literature, clozapine blocks several receptors, including dopamine D2/D4, serotonin 5-HT2A, muscarinic and adrenergic receptors. This broad profile plausibly contributes to antimanic and mood-stabilizing effects, particularly when mania does not respond to conventional treatment.

Many patients with mania do not respond adequately to standard therapy or cannot tolerate effective doses. This is the clinical gap that the completed Phase 2 double-blind study (NCT00029458) was designed to address. A systematic review and meta-analysis (PMID 32182485) also assessed clozapine in bipolar disorder. It found clozapine similar to other antipsychotics in manic episodes and better in treatment-resistant bipolar disorder, based on three small comparative studies.

The evidence is limited to a small Phase 2 trial and small comparative studies, and no Phase 3 trial specific to mania is available.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found in the interaction screening. Major-level examples include bupropion, morphine and potassium citrate. Moderate-level examples include metformin, pioglitazone, other antidiabetic agents (acarbose, alogliptin, canagliflozin), anticholinergic agents (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium), famotidine, cimetidine and omeprazole.
- **Conditions of concern (not label text)**: Relationship data list conditions in which clozapine may be problematic, including neutropenia, agranulocytosis, leukopenia, epilepsy, liver disease, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperglycemia, coronary artery disease, stroke, cardiac conduction disease, orthostatic hypotension, angle-closure glaucoma, paralytic ileus and respiratory failure.
- **Literature-reported safety themes**: Safety reviews and the bipolar literature highlight agranulocytosis (blood count monitoring), myocarditis and cardiomyopathy, seizures and metabolic syndrome.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 2 double-blind trial and a systematic review and meta-analysis directly address clozapine in mania and treatment-resistant bipolar disorder. This supports evidence level L2. However, there is no Phase 3 trial, the studies are small, and clozapine carries serious risks that need strict monitoring.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Confirmation of the results of NCT00029458 and larger controlled trials in mania
- A safety monitoring plan covering neutrophil counts, cardiac effects, seizure risk and metabolic parameters, together with a review of interacting medicines
