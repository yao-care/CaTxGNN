# Hydroxyzine: From Allergic and Atopic Conditions to Allergic Urticaria

## One-Sentence Summary

Hydroxyzine is a first-generation H1-antihistamine, originally used for allergic and itch-related conditions such as atopic eczema, allergic rhinitis, conjunctivitis, urticaria and angioedema.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Allergic Urticaria**, but **no registered clinical trials** and only **indirect literature** (mostly reviews of other antihistamines) currently support this prediction. Urticaria is already a listed original indication, so this is closer to an on-label use than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, hydroxyzine is a first-generation H1-receptor inverse agonist. Histamine released from mast cells drives the wheal-and-flare reaction in allergic urticaria, so blocking H1 receptors is a direct mechanistic fit.

Urticaria and angioedema are already among hydroxyzine's original indications, so the prediction largely restates known use. The supplied literature consists mostly of reviews of H1-antihistamines, especially cetirizine (a hydroxyzine metabolite) and other second-generation agents. It supports the drug class but contains no hydroxyzine-specific trial data for allergic urticaria.

Current guidance, including a Canadian position statement, favours newer-generation antihistamines as first-line therapy for urticaria and allergic rhinitis because of the sedation, cognitive impairment and other side effects of first-generation drugs such as hydroxyzine.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text):** The knowledge graph links hydroxyzine to a number of conditions, including:
  - Cardiovascular: hypertension, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, heart conduction disease, sudden cardiac arrest
  - Neurological and psychiatric: epilepsy, psychotic and schizoaffective disorders, substance abuse or dependence
  - Eye: open-angle and angle-closure glaucoma
  - Endocrine: hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis
  - Other: liver disease, gallbladder disease, peptic ulcer disease, benign prostatic hyperplasia, hemoglobinopathy

  These should be confirmed against the official product monograph.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded. Examples among those listed:
  - **Major:** morphine, potassium citrate, dolasetron
  - **Moderate:** famotidine, hyoscyamine, loperamide, atropine, clarithromycin, levofloxacin, dicyclomine, dronabinol, palonosetron, trospium, and others
  - **Minor:** cimetidine
- **Class-level concerns from the literature:** First-generation antihistamines are associated with sedation, cognitive impairment, dry mouth, dizziness, orthostatic hypotension and, in some reports, sudden cardiac death.

Please refer to the package insert for official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism fits well and urticaria is already an original indication, but there are no registered trials and no hydroxyzine-specific efficacy evidence for allergic urticaria. Guidance also prefers second-generation antihistamines. Official safety information for the Canadian products could not be obtained, so safety screening cannot proceed yet.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap)
- Approved indication text and dosage forms for the Canadian DINs
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA)
- Hydroxyzine-specific clinical evidence in urticaria, ideally compared with second-generation antihistamines
