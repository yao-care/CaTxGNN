# Terbutaline: From Established Bronchodilator Use to Obstructive Lung Disease

Terbutaline is a selective beta-2 adrenergic agonist used as a bronchodilator, marketed in Canada as BRICANYL TURBUHALER.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Terbutaline is a selective beta-2 adrenergic agonist. It relaxes airway smooth muscle through the beta-2/cAMP pathway, which produces bronchodilation. This is the main treatment goal in both asthma and COPD, so the mechanism fits the predicted indication directly.

Detailed mechanism-of-action data and the original indication are not available in the Evidence Pack. Asthma and COPD are both obstructive airway conditions, and the published literature already describes terbutaline for bronchospasm in both. The prediction is therefore probably an established (on-label) use rather than a new one. Confirm it against the Canadian label before treating it as a new indication.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3 RCTs involve terbutaline, and its beta-2 bronchodilator mechanism directly fits obstructive airway disease. Most of that evidence is in asthma, with terbutaline often a comparator. COPD-specific evidence is mostly older, small studies, and safety data are not yet available.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada product monograph (warnings, contraindications and approved indications). This is a blocking gap for safety screening.
- Confirmation of whether obstructive lung disease is already on the Canadian label, which would make this an established use rather than repurposing.
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.
- A drug interaction check, since no interaction records were found.
- Stronger COPD-specific evidence, since most listed trials are in asthma.

The other two predictions, respiratory malformation (L4) and Rienhoff syndrome (L5), have little or no supporting evidence and are rated **Hold**.
