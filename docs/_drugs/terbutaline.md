## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, terbutaline is a selective beta-2 adrenergic agonist. It activates beta-2 receptors on airway smooth muscle, raises cAMP and relaxes the muscle, which produces bronchodilation.

Asthma, bronchitis and emphysema are all conditions with airflow obstruction, so they fall under the broader heading of obstructive lung disease. Relieving bronchospasm is a mechanism that applies across this group, including COPD. Several of the retrieved studies test terbutaline in COPD patients.

Because the predicted indication substantially overlaps with terbutaline's established uses, the high TxGNN score is consistent with known pharmacology. It should not be read as the discovery of a new therapeutic area.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications / conditions of concern (from the knowledge graph, not from label text):** hypertension, epilepsy, hyperglycemia and diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, potassium deficiency, hyperthyroidism (including Graves disease and thyrotoxicosis), coronary artery disease and myocardial ischemia, heart conduction disease, sudden cardiac arrest, pulmonary edema, respiratory failure, and placental abruption.

**Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified; 20 are shown here, none rated major. The main ones:
- **Moderate:** antidiabetic drugs (acarbose, albiglutide, alogliptin, pioglitazone, canagliflozin, chlorpropamide), epinephrine, isometheptene, famotidine, loperamide, clarithromycin, and bowel-preparation or laxative agents (bisacodyl, picosulfuric acid, polyethylene glycol with electrolytes)
- **Minor:** corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, triamcinolone, dexamethasone, beclomethasone, betamethasone, budesonide)

Please refer to the Health Canada package insert for the official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple large Phase 3 trials and several randomized COPD studies support terbutaline as a bronchodilator in obstructive airway disease. The indication largely overlaps with existing uses, so the main need is to define the scope and safety rather than to prove a new effect. Terbutaline is mostly a comparator or reliever in the Phase 3 trials, so direct efficacy evidence for COPD is limited and mostly older.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Clarification of the approved indication and dosage form for DIN 00786616
- Review of cardiovascular, metabolic (blood glucose, potassium) and thyroid risks, given the knowledge-graph contraindications
- Confirmation of whether the predicted indication goes beyond the current labelled uses
