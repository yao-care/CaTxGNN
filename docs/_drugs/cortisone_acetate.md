## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, cortisone acetate is a prodrug converted to cortisol by 11-beta-HSD1, which produces glucocorticoid anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects.

Alopecia areata is an autoimmune disease in which T cells attack the hair follicle. Corticosteroids are an established therapy class for it (intralesional, topical, and short-course systemic), so an immunosuppressive glucocorticoid is mechanistically plausible. However, oral cortisone acetate itself is no longer used for this condition. Systemic steroids tend to be followed by relapse after withdrawal and carry significant adverse effects.
