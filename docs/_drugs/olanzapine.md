## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Olanzapine is an antipsychotic, and its efficacy in psychiatric conditions is established. The provided data do not show a mechanistic route to benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy.

This condition is a rare, self-limiting disorder of infancy. It is often linked to the migraine spectrum or to CACNA1A channelopathy. No established rationale connects an antipsychotic to it, and no study in the Evidence Pack supports one. The high TxGNN score (0.995) is therefore the only support for this prediction.

Safety in infants is also a concern. A self-limiting condition leaves little room for the risks of an antipsychotic.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has no registered trials and no literature, and no mechanistic link is supported by the data. The target population is infants, and the condition resolves on its own, so the bar for an antipsychotic is high.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for olanzapine (for example, from DrugBank)
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications
- Any published case, observational or preclinical evidence linking olanzapine to this condition
- A pediatric and infant safety assessment
- A biological rationale connecting olanzapine to migraine-spectrum or CACNA1A-related mechanisms

**Note on other predictions for this drug:** Agoraphobia (rank 2, L3) has more supporting evidence. It includes one 12-week open-label augmentation trial in treatment-resistant panic disorder and several case reports, all studying panic disorder rather than agoraphobia itself.
