# Perampanel: From Epilepsy to Visual Epilepsy

## One-Sentence Summary

Perampanel is an antiepileptic drug used to treat focal-onset and primary generalized tonic-clonic seizures in epilepsy. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Visual Epilepsy** (a photosensitive/reflex epilepsy subtype), currently supported by **3 clinical trials** and **20 publications** — though nearly all of this evidence comes from general epilepsy populations rather than trials designed specifically for this subtype.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Structured DrugBank mechanism-of-action data is not yet available for this entry. However, the retrieved literature consistently describes perampanel as a selective, non-competitive AMPA (α-amino-3-hydroxy-5-methyl-4-isoxazolepropionic acid) glutamate receptor antagonist — a first-in-class mechanism that reduces glutamate-mediated postsynaptic excitation and produces broad-spectrum anticonvulsant effects across multiple seizure models.

Visual (photosensitive) epilepsy is a reflex epilepsy subtype in which visual stimuli trigger abnormal cortical hyperexcitability. Since perampanel's core mechanism is dampening excessive postsynaptic excitation, there is a plausible mechanistic link to suppressing photically-triggered seizure activity, extending its established role in general focal and generalized epilepsy.

That said, the mechanistic rationale outpaces the direct evidence. The three retrieved trials evaluate perampanel's tolerability, pharmacokinetics, or effects on general neurophysiology tests (EEG, evoked potentials) in unselected epilepsy populations — none specifically enrolled or analyzed a visual/photosensitive epilepsy subgroup. Similarly, the 20 literature results are largely general epilepsy reviews, pregnancy/pediatric management guidance, and treatment-comparison studies, without direct confirmatory data for this subtype.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The AMPA-antagonist mechanism provides a plausible theoretical basis for efficacy in visual/photosensitive epilepsy, but no retrieved clinical trial or publication was designed for or specifically analyzed this subtype — all available evidence derives from general epilepsy populations. This corresponds to the evidence pack's own "Research Question" staging (L3, S1) rather than a Go or Guardrailed-proceed decision.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A dedicated mechanism-of-action data entry (currently missing from DrugBank record)
- Health Canada product monograph / prescribing information, since perampanel is not currently marketed in Canada
- Trial or registry data specifically enrolling photosensitive/visual epilepsy patients (e.g., photoparoxysmal response or EEG-photic stimulation endpoints)
- Formal safety/DDI review before any S1 safety assessment, as key warnings, contraindications, and interaction data are currently unavailable
