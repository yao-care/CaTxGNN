## Aripiprazole: From Bipolar Disorder and Schizophrenia to Major Affective Disorder

The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **major affective disorder** (in practice, major depressive disorder as add-on therapy),
with **36 clinical trials** and **18 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Aripiprazole is a partial agonist at dopamine D2/D3 receptors and at serotonin 5-HT1A receptors, and an antagonist at 5-HT2A receptors. Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not available in the structured drug record, so this description comes from the mechanistic analysis prepared for this prediction.

This receptor profile can fine-tune dopamine and serotonin signalling in mood circuits. That is why it may boost the effect of serotonergic antidepressants when a patient has responded only partly. Aripiprazole is already used for bipolar disorder, which is itself a mood disorder, so extending it to depressive illness is a natural step. Its add-on use in major depressive disorder is supported by several Phase 3 trials and network meta-analyses.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available. The information below comes from other sources and should be checked against the Canadian product monograph.

- **Conditions linked to contraindication in the knowledge graph** (not label text): orthostatic hypotension and other low blood pressure disorders; epilepsy; hyperglycaemia, diabetes and diabetic ketoacidosis; neutropenia, leukopenia and agranulocytosis; hyperlipidaemia and obesity; myocardial infarction, coronary artery disease and other cardiovascular disorders; heart conduction disease; neuroleptic malignant syndrome; and cerebrovascular disorder.
- **Drug Interactions** (100 interactions found; first 20 reviewed): most are Moderate. One is Major: **bupropion**. Moderate interactions include:
  - diabetes drugs (acarbose, metformin, pioglitazone, alogliptin, canagliflozin, albiglutide, acetohexamide, chlorpropamide);
  - anticholinergic drugs (atropine, hyoscyamine, clidinium);
  - others (epinephrine, famotidine, cimetidine, loperamide, aprepitant, cisapride, clarithromycin, clotrimazole).
- **Risks noted in the literature**: aripiprazole has been reported to cause impulsive-compulsive behaviours (such as problem gambling and hypersexuality) and, in some cases, trichotillomania. Monitoring for akathisia and metabolic effects is also advised.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several large, completed Phase 3 placebo-controlled trials (NCT00095823, NCT00095758, NCT00105196, NCT00876343) and multiple network meta-analyses support adjunctive aripiprazole in major depressive disorder. Aripiprazole is already marketed in Canada with 20 DINs. The main gaps are missing label-based safety data and mechanism data in the structured record.
