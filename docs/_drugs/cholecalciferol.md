## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, cholecalciferol is a vitamin D precursor. It is converted in the liver and kidney to the active hormone 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D, which regulates calcium and phosphorus balance. Vitamin D analogs are used in hypoparathyroidism to correct low blood calcium.

The prediction is only weakly plausible. In hypoparathyroidism, vitamin D therapy is symptomatic calcium management, not repurposing. Cholecalciferol also needs PTH-driven renal 1-alpha-hydroxylation to become active, and this pathway is impaired when PTH is deficient. Active forms such as calcitriol or alfacalcidol are therefore the more logical choice. The high TxGNN score is likely to reflect proximity in the knowledge graph (calcium and bone metabolism nodes) rather than a demonstrated therapeutic effect.
