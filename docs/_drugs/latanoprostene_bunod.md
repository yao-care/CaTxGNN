## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the record. Latanoprostene bunod combines a prostaglandin F2-alpha analog with a nitric oxide (NO) donor. The prostaglandin component increases uveoscleral outflow, and the NO component increases trabecular outflow. Both are linked to vasodilation.

The predicted link to visceral calciphylaxis, a systemic calcific vasculopathy, rests on this NO donation and prostaglandin-mediated vasodilation. However, the connection is weak:

- No trials or publications support a topical ocular agent for this condition.
- Systemic exposure after ocular dosing is minimal, so it is doubtful that enough drug would reach the affected small vessels.

The high TxGNN score is best read as a hypothesis-generating signal, not as evidence of benefit.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is model-only (L5), with no trials, no literature and a weak mechanistic link. Systemic exposure from an ocular product is minimal, and calciphylaxis is a serious systemic disease.

Other predictions for this drug have more support:
- **Primary hereditary glaucoma** (L4) is essentially an on-label mechanism, so it is not a true repurposing.
- **Vascular disease** (L4) has one completed Phase 4 nailfold capillary blood flow study, which is a physiological surrogate only.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data, for example from DrugBank
- A preclinical or mechanistic study showing a plausible effect of NO donation or prostaglandin agonism on vascular calcification
- Evidence on whether a topical ocular route can achieve relevant systemic exposure, or whether another route would be required
