## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for ruxolitinib is not yet available in this evidence pack (DG002, High severity). Based on the drug identifier and target class implied in the rationale fields, ruxolitinib acts as a **JAK1/2 inhibitor**, blocking downstream STAT signaling triggered by inflammatory cytokines (notably IFN-γ and IL-6).

Seven of the ten candidates generated by TxGNN (uterine corpus PEComa, benign PEComa, lymphangiomyoma, lymphangioleiomyomatosis, familial rhabdoid tumor, lung PEComa, ovarian myxoid liposarcoma) share a **TSC1/TSC2–mTOR** or **SMARCB1** pathological driver with no established JAK-STAT dependency. The evidence pack's own rationale text explicitly flags these as likely **graph artifacts** — high TxGNN scores reflecting indirect node proximity in the knowledge graph rather than real biology — and none carry any clinical trial or literature support (L5, Hold).

The picture is different for **hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH)**. HLH is a hyperinflammatory cytokine-storm syndrome in which IFN-γ and IL-6 signal through the JAK-STAT axis — precisely the pathway ruxolitinib blocks. This mechanistic fit is corroborated by murine mechanistic studies, multiple pediatric and adult cohort studies, and two registered clinical trials (one Phase 3), placing this candidate at **L2/S3 (Proceed with Guardrails)** — a materially stronger evidentiary position than the top-ranked TxGNN score would suggest. A related, lower-confidence signal for **myxoid liposarcoma** (L4) is grounded in FUS-DDIT3-driven JAK-STAT activation but remains preclinical/mechanistic only.
