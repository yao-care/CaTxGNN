## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not recorded for ezetimibe in this pack. Based on the pack's rationale notes, ezetimibe inhibits NPC1L1-mediated intestinal cholesterol absorption. This lowers LDL-C and adds to the effect of statins, which act through a different pathway.

Hyperlipoproteinemia is a disorder of elevated blood lipoproteins, and LDL-C reduction is the main treatment goal. The predicted indication therefore matches ezetimibe's known pharmacology, and trials with ezetimibe as the direct intervention already exist (see below).

Because the pack has no original indication and the trials directly test lipid lowering, this is probably an already-labelled use rather than a new one. Confirm the on-label status against the Health Canada product monograph before classing it as a repurposing candidate.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3 randomized trials support ezetimibe for lipid lowering. However, much of the evidence comes from combination or comparator-arm designs. The pack also cannot show whether this is a labelled use, so it may not be true repurposing.

Three lower-ranked predictions (familial hypercholesterolemia, CYP7A1-deficiency hypercholesterolemia, CETP deficiency) are outside this report. Familial hypercholesterolemia has direct Phase 3 ezetimibe trials. The other two have no direct evidence and are on Hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm the approved indications against the Health Canada product monograph, and classify the candidate as on-label or true repurposing.
- Obtain the mechanism-of-action record from DrugBank.
- Obtain Health Canada warnings and contraindications from the package insert, since safety screening cannot proceed without them.
- Re-appraise the evidence for ezetimibe-specific trials, separating those where ezetimibe is only a comparator or background therapy.
