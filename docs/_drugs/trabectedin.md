# Trabectedin: From Soft Tissue Sarcoma and Ovarian Cancer to Female Breast Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Trabectedin is a marine-derived cytotoxic agent. According to the literature it is registered in Europe for soft tissue sarcoma and for ovarian cancer (in combination with pegylated liposomal doxorubicin). The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **female breast carcinoma**. Support is **2 registered clinical trials** (neither shows breast cancer efficacy) and **20 publications**, including several Phase 2 breast cancer studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the drug profile. The mechanism below comes from the published literature. Trabectedin binds the DNA minor groove and interferes with transcription regulation and DNA repair, particularly transcription-coupled nucleotide excision repair, which leads to DNA double-strand breaks. It also modulates the tumour microenvironment, for example by reducing tumour-associated macrophages.

This gives a plausible link to breast cancer. Tumours with homologous recombination deficiency, such as BRCA1/2-mutated breast cancer, may be more sensitive to trabectedin (PMID 27710871). The 2014 Phase 2 trial in germline BRCA1/2 carriers with metastatic breast cancer (PMID 24692579) tested this idea directly. Preclinical work also shows:
- apoptosis induction in ER+/HER2- and HER2+ breast cancer cell lines (PMID 23792433);
- anti-angiogenic effects in breast cancer cells (PMID 24941346);
- enhanced IL-12 antitumour activity in triple-negative breast cancer (PMID 39777457).

The original sarcoma and ovarian indications are DNA-damage-sensitive, and the ovarian setting is already linked to BRCA deficiency. That makes breast cancer, especially BRCA-deficient or HR+/HER2- subsets, a reasonable extension. The very high TxGNN score is a model output and should not be read as proof of efficacy.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. Literature-reported toxicities are mainly hematological and hepatic (see Cytotoxicity above). A dedicated QT study (NCT00786838) exists in the trial record, but its results are not summarised here.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The biological rationale is credible, and the Phase 2 breast cancer studies (BRCA-mutated, HR+/HER2-, post-anthracycline/taxane) show the idea has been tested clinically. However, the evidence is capped at L2. There is no Phase 3 RCT, and no registered trial in the record shows breast cancer efficacy. The Canadian package insert safety data is also missing, which blocks safety screening. At this stage the prediction is a research question rather than an actionable repurposing candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada product monograph (warnings, contraindications, approved indications, dosage form) to complete safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Full-text review of the Phase 2 breast cancer studies (PMIDs 25239225, 27266804, 24692579) to extract response rates and safety results
- A confirmatory or biomarker-stratified study (e.g., BRCA-deficient or HR+/HER2- subsets)
- A myelosuppression and hepatic monitoring plan for breast cancer populations
