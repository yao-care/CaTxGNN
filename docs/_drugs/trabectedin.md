## Trabectedin: From Ovarian Cancer to Female Breast Carcinoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Trabectedin is a marine-derived DNA-binding cytotoxic agent, originally used to treat ovarian cancer (and, in many countries, soft tissue sarcoma).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **female breast carcinoma**,
with **1 registered clinical trial** (a QT-interval safety study, not an efficacy trial) and **20 publications**, including several Phase 2 studies, currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the database record compiled for this report. Based on the published literature, trabectedin binds the minor groove of DNA and triggers DNA double-strand breaks that depend on transcription-coupled nucleotide excision repair. It also affects the tumour microenvironment by reducing tumour-associated macrophages.

Ovarian and breast cancers share a biology of DNA-repair deficiency. Tumours with BRCA1/2 deficiency (homologous recombination deficiency) appear especially sensitive to trabectedin. This gives a plausible rationale in a subset of breast cancers, particularly BRCA-deficient and triple-negative disease. Preclinical work also suggests trabectedin may enhance the antitumour effect of IL-12 in triple-negative breast cancer.

This rationale rests on the literature rather than on the supplied database record, so it should be treated as hypothesis-generating.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs in total (the first 20 were provided). Most are Moderate. One is **Major: clarithromycin**. Other Moderate interactions include aprepitant, dexamethasone, cimetidine, metronidazole, miconazole, levofloxacin, minocycline, bupropion, naltrexone, simvastatin and rosuvastatin. Many of these are commonly co-prescribed with chemotherapy, so a full interaction review is needed before use.

Warnings and contraindications from the Canadian package insert are not available in the compiled data. Please refer to the package insert for them.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic rationale is plausible, particularly for BRCA-deficient and triple-negative breast cancer, and the TxGNN score is high (0.9973). However, the only registered trial is a QT-safety study. Efficacy support comes from Phase 2 publications whose results are not available in the compiled data. Package insert warnings and contraindications are also missing, so safety screening cannot proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The Health Canada package insert (warnings, contraindications, approved indications, dosage form)
- Full texts and outcome data of the Phase 2 breast cancer studies (PMIDs 25239225, 24692579, 27266804)
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A biomarker-defined target population (for example BRCA1/2 or XPG status)
- A review of the major and moderate drug interactions, especially clarithromycin
