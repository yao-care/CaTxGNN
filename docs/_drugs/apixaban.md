## Apixaban: Migraine disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Apixaban is a direct factor Xa inhibitor anticoagulant marketed in Canada by many manufacturers. The TxGNN model predicts it for **migraine disorder**, but the evidence level is **L5 (model prediction only)**. One clinical trial was retrieved and it does not study migraine. The available case reports describe migraine with aura that worsened, or did not resolve, on apixaban.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Apixaban is known as a direct factor Xa inhibitor, which reduces thrombin generation and clot formation.

There is no established mechanistic rationale for apixaban in migraine. One hypothetical link is that anticoagulation might reduce microembolic triggers of aura, as proposed in the patent foramen ovale (paradoxical embolism) hypothesis, or in thrombosis associated with antiphospholipid antibodies. This remains speculative.

The case-level observations point the other way. One report describes migraine with aura worsening after apixaban was started. Another describes aura that resolved on warfarin but not on apixaban. The high score of 0.9902 is a computational output and is not backed by clinical data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score alone, so the evidence level is L5. No study supports apixaban for migraine, and the available case reports describe migraine with aura that worsened, or did not resolve, on apixaban.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism-of-action data for apixaban and a plausible link to migraine
- Controlled studies of apixaban in migraine, for example in patients with PFO or antiphospholipid antibodies
- Review of the Health Canada package insert for warnings and contraindications
