## Cilastatin: Dehydropeptidase-I inhibitor (imipenem co-formulant) → Staphylococcus aureus infection
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Cilastatin is a renal dehydropeptidase-I inhibitor that is combined with the antibiotic imipenem to prevent the carbapenem from being broken down in the kidney.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant to **Staphylococcus aureus infection**, with **1 clinical trial** and **20 publications** compiled for this direction.
However, the trial tests linezolid with or without a carbapenem, and the antibacterial activity in the literature comes from imipenem rather than cilastatin, so the evidence is indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for cilastatin is not available in the data compiled for this report. From the pharmacology data, cilastatin inhibits dehydropeptidase-1 and is given in fixed-dose combination with imipenem to increase imipenem's effectiveness. Cilastatin itself has no intrinsic antibacterial activity.

Any anti-staphylococcal effect therefore comes from imipenem. The high TxGNN score most likely reflects the imipenem/cilastatin combination and its existing antibacterial uses, not an independent effect of cilastatin. Older clinical studies and case reports describe imipenem/cilastatin in methicillin-sensitive and methicillin-resistant S. aureus infections, including a combination with fosfomycin for refractory MRSA. These support the clinical use of the combination, but they cannot separate cilastatin's contribution.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only listed trial does not isolate cilastatin or the imipenem/cilastatin combination, and the supporting studies are mostly older, small or preclinical. Cilastatin has no antibacterial activity of its own, so the model's score probably reflects imipenem. The evidence level is L3 at most, and the package insert safety data has not yet been obtained.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Studies that separate the contribution of imipenem from cilastatin in S. aureus infection
