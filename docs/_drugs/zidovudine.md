# Zidovudine: From HIV Infection and AIDS to Feline Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Zidovudine is a nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS in humans.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (FIV/FeLV-related disease in cats)**.
This direction currently rests on **0 registered clinical trials** and **19 publications**, which are almost entirely animal or veterinary studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not available in the data compiled for this report. Zidovudine is, however, a known nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NRTI). It is phosphorylated in cells and blocks viral DNA synthesis by chain termination.

Feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) is a lentivirus whose reverse transcriptase is closely related to that of HIV-1. This makes the prediction biologically plausible. FIV-infected cats are also widely used as an animal model of HIV infection.

Preclinical and veterinary reports describe lower plasma virus titres, reduced provirus burden and some immune benefit in treated cats. Results are mixed, though. Zidovudine did not prevent infection in several studies, and benefits were often not sustained after treatment stopped.

This is a **veterinary indication**, not a new human use. Its value for human medicine is mainly as a model for HIV research.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications and risk conditions from the knowledge graph (not label text):** These are the disease associations recorded in the TxGNN knowledge graph:
  - Haematological: neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia (including macrocytic anaemia)
  - Hepatic: liver failure, liver disease, fatty liver disease, non-alcoholic steatohepatitis, gallbladder disease
  - Metabolic: lactic acidosis, hypertriglyceridaemia, obesity disorders
  - Muscle: myopathy, myositis, dermatomyositis, muscular disease, skeletal muscle disease, familial periodic paralysis
  - Other: kidney disease, pancreatitis, pyoureter
- **Drug Interactions:** The interaction screen returned 100 interactions in total, of which 20 were reviewed here.
  - *Moderate:* amphotericin B (including the cholesteryl sulfate and lipid complex forms), corticosteroids (betamethasone, budesonide, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone, prednisolone, prednisone, triamcinolone), statins (rosuvastatin, simvastatin), naltrexone, orlistat

- **Veterinary toxicity:** A toxicity study in FeLV-infected cats (PMID 2163339) is available and should be reviewed before any dose planning.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high (99.96%), but support is limited to animal and veterinary studies (L4). There are no registered clinical trials. The indication is veterinary and does not extend human use of the drug.

Two other predictions for this drug have much stronger evidence: congenital HIV and AIDS-related complex. Both have Phase 3 trial support and were rated "Proceed with Guardrails". However, they fall within the drug's existing HIV/AIDS indication rather than being true repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism-of-action data for the drug
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada
- Clarification of whether a veterinary indication is within the programme's scope
- Controlled veterinary trials with defined endpoints, if the feline indication is pursued
- A review of the toxicity data from cat studies, including haematological effects
