# Meropenem: From Bacterial Infections (Meningitis, Peritonitis) to Bacterial Arthritis

## One-Sentence Summary

Meropenem is a broad-spectrum carbapenem antibiotic, originally used for serious bacterial infections such as Pseudomonas aeruginosa infection, bacterial meningitis and peritonitis.
The TxGNN model's top prediction is **bacterial arthritis**, but the evidence for this is indirect: **0 registered clinical trials** and **19 publications**, mostly case reports, reviews and susceptibility studies.
Among the ten predicted indications, the strongest evidence is for **urinary tract infection** (9 clinical trials, 20 publications), which is covered at the end of this report.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, meropenem is a carbapenem that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins. Its efficacy in serious Gram-negative and Gram-positive infections is established.

Bacterial arthritis (septic arthritis) is caused by organisms that meropenem often covers, including Enterobacterales and some streptococci. The supporting literature is indirect:
- Melioidosis cohorts with osteoarticular involvement, where all isolates were meropenem-susceptible
- A case report of ESBL-producing *Klebsiella pneumoniae* septic arthritis treated successfully with meropenem plus amikacin
- Pharmacokinetic and tissue penetration studies, and in vitro bone cement elution work

There are no registered trials for this indication. Narrower-spectrum agents are normally preferred where the pathogen is susceptible, so meropenem's role would be as a reserve option for resistant or polymicrobial disease.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for the top-ranked indication, bacterial arthritis)

**Rationale:**
- The prediction is mechanistically plausible, but support is limited to case reports, cohort data and susceptibility studies. There are no registered trials, so the evidence level is L4.
- Urinary tract infection is the only predicted indication with strong evidence (L1, **Proceed with Guardrails**), but it is largely an established use and the evidence comes mostly from comparator-arm data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- For urinary tract infection: meropenem-specific arm results from each trial, restriction to documented or suspected resistant pathogens, and carbapenem stewardship
- For bacterial arthritis: comparative clinical data or bone and joint penetration studies before any further consideration
