# Meropenem: From Serious Bacterial Infections to Bacterial Arthritis

## One-Sentence Summary

Meropenem is a broad-spectrum carbapenem antibiotic originally used to treat serious bacterial infections such as complicated intra-abdominal infections, hospital-acquired pneumonia, and bacterial meningitis. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Bacterial Arthritis** (septic arthritis), with **1 clinical trial** and **20 publications** currently identified, though most are case reports, pharmacokinetic studies, or indirectly related work rather than indication-specific controlled trials.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on known pharmacology, meropenem is a carbapenem-class β-lactam antibiotic that inhibits penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs), blocking bacterial cell wall synthesis. It has broad-spectrum bactericidal activity against Gram-positive cocci, Gram-negative bacilli, and anaerobes.

Bacterial (septic) arthritis is most commonly caused by *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Streptococcus* species, and Gram-negative bacilli — pathogens that fall squarely within meropenem's established spectrum of activity. Since the original use case for meropenem is treatment of serious, often polymicrobial or resistant bacterial infections, extending this to joint-space infections is mechanistically plausible: both indications rely on the same core action of eliminating susceptible or multidrug-resistant bacteria in a deep-tissue infection site.

As noted in the repurposing rationale: septic arthritis is frequently caused by pathogens meropenem broadly covers, so the mechanistic link is reasonable. However, current evidence supporting this specific indication is limited mainly to case reports and pharmacokinetic/in-vitro studies, with no indication-specific controlled trials identified to date.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence for meropenem in bacterial arthritis is currently limited to case reports, retrospective cohorts, and pharmacokinetic data (Evidence Level L3, decision stage S1 — "Research Question"), with no indication-specific controlled trials. In addition, the drug is not currently marketed in Canada and a Blocking-severity gap exists for TFDA/label safety data, preventing progression to formal safety evaluation.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/Health Canada package insert warnings, contraindications, and DDI data (Blocking gap, DG001)
- Detailed mechanism of action (MOA) documentation (High-severity gap, DG002)
- A prospective or controlled study specifically evaluating meropenem in bacterial/septic arthritis
- Confirmation of Canadian market/regulatory status and any available access pathway (e.g., Special Access Programme)
