## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for dicyclomine: "Dicyclomine relieves smooth muscle spasm of the gastrointestinal tract. Animal studies indicate that this action is achieved via a dual mechanism: a specific anticholinergic effect (antimuscarinic) at the acetylcholine-receptor sites with approximately 1/8 the milligram potency of atropine ( in vitro, guinea pig ileum); and a direct effect upon smooth muscle (musculotropic) as evidenced by dicyclomine’s antagonism of bradykinin- and histamine-induced spasms of the isolated guinea pig ileum." ([DailyMed: dicyclomine hydrochloride prescribing information, Slate Run Pharmaceuticals, LLC](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=e20a1ccf-2f3a-4872-811e-d93fb56b8e83), label version 2026-11-13; accessed 2026-11-19.) Dicyclomine is generally understood as an antimuscarinic/antispasmodic agent that relaxes smooth muscle.

Cauda equina syndrome often presents with bladder and bowel dysfunction. A theoretical link exists only for symptom management, such as bowel spasm or detrusor overactivity. This link is weak: anticholinergic activity could worsen urinary retention, which is a core feature of the condition.

Cauda equina syndrome is a surgical emergency caused by nerve root compression. No mechanism is established by which dicyclomine would treat the underlying cause. The high score is a computational prediction only and is not backed by clinical evidence.
