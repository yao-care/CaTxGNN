# Lactulose: From Hepatic Encephalopathy to Obstructive Jaundice

Lactulose is a non-absorbable sugar originally used to treat hepatic encephalopathy, hepatic coma and constipation.

That prediction currently has **0 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications**, mostly older or preclinical.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Based on known information, lactulose is a non-absorbable disaccharide that acts in the gut as an osmotic laxative. It acidifies the colon and traps ammonia, which is why it works in hepatic encephalopathy and constipation.

In obstructive jaundice, bile salts are absent from the gut. This promotes bacterial overgrowth, a leaky intestinal barrier and endotoxaemia, which in turn contribute to kidney dysfunction and infections after surgery. Lactulose has been reported to reduce gut endotoxin absorption and to help restore barrier function. That gives a plausible gut-mediated link to its existing liver and bowel uses.

Several cautions apply. Most of the evidence is old, and perioperative care for this condition has changed since. Only titles and short abstracts were available, so study designs and effect sizes could not be confirmed. A review (PMID 9145459) notes that kidney protection from lactulose has not been conclusively shown in clinical studies.

The other predictions have little or no mechanistic support. For acute urate nephropathy and exercise-induced malignant hyperthermia, no link to lactulose's gut action is apparent, and the high scores likely reflect knowledge-graph artefacts.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report, so please refer to the package insert for safety information.

**Contraindication signals from the knowledge graph** (not label text):
- Diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis
- Galactosemia
- Glycogen storage disease
- G6PD deficiency
- Cystinuria
- Glucose-galactose malabsorption
- Appendicitis

**Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded. The 20 reviewed here are mostly moderate, with one minor. Examples include:
- Abiraterone
- Tramadol
- Acetazolamide
- Hydrocortisone
- Salbutamol
- Hydrochlorothiazide
- Amiloride
- Amiodarone
- Propofol
- Triamcinolone
- Arsenic trioxide

Several of these are QT-prolonging drugs or drugs affecting electrolytes. Lactulose can cause diarrhoea and electrolyte loss, so that may be relevant, but this is not stated in the data and should be checked against the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- Obstructive jaundice has a plausible gut-barrier and endotoxin mechanism and some older clinical and animal reports (L3), but no registered trials. The one randomized study's results and the overall benefit are unconfirmed, and a review notes that kidney protection was never conclusively shown. The other seven predictions are supported only by indirect evidence or none (L4–L5).
- Health Canada package insert data are missing, and the safety screening cannot proceed without them.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada, which block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Full-text review of PMID 2032107 and 3768644 to confirm design, outcomes and effect sizes
- A check of whether more recent studies (the existing evidence is mostly from 1986–2005) support lactulose in current perioperative care for obstructive jaundice
- Review of the diabetes-related and galactosemia contraindication signals before any use in affected patients
- No further work is warranted on acute urate nephropathy, nephrolithiasis or exercise-induced malignant hyperthermia without a supporting mechanistic rationale
