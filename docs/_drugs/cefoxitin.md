# Cefoxitin: From a Systemic Cephamycin Antibiotic to Infectious Otitis Media

## One-Sentence Summary

Cefoxitin is a cephamycin-class injectable antibiotic. The source data does not record its original approved indication.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **infectious otitis media**, but there are **0 clinical trials** and only **2 publications** on this indication, and neither shows that cefoxitin treats it.
This is a model-driven hypothesis with very little direct support.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Cefoxitin is a cephamycin that inhibits bacterial cell-wall synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs). It also resists hydrolysis by many beta-lactamases. Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source record, so this rationale rests on general knowledge of the drug class.

Otitis media is often bacterial, so an antibacterial agent is a plausible fit. But the case is weak:
- No otic formulation is listed, and only systemic injectable products are recorded.
- There are no comparative efficacy data against first-line agents.
- The supporting literature only describes pathogens that can cause otitis media. It does not report cefoxitin treatment outcomes.

The prediction is best read as a class-level suggestion, not evidence of efficacy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The 99.30% model score is not backed by any clinical trial or treatment-outcome study in otitis media, and the only literature is background on pathogens. Other predictions in this pack have stronger support, notably **urinary tract infection** (Evidence Level L3) and **gonococcal urethritis** (Evidence Level L3). Both are probably historically labeled uses rather than true repurposing, and the gonococcal data are old and small. The ESBL-related urinary infection trials are terminated and underpowered (n=6 and n=9).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- The approved indications for the two Canadian DINs, to establish the true original indication
- Any controlled efficacy data for cefoxitin in otitis media, including a comparison against first-line agents
