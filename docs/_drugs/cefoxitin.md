# Cefoxitin: From Bacterial Infections to Urinary Tract Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Cefoxitin is a cephamycin antibiotic originally used for bacterial infections such as pneumonia, peritonitis, gonorrhea and staphylococcal, streptococcal and *E. coli* infections.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **urinary tract infection**, with **1 clinical trial** (terminated, 6 participants) and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.

Note: the top-ranked prediction by score is infectious otitis media (99.30%), but it has no clinical trials and only one irrelevant review. This report therefore focuses on the best-supported prediction, urinary tract infection (99.26%). Other predictions are summarised at the end of the report.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, cefoxitin is a cephamycin (a beta-lactam class related to cephalosporins) with stability against many beta-lactamases and activity against Gram-positive cocci, Enterobacterales such as *E. coli*, and anaerobes. Its efficacy in several bacterial infections is established.

Urinary tract infections are commonly caused by *E. coli* and other Enterobacterales, which overlap with the organisms covered by cefoxitin's existing indications. A murine model of UTI caused by CTX-M-15 ESBL-producing *E. coli* suggested cefoxitin could be a carbapenem-sparing option. Older clinical studies from 1978–1979 also describe its use in complicated UTI.

Two caveats apply. UTI is not among the listed original indications, so this would be a true expansion. Current resistance patterns also need verification before any use.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related diseases (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: kidney disease, pyoureter, *Clostridium difficile* colitis, perinatal necrotizing enterocolitis, colitis. Kidney disease is relevant to UTI patients, who may need renal function assessment and dose consideration.
- **Drug Interactions**: 35 interactions were identified. Moderate-level interactions include:
  - Aminoglycosides: kanamycin, neomycin, streptomycin
  - Picosulfuric acid
  - Dicoumarol

  Heparin is a minor interaction. Many others (such as vancomycin, metronidazole, omeprazole and morphine) are of unclassified severity.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available. Please refer to the package insert for complete safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails** (research question only)

**Rationale:**
Cefoxitin has plausible activity against the common UTI pathogens, and there is preclinical and older clinical support, particularly for ESBL *E. coli*. However, the only registered trial was terminated with 6 patients, and no Phase 2 or 3 RCT evidence exists. The evidence therefore supports further investigation, not clinical adoption.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada, and approved indication text for both DINs
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Current local resistance data for *E. coli* and other uropathogens, including ESBL and AmpC producers
- A properly powered randomised trial against a standard comparator
- Renal dosing guidance and a monitoring plan for patients with kidney disease or a history of colitis
