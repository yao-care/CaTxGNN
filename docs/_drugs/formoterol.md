# Formoterol: From Obstructive Airway Disease (Asthma/COPD) to Respiratory Malformation

## One-Sentence Summary

Formoterol is a long-acting beta2-agonist bronchodilator sold in Canada mainly in inhaled combination products for asthma and COPD.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **respiratory malformation**, but none of the **25 clinical trials** and **10 publications** retrieved for this term study malformation.
All of them concern asthma or COPD, so this prediction has no direct support.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Formoterol is a beta2-adrenergic agonist that relaxes airway smooth muscle and improves airflow in asthma and COPD. This is general pharmacology and not taken from the Evidence Pack.

Respiratory malformation means structural congenital defects of the lung or airways. Bronchodilation cannot correct such defects, so no plausible mechanistic link to the original use is evident.

The high score most likely reflects proximity to respiratory terms in the knowledge graph. The retrieved studies support this reading: every one concerns asthma or COPD.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a knowledge-graph score. There is no plausible mechanism for a bronchodilator to treat structural congenital defects, and no trial or paper studies this indication.

The pack's stronger predictions for this drug are **asthma** and **obstructive lung disease** (ranks 5 and 4). Both are label-consistent uses supported by Phase 3 RCTs, so they are existing indications and not new repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada monograph warnings and contraindications (blocking data gap)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Original approved-indication text for the Canadian licences
- Evidence that formoterol benefits patients with a respiratory malformation. Without it, this candidate should not advance.
