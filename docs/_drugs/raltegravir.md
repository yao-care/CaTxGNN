## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Raltegravir is an HIV-1 integrase strand transfer inhibitor. It blocks the viral enzyme that inserts viral DNA into the host genome.

Feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) is a lentivirus, like HIV-1, and also depends on an integrase enzyme. This shared target is a plausible reason for the high TxGNN score.

The score appears to reflect similarity to HIV-1 rather than FIV-specific data. No feline studies were retrieved. FIV is also a veterinary condition, so it has limited relevance as a human drug-repurposing indication.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on mechanistic similarity between HIV-1 and FIV. No feline data exist, and the two Phase 3 trials are human HIV-1 studies with raltegravir as the comparator. The condition is also veterinary, which limits its value as a human repurposing indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FIV-specific in vitro or in vivo data (integrase susceptibility, feline efficacy studies)
- Confirmation of whether a veterinary indication is within the project's scope
- Raltegravir's approved indication, mechanism of action, and package insert safety information from Health Canada and DrugBank

**Related prediction (rank 2):** Simian immunodeficiency virus infection has macaque preclinical literature (19 records) supporting raltegravir activity. It is more useful as a translational model for HIV reservoir, resistance, and cure research than as a new human indication. The only listed trial (NCT00863668) was withdrawn with zero enrollment.
