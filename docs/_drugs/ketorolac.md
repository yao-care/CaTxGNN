## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on established NSAID pharmacology, ketorolac is a non-selective COX-1/COX-2 inhibitor. By blocking prostaglandin synthesis, it reduces peripheral and central sensitization and neurogenic inflammation, which contribute to migraine and tension-type headache pain.

Its original indications are inflammatory eye conditions, where reducing prostaglandin-driven inflammation is the benefit. The same anti-inflammatory and analgesic action is plausible for headache pain. This link rests on general NSAID pharmacology, not on the supplied data, but it is consistent with the clinical evidence below. Parenteral ketorolac is already used in emergency departments for acute migraine.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from knowledge-graph drug–disease edges, not label text):** hypertension, gastric and peptic ulcer disease (including active), liver disease, gallbladder disease, rheumatoid arthritis, inflammatory bowel disease, asthma (intrinsic and allergic), diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, blood coagulation disorders and hemophilia, stroke and cerebrovascular disorders, cerebral infarction, myocardial infarction and coronary thrombosis, hemoglobinopathy and anemia, impaired renal function, cardiovascular and heart disease, and dry eye syndrome.
- **Safety signal in the literature:** a case report describes renal papillary necrosis after emergency treatment of migraine with parenteral DHE, sumatriptan and ketorolac. Another case report describes lithium toxicity when ketorolac was co-administered.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were identified; 20 are shown in the compiled data.
  - **Major:** acetylsalicylic acid.
  - **Moderate:** sulfonylureas (acetohexamide, chlorpropamide, glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide), corticosteroids (betamethasone, budesonide, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone), balsalazide, exenatide, fenfluramine, dexfenfluramine, kanamycin and levofloxacin.
  - **Minor:** famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine and linaclotide.
- Package insert warnings were not available in the compiled data. Please refer to the Health Canada package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3/4 trials, meta-analyses and guidelines support parenteral ketorolac for acute migraine and tension-type headache, and the drug is already widely marketed in Canada. Known NSAID risks (renal toxicity, GI bleeding, cardiovascular events) and many interactions require explicit limits on dose and treatment duration.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Approved indication text and dosage forms for the Canadian licences, to confirm which formulations (e.g., injection vs ophthalmic) match headache use
- A screening protocol for renal function, GI bleeding risk and cardiovascular history, and a check of concomitant medications (e.g., lithium, aspirin)
- Route compatibility assessment for the intended headache formulations (IV, IM, intranasal)
