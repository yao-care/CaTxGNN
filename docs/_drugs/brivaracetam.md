## Brivaracetam: From Partial Epilepsy to Visual Epilepsy
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Brivaracetam is an antiseizure medication originally used to treat partial (focal-onset) epilepsy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **visual epilepsy**, but the data compiled for this report contain **no clinical trials** and **18 publications**, none of which address visually induced seizures specifically, so the supporting evidence is indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. The literature does describe brivaracetam as a high-affinity ligand of synaptic vesicle protein 2A (SV2A), a propyl analogue of levetiracetam. It binds SV2A with 15- to 30-fold higher affinity than levetiracetam, and it penetrates the brain rapidly.

Its antiseizure effect in focal-onset seizures is established. Visual epilepsy (photosensitive or visually triggered seizures) is a reflex epilepsy. Reducing synaptic vesicle release and neuronal hyperexcitability could plausibly help here as well.

However, none of the retrieved articles examine visually induced seizures directly. They are general reviews on status epilepticus, antiseizure drug mechanisms, pregnancy, and adjunctive focal-seizure trials. One related item is a 2023 paper on the photosensitivity model (PMID 36528008), which used photoparoxysmal EEG responses to compare brivaracetam and levetiracetam. It was retrieved under the status epilepticus prediction rather than this one. The link to visual epilepsy therefore remains a hypothesis.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered for visual epilepsy.

For context, among the other predicted indications, one completed trial exists for status epilepticus: [NCT07163572](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07163572) (IV brivaracetam vs levetiracetam in children, n=152, phase NA, no results available).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is high, and brivaracetam's SV2A mechanism makes a benefit in reflex epilepsy plausible. However, there are no clinical trials and no literature specific to visual epilepsy, so the support is indirect. Package insert safety information has not yet been obtained, so safety screening cannot proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Download and review the Health Canada package insert for warnings, contraindications, dosage forms and approved indications
- Detailed mechanism of action data (for example, from DrugBank)
- A targeted literature search on brivaracetam in photosensitive epilepsy, including the photosensitivity model study (PMID 36528008)
- If the hypothesis holds up, a small exploratory study using photoparoxysmal EEG response as an endpoint

**Note on other predicted indications:** Status epilepticus has the strongest supporting evidence (L3: two systematic reviews, several observational studies, and one completed but unreported comparative trial) and may be a better first candidate to pursue. Audiogenic seizures have preclinical support only. The remaining predictions (eating, thinking, orgasm-induced and micturition-induced seizures, startle epilepsy, adolescent/adult onset autosomal dominant epilepsy with auditory features, and beta-ketothiolase deficiency) rely on the model score alone or on indirect evidence. The beta-ketothiolase deficiency prediction appears to reflect network proximity to epilepsy rather than biology.
