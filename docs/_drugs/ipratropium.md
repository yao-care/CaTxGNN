# Ipratropium: From Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease to Obstructive Lung Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Ipratropium is an inhaled anticholinergic bronchodilator originally used for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), rhinitis, bronchitis and related airway conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **obstructive lung disease**, with **25 clinical trials** and **19 publications** compiled for this direction.
This prediction largely overlaps with the existing COPD use, so it is closer to on-label use than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Ipratropium is a muscarinic receptor antagonist. It blocks muscarinic receptors on airway smooth muscle, which reduces cholinergic (vagal) bronchoconstriction and mucus secretion. Detailed mechanism-of-action data was not available in the data compiled for this report, but the published reviews describe this as the established mechanism.

COPD is itself a form of obstructive lung disease. The predicted indication therefore sits within the drug's existing use rather than outside it. Asthma, acute exacerbations and COPD all involve airway narrowing that is partly driven by cholinergic tone, which is why anticholinergics are used alone or combined with salbutamol (a beta-agonist).

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease contraindication signals (knowledge graph, not label text):** The graph links ipratropium to caution in angle-closure glaucoma, benign prostatic hyperplasia and bladder neck obstruction, which fit anticholinergic effects. It also lists cardiovascular conditions (hypertension, long QT syndrome, myocardial ischaemia, coronary artery disease, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease), diabetes and diabetic ketoacidosis, hyperthyroidism, epilepsy, potassium deficiency and metabolic acidosis. Some of these probably reflect the salbutamol component of combination products. They should be confirmed against the approved labelling.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were found, all of Moderate level among those shown. Most involve other anticholinergic or antimuscarinic agents (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium, scopolamine, dicyclomine, trospium, aclidinium), with additive anticholinergic effects. Others include promethazine, chlorpheniramine, eluxadoline, prucalopride and inhaled insulin.
- **Case reports in the literature:** Severe anaphylaxis after inhaled ipratropium (PMID 8449120) and unilateral mydriasis after inhalation in an infant (PMID 40069469).

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several large, completed Phase 3 trials, including one with 1,118 COPD participants, plus Cochrane reviews support ipratropium as a bronchodilator in obstructive airway disease. Because this overlaps with its approved COPD use, the case is strong but is closer to on-label use than to new repurposing. The evidence level of L1 should be read cautiously: many trials are active-comparator or device studies, and the data contains no placebo-controlled efficacy summary specific to ipratropium alone.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Canadian package insert warnings, contraindications and approved-indication text, which are missing from the compiled data
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A clear definition of the target population within "obstructive lung disease" (COPD vs asthma, adult vs paediatric)
- A review of the anticholinergic and cardiovascular safety signals, especially in patients with glaucoma, prostatic or bladder outlet obstruction, or heart rhythm disorders
