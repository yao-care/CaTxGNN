## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank is not available in the Evidence Pack. Ipratropium is a non-selective muscarinic antagonist (M1–M3). It blocks vagally mediated cholinergic bronchoconstriction and reduces airway mucus secretion. Published reviews in the evidence set describe it as a quaternary derivative of atropine that acts on cholinergic receptors of bronchial smooth muscle.

Blocking cholinergic tone is directly relevant to obstructive lung disease such as COPD and asthma, where increased vagal tone contributes to airflow limitation. The trials and reviews linked to this prediction are dominated by COPD bronchodilator studies. These include comparisons with tiotropium, salbutamol/albuterol combinations and long-acting beta-agonists.

The input lists no original indication, so the prediction cannot be shown to be a new use. Ipratropium is a long-established COPD bronchodilator, and this prediction most likely restates an approved use.

## Safety Considerations

Warnings, contraindications and drug interaction data are not available in the Evidence Pack. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

Two safety signals appear in the literature linked to other predicted indications and are worth noting:
- **Anaphylaxis**: A case report describes severe anaphylaxis after ipratropium inhalation (PMID 8449120).
- **Unilateral mydriasis**: A case report describes it in an infant after inhaled ipratropium (PMID 40069469).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two completed Phase 3 randomized trials involving ipratropium (NCT02177253, NCT00274040) support Evidence Level L1 for obstructive airway disease. The plausible mechanism is muscarinic blockade of cholinergic bronchoconstriction. The evidence reflects established COPD use and should not be treated as a new repurposing finding until the approved label is checked.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking safety data gap).
- Approved indication text for the Canadian DINs, to confirm whether this is on-label use.
- DrugBank mechanism of action data.
- Confirmation of ipratropium's role in the trials with truncated titles (NCT01218126, NCT00691405, NCT02194205).

**Other predicted indications** (nasal cavity disease, pharyngitis, respiratory malformation, tracheal disease, anaphylaxis and others) are graded L4–L5 with Hold or Research Question recommendations. They have little or no supporting evidence, and the anaphylaxis prediction conflicts with a reported safety signal.
