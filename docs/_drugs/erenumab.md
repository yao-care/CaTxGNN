# Erenumab: From Migraine to Migraine with Brainstem Aura

## One-Sentence Summary

Erenumab is a monoclonal antibody that blocks the CGRP receptor and was originally developed for migraine prevention.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**,
but there are currently **no registered clinical trials** for this subtype. The direction is supported only by indirect evidence from **20 publications** on migraine in general and migraine with aura.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Erenumab blocks the CGRP receptor. In laboratory cell assays it abolished CGRP-stimulated cAMP production (IC50 about 2.3 nM) and was about 5000-fold more selective for the CGRP receptor than for other human calcitonin-family receptors. CGRP signalling plays a central role in migraine, and erenumab is already used as a once-monthly preventive injection. Its approval was supported by the Phase 3 ARISE trial and Phase 2 data showing fewer migraine days at the highest dose (70 mg).

Migraine with brainstem aura is a rarer subtype that shares the core biology of the approved indication, which likely explains the high TxGNN score. Indirect support comes from studies in migraine with and without aura. A post hoc analysis of randomized trials in JAMA Neurology (2022) assessed erenumab safety and efficacy in both groups. The REFORM study (2026) examined how erenumab affects aura frequency.

However, no study specific to brainstem aura was identified. Whether this subtype responds to CGRP-receptor blockade remains unconfirmed. Detailed mechanism-of-action data are also not available in the compiled data, so this assessment relies on the known pharmacology described above.

## Safety Considerations

- **Vascular risk:** Aura and brainstem symptoms can overlap with ischemic events. The available hemodynamic and vascular-risk studies in general migraine populations are reassuring, but they do not cover brainstem aura specifically.
- **Drug interactions:** The only interaction on record is pharmacological. Erenumab acts on its target, the CGRP receptor. No drug–drug interactions were identified.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

---

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high and the mechanism is plausible. Erenumab is already marketed in Canada for migraine. However, no trials target brainstem aura, and the supporting literature covers migraine in general or migraine with aura only. The evidence is therefore at the L4 level, which makes this a research question rather than a ready candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data for erenumab
- Subgroup or case-based evidence specific to migraine with brainstem aura, and ideally a registered trial
- A dedicated vascular and ischemic safety assessment for patients with brainstem aura symptoms
