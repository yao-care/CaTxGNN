## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Cefprozil is a second-generation oral cephalosporin. It inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins. Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report, so this description reflects the known class pharmacology.

The drug is active in vitro against common urinary pathogens such as *E. coli*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae* and *Proteus mirabilis*. A study of clinical isolates from Taiwan also found good activity against *E. coli* and *K. pneumoniae*. A urinary tract infection indication is therefore mechanistically plausible for an antibacterial of this kind.

Three comparative studies (1991–1992) tested cefprozil once daily against cefaclor in acute uncomplicated urinary tract infection. One of them, in 108 college women, reported clinical and bacterial cure rates of 94% and 93% with cefprozil, versus 94% and 94% with cefaclor, with no significant difference. The study designs and phases here are inferred from titles and abstracts, so the evidence level is provisional.
