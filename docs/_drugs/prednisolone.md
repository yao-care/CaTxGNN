# Prednisolone: From Autoimmune and Inflammatory Diseases to Alopecia Areata

## One-Sentence Summary

Prednisolone is a systemic glucocorticoid originally used for autoimmune and inflammatory conditions (such as Crohn disease, inflammatory bowel disease and adrenocortical insufficiency), as well as for certain leukaemias.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia areata**, with **no registered clinical trials** but **20 publications** (including a network meta-analysis, systematic reviews and a placebo-controlled trial of oral pulse prednisolone) supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Based on known information, prednisolone is a glucocorticoid whose efficacy in autoimmune and inflammatory disease is well established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to alopecia areata.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune attack on hair follicles, with a collapse of the follicle's immune privilege. Glucocorticoids suppress T-cell activation and pro-inflammatory cytokines, which directly addresses this pathology. One included study also reports changes in serum and tissue TNF-α levels as a possible mechanism of oral pulse steroids in this disease.

The original indications already include several autoimmune and inflammatory diseases, so extending to another autoimmune condition is a natural step. The main limitations are relapse after treatment is withdrawn and the toxicity of long-term systemic steroids, which is why pulse regimens are the focus of the literature.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text):** the knowledge graph links prednisolone with contraindications including hypertension, congestive heart failure, hyperglycaemia, epilepsy, herpes simplex keratitis, measles, porphyria, and several urea cycle and pituitary-related disorders. These should be checked against the product label.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified in total. Among those reviewed, the one rated **Major** is **adalimumab**. Moderate interactions include ibuprofen, ketorolac, nifedipine, ethinylestradiol, isotretinoin, acarbose, acetazolamide, aldesleukin, alemtuzumab and aliskiren. Minor interactions include formoterol and salbutamol.
- **Treatment-related risks noted in the literature:** relapse after withdrawal and systemic steroid toxicity limit long-term use.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Evidence for alopecia areata includes a network meta-analysis, systematic reviews and a placebo-controlled oral pulse prednisolone trial, and the mechanism fits a T-cell-mediated autoimmune disease. However, no registered trials are available, relapse after withdrawal is common, and long-term systemic steroid toxicity limits use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, which are currently missing
- Mechanism of action data
- Registered or new controlled trials with defined pulse dosing regimens, especially in children
- A relapse-management and steroid-toxicity monitoring plan (growth, bone, infection and metabolic effects)
- A review of interactions, particularly with adalimumab
