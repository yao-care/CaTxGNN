## Magnesium Hydroxide: From Peptic Ulcer Disease to Stomach Disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Magnesium hydroxide is an antacid that neutralizes stomach acid. It is a known antacid used for conditions such as peptic ulcer disease, gastroesophageal reflux disease, esophagitis, constipation and dyspepsia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **stomach disease**, but the data compiled for this report contain **no registered clinical trials** for this condition. The 20 publications are mostly older reviews, pharmacology studies, animal studies and studies of antacid combinations.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available. Based on general antacid pharmacology, magnesium hydroxide neutralizes gastric acid and raises intragastric pH.

"Stomach disease" is a broad parent category that largely overlaps with those recorded conditions. The high TxGNN score (0.9923) most likely reflects closeness within the disease ontology rather than a genuinely new repurposing signal.

One rat study suggests an aluminium/magnesium hydroxide antacid protects the gastric mucosa, possibly through endogenous prostaglandins (PMID 2595273). This is mechanistically plausible but has not been validated in humans. The study also tested a combination product, not magnesium hydroxide alone.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

Currently no related clinical trials registered.

## Safety Considerations

- **Relations recorded in the knowledge graph (not label text):** The knowledge graph lists contraindication relations with kidney disease and impaired renal function, liver disease, nephrolithiasis, hypercalcemia, hyperparathyroidism, hypophosphatemia, diarrheal disease, appendicitis, intestinal impaction, hemorrhoid, and several gastric cancers. These are not confirmed by package insert text and need checking against the label.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found in total. The only Major one in the displayed list is **dolutegravir**. Moderate interactions include doxycycline, alendronic acid, hydrochlorothiazide, amiodarone, tramadol, acetazolamide, hydrocortisone and abiraterone. Minor ones include famotidine, ranitidine and ibuprofen. Antacids commonly reduce absorption of some drugs, so dosing separation may be needed.
- **Literature notes:** Reviews of antacid therapy note changes in mineral metabolism, mainly with aluminium-containing antacids.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
"Stomach disease" is a broad category that mostly overlaps with the conditions already recorded for this drug, so the high score is probably not a new repurposing signal. No registered trials or published studies directly address this condition, so the evidence level is L5. Package insert warnings are also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A narrower, clinically defined target condition (for example gastritis) instead of the broad "stomach disease" label
- Clinical studies of magnesium hydroxide alone, rather than combinations with aluminium hydroxide
- A review of the DDI list, especially dolutegravir, and of the knowledge graph contraindications against the label
