# Cyproheptadine: From Allergic Rhinitis to Allergic Urticaria

## One-Sentence Summary

Cyproheptadine is a first-generation antihistamine, originally used for allergic conditions such as allergic rhinitis, urticaria and conjunctivitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **allergic urticaria**, and it also ranks **cold urticaria** as a second prediction.
For allergic urticaria, **no registered clinical trials** and **9 publications** (mostly older studies) are available. For cold urticaria, there are **no registered trials** and **17 publications**, including several double-blind comparative studies.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, cyproheptadine is a first-generation H1 antihistamine with additional antiserotonergic (5-HT2) activity. Its efficacy in allergic conditions is long established, and mechanistically it is well suited to urticaria.

In allergic urticaria, histamine released from mast cells drives wheals and itching, so H1 blockade acts directly on the cause of symptoms. Urticaria and angioedema are already among cyproheptadine's recognised original indications. This prediction is therefore better understood as an on-label or near-label use than as a true repurposing signal, and the very high model score is consistent with known pharmacology.

The second-ranked prediction, **cold urticaria** (score 99.76%), follows the same logic. Cold triggers mast cell degranulation and histamine release, and cyproheptadine's antiserotonergic activity may add benefit. The older literature includes a double-blind comparison against chlorpheniramine and placebo. However, second-generation antihistamines are now generally preferred first-line because they are better tolerated, so cyproheptadine is best regarded as a historical or alternative option.

Two further predictions, **nasal cavity disease** (99.21%) and **acute laryngopharyngitis** (99.13%), rest on the model score and class pharmacology alone, with no trials or literature. Nasal cavity disease is a broad term that adds no new specific entity. For laryngopharyngitis, antihistamines have limited proven benefit in acute infection, and their drying effect could worsen throat irritation.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (not taken from label text): hypertension and hypertensive disorder, peptic ulcer disease, benign prostatic hyperplasia, ocular hypertension, angle-closure (closed-angle) glaucoma, hyperthyroidism (including Graves disease and thyrotoxicosis), and bladder neck obstruction.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Major interactions include **potassium citrate** and **potassium chloride**. Moderate interactions include anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, scopolamine, glycopyrronium, dicyclomine, trospium, propantheline, methscopolamine, mepenzolate), opioids and related agents (morphine, opium, loperamide, eluxadoline), cannabinoids (dronabinol, nabilone), metoclopramide and fenfluramine.
- **Practical cautions** (from class pharmacology): sedation and anticholinergic effects; extra care in children, the elderly, and patients with glaucoma or urinary retention; awareness of systemic cold-induced reactions in cold urticaria.

Please also refer to the package insert, as Health Canada label warnings and contraindications were not available in the compiled data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
- Urticaria is already a recognised indication, and controlled comparative studies exist, particularly in cold urticaria, so efficacy is plausible. However, there are no registered trials, the studies are old and small, and second-generation antihistamines are now preferred first-line. The nasal cavity disease and acute laryngopharyngitis predictions should be held, as they have no supporting evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Approved indication text and dosage forms for the Canadian licences
- Review of current guideline positioning against second-generation antihistamines
- A safety plan for sedation, anticholinergic effects and the high interaction burden, especially in children and older adults
