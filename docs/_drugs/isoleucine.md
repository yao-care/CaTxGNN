## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Isoleucine is an essential amino acid involved in protein synthesis and glucose metabolism. It is supplied as part of amino acid nutrition solutions, and no formal original indication is recorded for it. Mechanistic reasoning for gastroparesis therefore has to be speculative.

One possible link is glycemic control, which matters in diabetic gastroparesis. This has not been validated, and no data in this pack supports it.

There is also a reason for caution. Dietary amino acids can slow gastric emptying through gut hormone signaling (for example CCK and GLP-1). An adverse effect on gastric motility is therefore plausible and cannot be excluded. The high TxGNN score (rank 11,790 among predictions) is a knowledge-graph association, not evidence of benefit.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is supported only by a model score (Evidence Level L5), with no clinical trials, no literature, and no validated mechanism. Amino acids may also slow gastric emptying, so harm in gastroparesis cannot be ruled out.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank), to test the proposed link to gastroparesis
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, which are needed before any safety screening
- A systematic search of trials and literature on amino acids or isoleucine and gastric emptying or gastroparesis
- Confirmation of the original indication and approved routes (the products are parenteral, so route compatibility with any gastroparesis use is unassessed)
