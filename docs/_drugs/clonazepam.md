# Clonazepam: From Epilepsy and Anxiety Disorder to Restless Legs Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Clonazepam is a benzodiazepine originally used for seizure disorders (such as absence epilepsy and Lennox-Gastaut syndrome) and anxiety disorder.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Restless Legs Syndrome**,

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, clonazepam is a benzodiazepine that enhances GABA-A receptor-mediated inhibition. Its efficacy in seizure disorders and anxiety is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to restless legs syndrome (RLS).

Enhanced inhibitory signalling may reduce the sensory-motor hyperexcitability and sleep fragmentation seen in RLS and periodic limb movements. Clonazepam has been used for RLS for decades, which is consistent with the very high TxGNN score.

An important caveat: the 2025 American Academy of Sleep Medicine guideline places benzodiazepines below first-line options such as alpha-2-delta ligands and iron therapy. Clonazepam should therefore be viewed as an alternative or adjunct, not an equivalent of first-line treatment.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (not label text): liver failure and liver disease, kidney disease, inherited porphyria and porphyria, open-angle and angle-closure glaucoma, obstructive sleep apnea and other sleep apnea syndromes, respiratory failure, congenital central hypoventilation syndrome, substance-related disorder and substance abuse/dependence, gallbladder disease, and pyoureter.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions are recorded. The major ones listed include **morphine** and **liposomal morphine** (additive central nervous system and respiratory depression is the usual concern). Moderate interactions include bupropion, aprepitant, omeprazole, cimetidine, clarithromycin, dronabinol, nabilone, metoclopramide, opium, sibutramine and teduglutide. Antacids such as magnesium oxide, aluminum hydroxide and calcium carbonate are rated minor.
- **Literature safety signal**: one case report describes mania associated with clonazepam (PMID 1949970).
- **Class concerns**: dependence, tolerance, cognitive impairment and falls, particularly in elderly patients. These are general benzodiazepine considerations, not drawn from Canadian label text.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications, as label text was not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Clonazepam has long-standing clinical use in RLS, a Cochrane systematic review, and a current AASM guideline that addresses it, and it is already marketed in Canada. However, it is positioned below first-line agents, no clinical trials are registered, and the available studies are small or of unspecified design. The high TxGNN score (99.65%) reflects existing use more than new evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the Cochrane review and AASM guideline for the exact strength of recommendation and the quality of underlying RCTs
- Canadian package insert warnings and contraindications, and approved indication text for the DINs
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A risk plan for dependence, falls, respiratory depression and opioid co-prescription, with attention to older patients and those with sleep apnea or hepatic/renal impairment
