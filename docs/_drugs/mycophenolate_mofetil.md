## One-Sentence Summary

Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) is an immunosuppressant marketed in Canada under many licences. It is predicted by the TxGNN model to be effective for **HIV infectious disease**, with **4 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** in the data compiled for this report. ## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for MMF were not available in the compiled data. The following rationale comes from the repurposing analysis. Its active form, mycophenolic acid, inhibits inosine monophosphate dehydrogenase (IMPDH), which depletes intracellular guanosine nucleotide pools. This has two effects relevant to HIV:

- It limits T-cell proliferation, and HIV disease progression is linked to chronic immune activation.
- It can lower HIV replication and potentiate nucleoside analogues such as abacavir and didanosine by shifting dNTP pool ratios.

Published work supports this link. A small series reported that adding MMF to an abacavir-containing regimen was associated with depletion of intracellular dGTP and a fall in plasma HIV-1 RNA. A randomised study (PMID 15353978) examined HAART with or without MMF in treatment-naive patients.

Clinical data are limited to small trials with mixed virologic benefit. Immunosuppression in people with HIV also carries a safety risk. The prediction is therefore mechanistically plausible but not clinically established.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindication-related conditions from the knowledge graph** (these come from drug-disease relationship data, not from label text):
- Haematological: neutropenia, agranulocytosis, aplastic anaemia (including inherited forms), Fanconi anaemia, Diamond-Blackfan anaemia
- Infections: cytomegalovirus infection, herpes simplex, herpes zoster, progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy
- Malignancy: cancer, lymphoma, primary CNS lymphoma, skin cancer
- Organ conditions: liver disease, gallbladder disease, active peptic ulcer disease

**Drug interactions:** 92 interactions were identified. Examples include:
- **Major:** activated charcoal
- **Moderate:** proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, rabeprazole, dexlansoprazole), antibiotics (amoxicillin, clarithromycin, doxycycline, minocycline, tetracycline, vancomycin, kanamycin, paromomycin), and antacids (aluminium hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magnesium oxide)
- **Minor:** zinc sulfate, zinc acetate, iron

Immunosuppression in HIV-infected patients carries an additional infection risk. Please refer to the package insert for the official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and there is early clinical work, but the only directly relevant Phase 2 HIV trial was withdrawn with no participants. The remaining evidence is small studies with mixed virologic benefit, and immunosuppression adds risk in HIV. The data also lack Health Canada label warnings and the original indication, so safety screening cannot proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indication text
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- A properly powered randomised trial of MMF in HIV, or a clear justification for such a study
- A safety plan for infection risk and myelosuppression in HIV-infected patients

**Other predictions:** Multiple sclerosis (score 0.9954, L2) has the most developed supporting evidence after HIV, including small Phase 2 MMF trials and a Cochrane review, but efficacy remains uncertain. Hemosiderosis has only indirect review-level support (L4). The other predictions (a rare neurodevelopmental disorder, Paget disease of bone, simian and feline immunodeficiency, Heiner syndrome, African iron overload, neonatal hemochromatosis) rest on model scores alone (L5) and should be held.
