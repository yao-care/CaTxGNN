# Ustekinumab: From Psoriasis to Dermatitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Ustekinumab was originally used to treat psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis and related skin conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dermatitis** (mainly atopic dermatitis), supported by **6 registered clinical trials** (2 directly relevant Phase 2 trials) and **20 publications**. The evidence is early-stage and shows modest, inconsistent efficacy.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, ustekinumab is a human monoclonal antibody that blocks the p40 subunit shared by interleukin-12 (IL-12) and interleukin-23 (IL-23). This inhibits Th1 and Th17/Th22 immune pathways.

These pathways drive inflammation in psoriasis, where ustekinumab's efficacy is established. They are also implicated in the chronic phase of atopic dermatitis, especially the Th22/IL-23 axis. This is the basis for the prediction.

The rationale is only partial, however. Atopic dermatitis is predominantly driven by Th2 immunity, which ustekinumab does not target directly. Published Phase 2 data show modest and inconsistent efficacy, so the link is plausible but not confirmed.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Two completed Phase 2 randomized trials exist in atopic dermatitis, but published efficacy is modest and inconsistent. The mechanistic rationale is only partial because the disease is mainly Th2-driven. No Phase 3 evidence exists, and package insert safety information has not yet been reviewed, which blocks safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, to enable safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Critical review of the Phase 2 trial results (NCT01945086 and NCT01806662) for effect size and responder subgroups
- Evidence that a subgroup (e.g., chronic-phase or Th22/IL-23-high disease) benefits, ideally from a larger controlled trial
- Review of interaction management, particularly with corticosteroids and anticoagulants
