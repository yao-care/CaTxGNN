# Ustekinumab: From Plaque Psoriasis to Dermatitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Ustekinumab is a monoclonal antibody targeting the shared p40 subunit of interleukin-12 (IL-12) and interleukin-23 (IL-23), historically used to treat moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, Crohn's disease, and ulcerative colitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Dermatitis** (most strongly supported for atopic dermatitis),
with **7 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, a structured mechanism-of-action record is not available in this evidence pack. However, the supporting literature within this pack consistently describes ustekinumab as a fully human IgG1 monoclonal antibody that binds the p40 subunit shared by IL-12 and IL-23, thereby suppressing downstream Th1, Th17, and Th22 activation (PMID 27304428, 36208443). This is the same immunological axis that ustekinumab already targets in its approved indication of plaque psoriasis.

Psoriasis and atopic dermatitis are both chronic, immune-mediated inflammatory skin diseases, though they are traditionally associated with different dominant T-helper pathways (Th17-driven psoriasis vs. Th2-driven atopic dermatitis). The rationale for repurposing rests on evidence that IL-12/IL-23 blockade also downregulates Th22 activity and epidermal barrier dysfunction pathways relevant to atopic dermatitis, which is why multiple randomized trials (including one in Japanese patients with severe AD) were conducted to test this hypothesis directly rather than relying on mechanism alone.

Because both diseases share inflammatory skin pathology and overlapping cytokine signaling nodes, and because ustekinumab already has an established safety and dosing record from its approved dermatologic indication (psoriasis), the TxGNN prediction for dermatitis is mechanistically plausible and is further corroborated by direct clinical investigation rather than being purely model-derived.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Clinical evidence for dermatitis (specifically atopic dermatitis) is moderately strong — two completed Phase 2 RCTs plus multiple systematic reviews support biological plausibility and efficacy signals. However, a **Blocking**-severity data gap exists: no regulatory safety/warning data (equivalent to a product monograph or package insert) is currently available, which prevents this candidate from entering the S1 safety pre-screening stage. Additionally, ustekinumab is not currently marketed in this jurisdiction (0 DINs), so no local prescribing/approval precedent exists to lean on.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Official product monograph / package insert (warnings, contraindications, drug interactions) — currently the single blocking gap (DG001)
- Confirmed mechanism-of-action documentation from a structured source (e.g., DrugBank API) rather than literature-derived inference (DG002)
- Regulatory pathway assessment given the drug is not currently marketed in this jurisdiction
- Consideration of pediatric-specific safety data, since several supporting trials/reviews involve pediatric populations
