# Ustekinumab: From Plaque Psoriasis to Dermatitis (Atopic Dermatitis)

## One-Sentence Summary

Ustekinumab is an IL-12/23 antagonist antibody, originally used for moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis, Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dermatitis**, and the evidence is almost entirely for atopic dermatitis.
Currently **6 clinical trials** and **20 publications** relate to this direction, including 2 placebo-controlled Phase 2 RCTs.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for the drug is not available in the dataset. From the repurposing rationale, ustekinumab blocks the p40 subunit shared by IL-12 and IL-23. This suppresses Th1 and Th17 signalling, and Th22 activity is also reported to be reduced.

Atopic dermatitis is mainly Th2-driven, so the link is only partial. Th17/Th22 activity is prominent in some subsets, such as Asian, paediatric, intrinsic and chronic-lesion patients. This gives a plausible rationale for benefit in those groups.

Efficacy in the Phase 2 RCTs and real-world data is described as modest and inconsistent. Any benefit is therefore likely limited to specific disease endotypes. "Dermatitis" is also a broad label, and nearly all supporting evidence concerns atopic dermatitis specifically.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Direct evidence is limited to small Phase 2 studies in atopic dermatitis (n=32 to 79). Efficacy is described as modest and inconsistent, and the Th2-driven biology of atopic dermatitis fits ustekinumab's mechanism only partially. Canadian safety information is also missing, and that gap blocks safety screening.

The other nine predicted indications (for example neonatal dermatomyositis, pityriasis lichenoides and diabetic retinopathy) have no trials or literature. They are prediction-only (L5) and should also be held.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking gap)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Approved indication text and dosage forms for the Canadian licences
- Curated evidence on atopic dermatitis endotypes (for example Th17/Th22-high, Asian or paediatric patients) that might respond
- Phase 3 or larger controlled data, or a pooled analysis of the existing Phase 2 RCTs, to resolve the inconsistent efficacy signal
