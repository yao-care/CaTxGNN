Those publications report adverse psychiatric effects and drug interactions, not benefit, so this prediction is probably a safety association rather than a therapeutic signal.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, propafenone is a class IC antiarrhythmic that blocks sodium channels. Its efficacy in arrhythmia is established, but nothing supports an antimanic role for this mechanism.

The literature suggests the high score may reflect a safety association. A 1985 case report describes mania appearing after propafenone was started. The author speculated that, because propafenone is chemically related to the antidepressant bupropion, it might have mood-altering activity. A 2001 case report describes an organic psychosis caused by a venlafaxine–propafenone interaction in a patient with bipolar disorder. A 2020 review covers harmful interactions between antipsychotics and cardiovascular drugs in patients with bipolar disorder or schizophrenia.

The graph link between propafenone and bipolar mania therefore most likely comes from co-occurrence: patients with bipolar disorder often have cardiovascular disease and receive both drug types. It is not a sign that propafenone treats mania.

## Safety Considerations

- **Reported psychiatric signals**: Propafenone has been linked to a case of drug-induced mania (PMID 2579063) and to a psychosis from a venlafaxine interaction (PMID 11949740). These are single case reports, so causality and frequency are unknown.
- **Drug Interactions**: No interaction data were retrieved for this drug. Interactions with antipsychotics and antidepressants should be checked, given the reports above.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only literature for this indication shows propafenone causing or contributing to manic and psychotic episodes, and there are no trials. Sodium channel blockade has no established antimanic role, so the 99.80% score likely reflects a safety association. This candidate is not suitable for repurposing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (MOA), to confirm whether any plausible mood-related pathway exists
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications
- Preclinical or clinical evidence of antimanic benefit, which does not currently exist

**Other predictions for this drug:** Among the other predictions in this pack, **catecholaminergic polymorphic ventricular tachycardia (CPVT)** (rank 2, L3) is the most plausible direction. Preclinical work shows class IC agents inhibit RyR2 calcium release channels, and human cohorts and case reports describe propafenone use, including a 35-year effective treatment course. Flecainide is the better-characterised agent, no RCT exists for propafenone, and structural heart disease must be excluded because class IC agents are contraindicated there. This report does not evaluate it further.
