# Capecitabine: From Gastric Cancer to Gastric Adenocarcinoma and Proximal Polyposis of the Stomach

## One-Sentence Summary

Capecitabine is an oral fluoropyrimidine chemotherapy, originally used for colorectal and gastric cancers.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **gastric adenocarcinoma and proximal polyposis of the stomach** (a rare hereditary syndrome), but **no clinical trials or publications** were retrieved for this top-ranked prediction.
Stronger evidence exists for other, closely related gastric tumour subtypes among the model's top 10 predictions, notably gastric tubular adenocarcinoma (19 publications, many Phase 3 RCTs) and gastric cardia adenocarcinoma (3 completed Phase 2 trials).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, capecitabine is an oral prodrug of 5-fluorouracil (5-FU). It is converted to 5-FU in tissue by thymidine phosphorylase, and 5-FU inhibits thymidylate synthase, which blocks DNA synthesis in rapidly dividing tumour cells. This general mechanism applies to gastric adenocarcinoma.

The predicted disease is a rare hereditary syndrome (GAPPS) in which gastric adenocarcinoma is a component. That overlap with capecitabine's established gastric cancer use is the basis of the prediction. However, no GAPPS-specific evidence was found, so the link rests on disease-class similarity alone.

The data also cover nine other predicted gastric tumour subtypes. Most are subtypes of gastric adenocarcinoma already covered by the existing gastric cancer indication, so they are better understood as extensions of the original use than as true repurposing signals.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, gastric adenocarcinoma with proximal polyposis of the stomach, has a very high model score (99.94%) but no trials or publications, so it rests on model prediction alone (L5). It is also a rare hereditary syndrome whose gastric cancer component is already covered by capecitabine's established use.

Among the other predictions, gastric tubular adenocarcinoma (L1, Proceed with Guardrails) and gastric cardia adenocarcinoma (L2, Proceed with Guardrails) have the strongest support. However, their evidence comes from general gastric/GEJ populations and mostly overlaps with the existing indication, so they are not true repurposing signals.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Approved indication text and dosage forms for the Canadian licences
- Any GAPPS-specific case series or trial data, or a decision to prioritise the better-supported subtypes
- Confirmation that evidence for tubular and cardia adenocarcinoma applies to those histologies and falls within the label
- DPD deficiency screening and a haematological, liver and renal monitoring plan
