## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for methionine is not available (data gap), and no original approved indication is recorded in this evidence pack, so a direct mechanistic bridge from "original use" to "acne" cannot be constructed from the data on hand.

The supporting literature for acne is indirect rather than interventional: one cohort study found elevated plasma homocysteine (a downstream metabolite of methionine metabolism) in patients on isotretinoin therapy for cystic acne; a case report describes a neonate with an MTHFR mutation (a gene involved in homocysteine/methionine metabolism) presenting with neonatal acne among other features; and two basic-research papers describe neutrophil/chemotactic activity abnormalities in inflammatory skin conditions including acne, without testing methionine as an intervention.

None of these studies administer or test methionine as a treatment for acne — they report methionine-pathway metabolites (homocysteine, MTHFR-related enzymes) as **associated with** acne-related conditions or treatments, not as a mechanism by which methionine would treat acne. The causal direction is therefore ambiguous, and the mechanistic link should be considered a hypothesis-generating signal only.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence for methionine in acne is limited to four indirect, non-interventional publications (L4) with no clinical trials, no established original indication, and no MOA data — the mechanistic link is speculative and the causal direction is unclear.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action (MOA) data for methionine (currently a data gap; would clarify plausibility of the acne link)
- Direct interventional or observational studies testing methionine (not just downstream metabolites like homocysteine) in acne populations
- Canadian regulatory/market status confirmation, since the product currently has no DIN on file
- Product safety labeling (key warnings, contraindications, drug interactions) — currently unavailable and blocking any safety-stage (S1) review
