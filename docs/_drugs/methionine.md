## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, methionine is an essential sulfur-containing amino acid that is used in Canada within parenteral nutrition products (for example CLINIMIX, TRAVASOL, PRIMENE and OLIMEL). It serves as a nutritional substrate rather than a drug with a defined therapeutic target.

The only link to acne in the data compiled for this report runs through one-carbon and homocysteine metabolism. Methionine is a precursor of homocysteine. One retrieved study found elevated plasma homocysteine in patients taking isotretinoin for cystic acne, and a case report of an MTHFR mutation described neonatal acne as one of several skin findings. Both are biomarker or association observations, not evidence that methionine treats acne.

Supplementing methionine could plausibly raise homocysteine, which argues against benefit. The very high TxGNN score therefore should be read as a knowledge-graph association, not as proof of a therapeutic effect.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but there are no clinical trials and the two retrieved papers are only indirect biomarker or association reports. The only mechanistic route (homocysteine metabolism) suggests possible harm rather than benefit, so there is no rationale to advance acne as a methionine indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for methionine, and a defined mechanistic hypothesis for acne
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada (these are required before safety screening)
- Preclinical or early clinical data showing that methionine, rather than homocysteine as a biomarker, affects acne
- An assessment of the homocysteine and cardiovascular risk at any proposed therapeutic dose
- Review of the other predicted indications (for example diabetic cataract, which has the most coherent indirect support through methionine sulfoxide reductase redox biology, but still only preclinical evidence), if acne is not pursued
