# Methionine: From Parenteral Nutrition Component to Acne

## One-Sentence Summary

Methionine is an essential amino acid. In Canada it is marketed as a component of parenteral nutrition products such as CLINIMIX and TRAVASOL. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **acne**, but **0 clinical trials** and only **4 loosely related publications** (case report, observational and laboratory studies) exist, and none tests methionine as a treatment.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Methionine is a sulfur-containing essential amino acid supplied as a nutritional component in parenteral amino acid solutions. Its use as a nutrient is well established. Its role in acne has not been studied.

The retrieved evidence does not support a therapeutic link to acne. The only clinical signal is an observational study reporting **elevated plasma homocysteine in patients taking isotretinoin for cystic acne** (PMID 11277950). Methionine is a precursor of homocysteine, so this finding is more likely a **safety concern** than support for efficacy. The other papers are off-target: an MTHFR mutation case report and two studies of neutrophil function in inflammatory skin disease.

The score of about 0.99999 is a graph-based prediction and has no clinical support behind it.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

Because methionine can raise homocysteine, and elevated homocysteine is associated with premature occlusive vascular disease (PMID 11277950), any exploration in acne, particularly alongside isotretinoin, would need close attention to this risk.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
No clinical trials exist, and the literature offers no evidence that methionine treats acne. The one relevant observation (raised homocysteine on isotretinoin) points toward possible harm rather than benefit. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction only.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data, to allow a real mechanistic-link analysis
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, which are needed before any safety screening
- Preclinical or mechanistic studies testing whether methionine modulates acne pathology, with homocysteine effects assessed
- Assessment of whether the parenteral-only routes of the marketed products are compatible with any acne use (route compatibility is still pending)

**Other predicted indications (all on Hold):**
- Cataract-related terms: diabetic cataract, nuclear senile cataract, cortical cataract and mature cataract have only in vitro, ex vivo or animal evidence.
- Diabetic cataract has the most plausible indirect link, through methionine sulfoxide reductase B1 (MsrB1) redox protection in lens cells (PMIDs 23270945, 22713178). It is a research question only, not a clinical recommendation.
- Severe nonproliferative diabetic retinopathy, diabetes mellitus type 2 associated cataract, craniostenosis cataract, immature cataract and tetanic cataract are prediction-only, with no trials or literature retrieved.
