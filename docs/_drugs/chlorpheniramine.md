## Chlorpheniramine: From Allergic Rhinitis to Allergic Urticaria
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Chlorpheniramine is a first-generation H1 antihistamine, originally used for allergic rhinitis, the common cold and urticaria.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **allergic urticaria**, but **no clinical trials** are registered for this indication, and the **20 publications** retrieved are mostly reviews, case reports and studies of other antihistamines.
This prediction largely overlaps with an existing labeled use (urticaria), so it is closer to an extension of current use than a true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, chlorpheniramine is a first-generation H1-receptor antagonist (inverse agonist). Allergic urticaria is driven by histamine released from skin mast cells acting on H1 receptors, so H1 blockade is mechanistically direct.

Urticaria is already among chlorpheniramine's known indications, so allergic urticaria is essentially a subtype of an existing use. The very high TxGNN score most likely reflects this overlap rather than a new therapeutic discovery.

Modern guidelines generally prefer second-generation antihistamines because of the sedation and anticholinergic burden of chlorpheniramine. It would therefore more likely serve as a comparator or fallback option than as a first-line choice.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (these come from drug-disease relationship data, not from package insert text): hypertension, liver failure and liver disease, epilepsy, insomnia, COPD, gastric and peptic ulcer, kidney disease, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, hypothyroidism, inflammatory bowel disease, psychotic and other mental disorders, diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis, benign prostatic hyperplasia, bronchitis, hypotensive disorder, and others.

**Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. The two **Major** interactions are with potassium chloride and potassium citrate. Moderate interactions include anticholinergics (atropine, dicyclomine, glycopyrronium, methscopolamine), opioids (morphine, opium, loperamide), cannabinoids (dronabinol, nabilone), bupropion, metoclopramide, eliglustat and lorcaserin.

**Hypersensitivity:** Case reports describe rare chlorpheniramine-induced urticaria and anaphylaxis, which is relevant when treating a hypersensitivity condition.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication overlaps with an existing use, no clinical trials are registered, and the supporting literature is mostly reviews, case reports and studies of other drugs. Newer second-generation antihistamines are generally preferred, so the added value of repurposing is low.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada (currently missing, which blocks safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Controlled clinical evidence for chlorpheniramine specifically in allergic urticaria or cold urticaria, ideally compared with second-generation antihistamines
- A defined repurposing question, since the indication largely overlaps with approved uses
- Review of the sedation and anticholinergic burden, and of the listed contraindicated conditions, for the intended population
