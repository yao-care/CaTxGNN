## Chlorpheniramine: Histamine H1 receptor antagonist → nasal cavity disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Chlorpheniramine is an antihistamine (histamine H1 receptor blocker) that is marketed in Canada, mostly in cold, sinus and allergy products.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **nasal cavity disease**, but the supporting evidence is limited to **8 publications**, all preclinical or mechanistic, and **no registered clinical trials**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Mechanism of Action section of the US FDA-approved prescribing information for codeine phosphate and chlorpheniramine maleate (a combination product containing chlorpheniramine): "Chlorpheniramine Chlorpheniramine is a propylamine derivative antihistamine (H 1 -receptor antagonist) of the alkylamine class that also possesses anticholinergic and sedative activity." ([DailyMed: Tuxarin (codeine phosphate and chlorpheniramine maleate) prescribing information, Mainpointe Pharmaceuticals](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=a7cfab67-6087-4ab4-b7cd-4f9295413180), label version 2024-01-10; accessed 2026-11-19.) Chlorpheniramine is a first-generation H1 antihistamine. Blocking H1 receptors reduces histamine-driven responses in the nasal mucosa, such as runny nose, sneezing and increased vascular permeability. This is the plausible link to nasal conditions.

However, "nasal cavity disease" is a very broad term and does not define a specific repurposing question. Much of what it covers (allergic and vasomotor rhinitis) overlaps with uses chlorpheniramine already has, so it adds little as a new indication.

The retrieved studies are animal models (cat, dog, rat) of nasal congestion and allergic rhinitis. Most of them test H1/H3 antagonists in general rather than chlorpheniramine alone. One cat study suggests that relieving nasal congestion may need H3 co-blockade together with H1 blockade, so H1 antagonism alone is unlikely to address congestion. None of the studies directly examines "nasal cavity disease" in patients, so the evidence level is L5.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found in total. Two are rated Major: **potassium chloride** and **potassium citrate**. Most others are rated Moderate, including anticholinergic drugs (atropine, dicyclomine, glycopyrronium, methscopolamine, mepenzolate), opioids and related drugs (morphine, opium, loperamide, eluxadoline), cannabinoids (dronabinol, nabilone), and bupropion, eliglustat, lorcaserin and metoclopramide.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high (0.9953), but the supporting studies are animal and mechanistic work, with no clinical trials. The predicted term is too broad to define a testable question, and it largely overlaps with existing allergic and vasomotor rhinitis use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A narrower, clinically defined target condition in place of "nasal cavity disease"
- Human clinical data on chlorpheniramine itself for that condition
- Mechanism-of-action data for chlorpheniramine
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada
- Review of the Major interactions (potassium salts) and the anticholinergic and CNS-depressant interactions
