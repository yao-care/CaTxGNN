## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Chlorpheniramine is a first-generation H1 antihistamine. Blocking H1 receptors reduces histamine-driven responses in the nasal mucosa, such as runny nose, sneezing and increased vascular permeability. This is the plausible link to nasal conditions.

However, "nasal cavity disease" is a very broad term and does not define a specific repurposing question. Much of what it covers (allergic and vasomotor rhinitis) overlaps with uses chlorpheniramine already has, so it adds little as a new indication.

The retrieved studies are animal models (cat, dog, rat) of nasal congestion and allergic rhinitis. Most of them test H1/H3 antagonists in general rather than chlorpheniramine alone. One cat study suggests that relieving nasal congestion may need H3 co-blockade together with H1 blockade, so H1 antagonism alone is unlikely to address congestion. None of the studies directly examines "nasal cavity disease" in patients, so the evidence level is L5.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high (0.9953), but the supporting studies are animal and mechanistic work, with no clinical trials. The predicted term is too broad to define a testable question, and it largely overlaps with existing allergic and vasomotor rhinitis use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A narrower, clinically defined target condition in place of "nasal cavity disease"
- Human clinical data on chlorpheniramine itself for that condition
- Mechanism-of-action data for chlorpheniramine
