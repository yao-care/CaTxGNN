# Duloxetine: From Depression and Other Approved Uses to Benign Paroxysmal Torticollis of Infancy

## One-Sentence Summary

Duloxetine is a serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (SNRI) approved for conditions such as major depressive disorder and generalized anxiety disorder. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy**, a self-limiting pediatric disorder. **No clinical trials and no publications** currently support this prediction, so it rests on the model score alone.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source record. Duloxetine is known to be an SNRI, and its efficacy in mood and anxiety disorders is established. Any link to benign paroxysmal torticollis of infancy is speculative.

This condition is a migraine-related paroxysmal disorder of infancy, associated with CACNA1A, that usually resolves on its own. The only conceivable connection is through migraine-spectrum pathways affected by serotonin and norepinephrine reuptake inhibition. There is no clear mechanistic link.

The very high graph score is most likely a knowledge-graph artifact rather than a true pharmacological signal. Safety data for duloxetine in infants are also lacking, which further weakens the case.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction has no supporting trials or publications (L5), no plausible mechanism, and no infant safety data. The condition is also self-limiting, so the risk-benefit balance for a psychotropic drug is unfavorable.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and a credible mechanistic rationale linking duloxetine to this condition
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications
- Any preclinical or clinical signal in migraine-related paroxysmal disorders of infancy, plus pediatric safety data

**Note on other predictions for this drug:**
Other predicted indications in the same Evidence Pack have far stronger support. **Obsessive-compulsive disorder** (rank 3, L2) is backed by a double-blind augmentation RCT in resistant OCD, open-label studies, and a completed Phase 4 trial (NCT00464698, n=20). **Agoraphobia** (rank 2, L4) has only indirect evidence from panic disorder and generalized anxiety disorder. Prioritizing OCD for further evaluation would be more productive than pursuing this prediction.
