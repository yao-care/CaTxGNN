## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not currently available in the Evidence Pack. Based on known information, cetirizine is a selective peripheral histamine H1-receptor antagonist. Its efficacy in allergic conditions such as allergic rhinitis is well established, and mechanistically it is applicable to allergic urticaria.

Urticaria is driven by histamine released from mast cells. Blocking H1 receptors directly targets the wheal, flare and itch. Experimental work shows that a single 10 mg dose of cetirizine inhibits histamine-induced wheal and flare (PMID 2959719).

The Evidence Pack shows no original indications for cetirizine, and cetirizine is widely marketed for urticaria. The prediction may therefore reflect an existing use rather than a new one. Check the Health Canada label before calling this repurposing.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Allergic urticaria has direct but limited support: one completed Phase 3 pilot (n=36) of IV cetirizine and a strong histamine-mediated rationale. There are no confirmatory trials, and the labelled indication is unknown, so this is not yet clearly a repurposing case.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm the labelled indication against the Health Canada product monograph, to establish whether this is true repurposing.
- Obtain package-insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada, which is a blocking gap.
- Obtain mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.
- Complete the pending relevance grading of the literature, and check whether cetirizine is an arm in NCT01940393.

**Other predictions (lower priority):**
- **Cold urticaria** (score 99.95%, L3): Research Question. Small studies support it, including a 7-patient crossover trial and an ice-cube challenge study in which cetirizine reduced wheals by about 50%.

- **Acute laryngopharyngitis, rosacea conjunctivitis, recalcitrant atopic dermatitis** (L5): Hold. Model prediction only, with no trials or literature found.
