## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for zinc oxide in this Evidence Pack. Based on general pharmacological knowledge, zinc oxide is part of the dermatological topical agent class, historically used for its skin-protectant, astringent, and mild antimicrobial properties across various minor skin conditions.

The mechanistic rationale linking zinc oxide to acne is well-supported by existing literature: zinc oxide exhibits anti-inflammatory activity (suppressing IL-6/TNF-α), antibacterial effects against *Cutibacterium acnes*, and sebum-regulating properties — all core pathophysiological targets in acne vulgaris. Zinc-based compounds already have an established practical history in dermatology for inflammatory skin conditions, meaning this is not a novel or speculative mechanism but rather a mechanistically well-grounded application supported by multiple review articles.

However, the current evidence base consists primarily of reviews and preclinical/formulation studies rather than controlled clinical trials specific to zinc oxide (as opposed to other zinc salts such as zinc sulfate/gluconate), which is why this prediction sits at Evidence Level L3 rather than a higher tier.
