# Zinc Oxide: From Topical Skin Protection to Acne

The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **acne** (score 99.86%), but there are currently **0 registered clinical trials** and **7 publications**, mostly reviews and preclinical work, behind this direction. ## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for zinc oxide is not currently available in the record. Based on the literature retrieved, zinc is plausibly relevant to acne in three ways:

- **Anti-inflammatory activity**
- **Modulation of sebum**
- **Antibacterial effects** against *Cutibacterium acnes*, from zinc ions or ZnO particles

Acne is a chronic inflammatory disease of the pilosebaceous unit in which *C. acnes* plays a role. Zinc oxide is already used on skin, so a topical acne application is a natural extension. Several preclinical studies describe ZnO-based nanomaterials and microneedles with antibacterial activity against acne-related bacteria.

There are important caveats:

- The clinical literature on zinc in acne mostly concerns zinc salts, given orally or topically, not zinc oxide specifically.
- The TxGNN score is a model prediction, not clinical evidence.
- Topical zinc oxide's long marketed use supports it as a low-risk research question.

The other four predictions are weaker and are rated **L5 (prediction only) with a Hold recommendation**:

- **Anorectal stricture:** a weak symptomatic link only, since zinc oxide is a perianal barrier ingredient.
- **Anal polyp:** no clear mechanism.
- **Papillary conjunctivitis:** no direct link, and ocular use would raise separate safety questions.
- **Postinfectious vasculitis:** no plausible mechanism.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The acne prediction has biological plausibility and the product has a long topical safety record. However, there is no registered trial of zinc oxide for acne, the supporting literature mostly concerns zinc salts or preclinical nanomaterials, and Health Canada safety labelling has not been reviewed. The other four predicted indications have no supporting evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada labelling (warnings and contraindications), which is currently a blocking gap
- Mechanism of action data for zinc oxide (e.g., from DrugBank)
- Clinical studies of zinc oxide itself, not other zinc salts, in acne, in order to define a testable hypothesis (formulation, concentration, comparator)
- Review of the one split-face acne study (PMID 15536660) to confirm what was actually tested
