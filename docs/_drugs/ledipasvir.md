# Ledipasvir: From Hepatitis C Virus Infection to Hepatitis B Virus Infection

## One-Sentence Summary

Ledipasvir is an HCV NS5A inhibitor, marketed in Canada as part of the Harvoni combination (with sofosbuvir) and originally used to treat chronic hepatitis C.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hepatitis B virus infection**, but the mechanistic rationale is weak.
Support consists of **1 directly relevant Phase 2 trial** (n=21, no control arm) and **several co-infection studies** that mainly address safety (HBV reactivation) rather than HBV efficacy.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, ledipasvir is an NS5A inhibitor used together with sofosbuvir, and its efficacy in hepatitis C is established.

HBV has no known counterpart to HCV NS5A, so an anti-HBV effect is not explained by ledipasvir's original mechanism. The most plausible source of signal is observational: in patients co-infected with HCV and HBV, treatment with ledipasvir/sofosbuvir was associated with a modest reduction in HBV surface antigen (HBsAg). That observation prompted a Phase 2 pilot in HBV mono-infected patients (NCT03312023, published 2023). The data compiled for this report do not show a robust HBV antiviral effect from that study.

The high TxGNN score is therefore best read as a hypothesis-generating signal, not as a mechanistically supported finding. The main clinical concern in HBV-exposed patients is the documented risk of **HBV reactivation** after HCV clearance with direct-acting antivirals.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 65 interactions were identified in total. Those listed here are mostly Moderate, with a few Minor.
  - Acid-reducing agents (reduce ledipasvir absorption): aluminum hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magaldrate, magnesium carbonate, magnesium hydroxide, magnesium oxide; H2 blockers cimetidine, famotidine, nizatidine; proton pump inhibitors omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole, pantoprazole (all Moderate)
  - Other Moderate interactions: eliglustat, linagliptin, loperamide, naldemedine
  - Minor interactions: fidaxomicin, naloxegol
- **HBV reactivation**: Multiple cohort studies and case reports describe HBV reactivation in HBV-exposed patients after HCV clearance with direct-acting antivirals. This is the central safety issue for any use in HBV-infected populations.

For other warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The only directly relevant evidence is a small, uncontrolled Phase 2 pilot (n=21) in HBV mono-infection. No robust antiviral effect is shown in the data compiled, and there is no mechanistic basis for NS5A inhibition acting on HBV. Co-infection data mainly document HBV reactivation risk, not benefit.
- Other predicted indications (hepatitis E, hepatitis A, HIV, Omsk hemorrhagic fever, Kyasanur forest disease and animal-model indications) have weaker or no supporting evidence, and none offers a stronger repurposing case than HBV.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full results of the HBsAg and HBV DNA endpoints from NCT03312023 (PMID 36045503), to judge the size and durability of any effect
- Mechanistic data (MOA) and an explanation of how an NS5A inhibitor could affect HBV, or evidence that any effect comes from sofosbuvir
- A controlled study, ideally randomized, in HBV mono-infection
- A monitoring plan for HBV reactivation
- The Health Canada package insert, to confirm warnings, contraindications and approved indication text
