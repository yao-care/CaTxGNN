# Nepafenac: From Ocular Inflammation After Cataract Surgery to Eye Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Nepafenac is a topical ophthalmic NSAID. The published literature describes it as approved in the US and Europe for pain and inflammation after cataract surgery. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **eye disease**, with **41 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction. However, "eye disease" is a very broad label, and most of this evidence matches the existing cataract-surgery use, so this may not be true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Nepafenac is a prodrug. Corneal and ocular tissue hydrolases convert it to amfenac, a COX-1/COX-2 inhibitor. This reduces prostaglandin-mediated inflammation, pain and breakdown of the blood-retinal barrier. Published work also shows that topical nepafenac and amfenac reach the posterior segment of the eye (PMID 26474497). This explains why trials extend beyond the front of the eye to macular edema and retinal conditions.

The prediction fits the drug's pharmacology, since inflammatory eye conditions share the same prostaglandin pathway. The trials cover post-cataract inflammation and pain, macular edema (including in diabetic patients), pain after photorefractive keratectomy (PRK, a laser corneal surgery), and inflammation after laser iridotomy.

There are two caveats:
- "Eye disease" is a broad category, and the supporting trials mostly address post-cataract-surgery inflammation, pain and macular edema. This is probably already on-label, so the prediction may not be true repurposing.
- The original indication field is empty in the source data. This is most likely a data gap rather than evidence that the drug has no established use.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 trials, mostly randomized and controlled, plus a 2025 systematic review support nepafenac in ocular inflammation, pain and macular edema around cataract surgery. This supports L1 evidence, but it largely confirms the existing use rather than a new one. "Eye disease" is also too broad to act on as a repurposing target.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Narrow the target to a specific condition not already on-label (for example, diabetic macular edema or post-laser inflammation), rather than the broad "eye disease" label
- Confirm the approved indications on the Health Canada product monographs for NEVANAC and ILEVRO, including dosage forms and indication text
- Obtain the Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Obtain detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank

Other lower-ranked predictions have weak or no support and should stay on hold:
- Optic papillitis has only a rabbit retinal-edema study, and no evidence shows the drug reaches the optic nerve head.
- Vitreous detachment has one Phase 4 biomarker study in a related condition.
- The remaining seven (hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp, seborrheic keratosis, von Hippel anomaly, congenital hypotrichosis milia, McPherson-Robertson-Cammarano syndrome, lagophthalmos and vulvar inverted follicular keratosis) have no trials or literature.
