## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general triptan pharmacology, almotriptan is a selective serotonin 5-HT1B/1D receptor agonist. The literature describes this action as constricting intracranial blood vessels and inhibiting trigeminovascular signalling during a migraine attack.

Migraine with brainstem aura is a migraine subtype with the same underlying trigeminovascular pathophysiology as the migraine already treated by almotriptan. This shared pathway makes the model's prediction biologically plausible.

There is an important caveat. Triptans have traditionally been used with caution, or avoided, in brainstem aura (formerly basilar-type migraine) and in hemiplegic migraine because of a theoretical risk of vasoconstriction. The knowledge graph also lists familial or sporadic hemiplegic migraine among almotriptan's contraindicated conditions. The mechanistic fit is therefore plausible, but the safety of this use is unresolved.

The other two predictions (atrophoderma vermiculata and ulerythema ophryogenesis) have no identified mechanistic link to serotonin receptor agonism and appear to be knowledge-graph artifacts. They are not evaluated further.

## Safety Considerations

Label-based warnings and contraindications are not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

- **Conditions flagged as contraindicated in the knowledge graph** (not label text), grouped by theme:
  - Cardiovascular and cerebrovascular: hypertension, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, myocardial ischemia, Prinzmetal angina, stroke, transient ischemic attack, cerebral infarction, brain ischemia, vertebrobasilar insufficiency, peripheral arterial disease, Raynaud disease, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease
  - Metabolic and other: liver disease, gallbladder disease, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, obesity, hemoglobinopathy
  - Neurological: serotonin syndrome, familial or sporadic hemiplegic migraine
- **Drug interactions:** 56 interactions were identified.
  - **Major:** dexfenfluramine, fenfluramine, lorcaserin, sibutramine (serotonergic agents), and the 5-HT3 antagonists dolasetron, granisetron, ondansetron and palonosetron.
  - **Moderate:** aprepitant, clarithromycin, morphine (including liposomal morphine).
  - Several others, such as pantoprazole, metformin, omeprazole and simvastatin, are listed with an unknown severity level.

The vertebrobasilar insufficiency, hemiplegic migraine and cerebrovascular items are particularly relevant to brainstem aura.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic link is plausible and almotriptan is well established in general migraine. However, there are no clinical trials in brainstem aura, and triptans are traditionally cautioned against in this subtype, a concern echoed by the contraindication data. The safety question must be resolved before this prediction can progress beyond a research question.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, to confirm how brainstem aura is addressed
- Mechanism of action data for almotriptan (e.g., from DrugBank)
- A focused review of the safety of triptans in brainstem aura and hemiplegic migraine, including any vasoconstriction-related events
- Clinical or observational data specific to brainstem aura, if any exist
- A risk-mitigation plan, including patient selection, cardiovascular and cerebrovascular screening, and serotonergic drug interaction checks
