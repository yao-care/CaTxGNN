# AMG-510 (Sotorasib): From KRAS G12C-Mutated Cancer to Transitional Cell Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

AMG-510 (sotorasib, marketed in Canada as LUMAKRAS) is a covalent KRAS G12C inhibitor. The Evidence Pack does not record its approved indication, but it is generally known as a treatment for KRAS G12C-mutated cancers.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **transitional cell carcinoma** (urothelial carcinoma), but currently **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** support this direction, so it remains a model-only prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not in the Evidence Pack. Based on known information, sotorasib is a covalent inhibitor of the KRAS G12C mutant protein. Its activity depends on the tumour carrying that specific mutation.

Urothelial carcinoma is a cancer type where this link is plausible only for a small subset of tumours. KRAS mutations are uncommon in urothelial cancers, and G12C is a minor fraction of them. The high TxGNN score is a graph-based prediction and is not independent evidence. It likely reflects proximity to other oncology and urothelial nodes in the knowledge graph.

A biomarker-selected hypothesis (KRAS G12C-positive urothelial tumours only) could be explored before any clinical recommendation.

**Other top-ranked predictions are weaker:**
- Sarcomatoid and prostatic urethra urothelial variants (ranks 2–4) apply only to KRAS G12C-mutant cases, which are rare.
- Papillary urothelial carcinoma (rank 5) is typically driven by FGFR3 and other alterations, so the rationale is weak.
- Middle ear neuroendocrine tumour, malignant cutaneous granular cell tumour and ectomesenchymoma (ranks 6–8) have no known KRAS G12C role.
- HHV-8-related tumour (rank 10) is driven by viral oncogenesis, with no plausible link.
- Esotropia (rank 9) is a non-oncologic eye movement disorder and is most likely a false positive.
