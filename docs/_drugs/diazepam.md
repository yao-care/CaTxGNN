## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for diazepam is not available in the data compiled for this report. From the retrieved literature, diazepam is a positive allosteric modulator of GABA-A receptors. This enhances inhibitory signalling and produces sedative, anxiolytic, anticonvulsant and muscle-relaxant effects.

Sedative-hypnotic activity makes a role in insomnia biologically plausible. Benzodiazepines as a class are already used for short-term insomnia. The retrieved literature, however, is mostly about the class in general (pharmacology reviews, GABA-A modulator reviews, herbal insomnia studies) and not about diazepam specifically. The very high TxGNN score is a model prediction, not clinical evidence. Long-term risks limit enthusiasm: dependence, disruption of sleep architecture, and a reported association with breast cancer progression.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Diazepam has a plausible mechanism for insomnia and the benzodiazepine class is already used short-term for it. However, there are no registered trials, and diazepam-specific direct evidence is limited to one small 1981 comparison where it was inferior to lormetazepam. This is best treated as a research question and not a ready-to-use new indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Diazepam-specific randomized trials in insomnia versus current standard treatments
- Package insert warnings and contraindications from Health Canada
- Mechanism of action data
- A plan limiting use to short courses, with dependence, respiratory and interaction monitoring (especially with opioids)
