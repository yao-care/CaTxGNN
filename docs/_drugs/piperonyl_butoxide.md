## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in this evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on known pharmacology, piperonyl butoxide is a cytochrome P450 (mixed-function oxidase) inhibitor that blocks metabolic detoxification of pyrethrin/pyrethroid insecticides in arthropods, thereby synergizing their acaricidal/insecticidal potency. It is not typically used as a standalone therapeutic agent but as a fixed-dose component of pyrethrin-based topical products.

Scabies (caused by the mite *Sarcoptes scabiei*) is treated with topical acaricides such as permethrin and pyrethrins. Because PBO's known mechanism directly enhances pyrethroid/pyrethrin efficacy against mites — including restoring activity against resistant strains — a mechanistic link to scabies treatment is biologically plausible and is not a purely novel hypothesis: pyrethrins/PBO combination formulations have already been studied and used clinically for scabies, including in permethrin-resistant cases.

This is therefore less a speculative "repurposing" signal and more a case where TxGNN's model has re-identified an existing, literature-supported use. The main open question is not biological plausibility but regulatory/commercial status: PBO-containing scabicide products are not currently licensed in Canada under this drug entry.
