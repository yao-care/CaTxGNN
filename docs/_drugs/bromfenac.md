## Bromfenac: From Postoperative Ocular Inflammation to Eye Disease (Dry Eye and VEGF-Driven Maculopathies)
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Bromfenac is a topical non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) marketed in Canada as an ophthalmic solution, and it is used in the eye for postoperative inflammation and pain.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **eye disease** (a broad category), with **39 clinical trials** and **20 publications** linked to this prediction. The most promising specific directions are dry eye disease and macular conditions.
The broad "eye disease" label largely overlaps with its established use, so the real repurposing value lies in these sub-indications.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, bromfenac is an NSAID that inhibits cyclooxygenase (COX-1/COX-2) and reduces prostaglandin E2 synthesis. This mechanism is directly relevant to ocular inflammation, post-cataract pain, cystoid macular oedema, and ocular surface inflammation.

Because bromfenac is already used as an ophthalmic solution for postoperative inflammation, "eye disease" is mostly a broad umbrella over established use. The repurposing value lies in more specific sub-indications:

- **Dry eye disease**: supported by a completed Phase 3 dose-ranging trial and a clinical study in patients not controlled by artificial tears.
- **VEGF-driven maculopathies** (neovascular AMD, diabetic macular oedema, retinal vein occlusion): a 2024 review and meta-analysis supports topical bromfenac as an add-on to anti-VEGF injections, and small studies exist for AMD and diabetic macular oedema.

The other predictions are much weaker. Optic papillitis has only an indirect link (topical bromfenac has limited posterior segment penetration). The remaining predictions have no supporting trials or are not mechanistically plausible (for example, von Hippel anomaly, seborrheic keratosis, luxation of globe, lagophthalmos). The literature counts for some of them reflect retrieval artifacts rather than real evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (from drug-disease relationships in the TxGNN knowledge graph, not from label text): rheumatoid arthritis, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis. These should be checked against the official product monograph.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Those listed here are mostly Moderate, with a few Minor. Examples include:
  - Corticosteroids: hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide
  - Antidiabetic agents: metformin, glimepiride, chlorpropamide
  - Aspirin and mesalazine-type agents: acetylsalicylic acid, mesalazine, balsalazide
  - Aminoglycosides and glycopeptides: kanamycin, vancomycin
  - Bowel preparations and potassium salts
  - Minor: cimetidine, linaclotide
- **Safety signals in the literature**: case reports describe corneal melting or perforation (including in Stevens-Johnson syndrome and undiagnosed Sjögren's syndrome) and toxic epidermal necrolysis after bromfenac eye drops. Systemic bromfenac was withdrawn from the market because of severe liver injury; this concerns the oral form and is not shown for topical use, but it is relevant to risk awareness.

Please refer to the package insert for the official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Phase 3 trials are completed in dry eye disease and in postoperative ocular inflammation, and a recent meta-analysis supports use in maculopathies. However, "eye disease" is a very broad prediction that largely overlaps with the existing approved use, and the other nine predictions (for example optic papillitis and the rare or unrelated conditions) have little or no support and should stay on Hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Approved indication text and dosage forms for the two Canadian licences
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A narrower target indication (for example dry eye disease or add-on therapy in diabetic macular oedema) with confirmed drug attribution for the ambiguous trials
- A monitoring plan for corneal adverse events in patients with ocular surface disease or dry eye, and a review of the interactions listed above
