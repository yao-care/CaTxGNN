# Vortioxetine: From Major Depressive Disorder to Neurotic Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Vortioxetine is an internationally marketed antidepressant that is not currently authorized for sale in Canada. The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction for this drug is efficacy in **Neurotic Disorder**, but this specific candidate is currently supported by only **1 clinical trial** and **1 publication**, both indirect. Substantially stronger evidence exists for two closely related, historically synonymous terms (neurotic depression, melancholia) ranked immediately below, which are effectively vortioxetine's own pivotal Major Depressive Disorder (MDD) registration program.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed, structured mechanism-of-action data is not available for this drug in the current evidence pack. Based on information present elsewhere in the pack (e.g., PMID 25016186, PMID 37032427), vortioxetine is known as a multimodal serotonergic antidepressant — it inhibits the serotonin transporter (SERT) while acting as an antagonist at 5-HT3, 5-HT7, and 5-HT1D receptors, a partial agonist at 5-HT1B, and an agonist at 5-HT1A, which in turn increases serotonergic, noradrenergic, dopaminergic, cholinergic, histaminergic, and glutamatergic neurotransmission relevant to mood regulation. Its efficacy in MDD has been established through an extensive Phase 2/3 registration program (see ranks 2–3 evidence below).

"Neurotic disorder" is an older, non-specific diagnostic classification that clinically overlaps heavily with the depression/anxiety spectrum and has no independent diagnostic entity in modern nosology (ICD-11/DSM-5). As a result, the evidence supporting this specific TxGNN candidate is inherently indirect — drawn from broad, non-disease-specific antidepressant comparison studies rather than trials designed around this diagnostic label.

Mechanistically, vortioxetine's serotonergic modulation is plausible for neurotic-spectrum presentations given their symptomatic overlap with depression, but because "neurotic disorder" itself lacks a distinct diagnostic framework, this candidate should be interpreted with caution. Notably, two adjacent predictions in this evidence pack — **neurotic depression** (rank 2, score 99.09%) and **melancholia** (rank 3, score 99.09%) — are essentially historical synonyms for MDD and are backed by six completed Phase 3 registration trials (N up to 1,075) plus multiple systematic reviews/meta-analyses (evidence level L1, "Proceed with Guardrails"). These are the stronger, better-defined signal underlying this drug's repurposing potential.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked TxGNN prediction, "Neurotic Disorder," is supported only by one indirect real-world cohort study and one non-specific case-based review (evidence level L3), and the diagnostic label itself lacks an independent clinical definition in current nosology. Combined with a blocking gap in TFDA/Health Canada safety labeling data (DG001) and an unresolved mechanism-of-action gap (DG002), this specific candidate does not yet meet the bar to proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/Health Canada approved label — warnings, contraindications, and full prescribing information (resolves DG001, currently blocking)
- Confirmed, sourced mechanism-of-action documentation from DrugBank (resolves DG002)
- Clarification of whether "neurotic disorder" maps to a modern billable diagnosis, or re-scoping of this evaluation toward the better-evidenced synonymous candidates in this same evidence pack — **neurotic depression** and **melancholia** (both L1, six completed Phase 3 RCTs, "Proceed with Guardrails")
- A Canadian regulatory pathway assessment, since vortioxetine currently holds zero DINs and is not marketed in this jurisdiction
