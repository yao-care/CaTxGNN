# Irinotecan: From Original Indication Not Documented to Female Breast Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Irinotecan (DB00762) is a cytotoxic topoisomerase I inhibitor prodrug; its original approved indication is not documented in the available Canadian regulatory data, and the drug is currently **not marketed** in Canada. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Female Breast Carcinoma**, with **22 clinical trials** and **20 publications** identified — though the majority of this evidence concerns **sacituzumab govitecan**, an antibody‑drug conjugate that delivers irinotecan's active metabolite (SN‑38), rather than free irinotecan itself.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed original mechanism-of-action data for irinotecan is not available in this evidence pack. Based on the mechanistic analysis included with the prediction, irinotecan is a prodrug that is metabolized by carboxylesterase to its active metabolite, SN‑38, a potent topoisomerase I (TOP1) inhibitor. TOP1 inhibition causes single-strand DNA breaks and replication fork collapse — a broadly cytotoxic mechanism with theoretical activity against highly proliferative tumors, including breast cancer subtypes such as triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC).

This mechanism has been clinically validated in breast cancer, but largely through **sacituzumab govitecan**, an anti-TROP-2 antibody-drug conjugate that delivers SN‑38 directly to tumor cells (validated in the ASCENT and TROPiCS-02 trials). This is an important distinction: sacituzumab govitecan is a separate drug entity from free irinotecan, and its clinical success in breast cancer cannot be directly extrapolated as efficacy evidence for irinotecan itself.

That said, a smaller body of evidence directly tests free irinotecan (or its liposomal formulation, nal-IRI) in breast cancer — including a completed Phase 2 monotherapy trial (NCT00072852) and a 2026 Phase 2 study of liposomal irinotecan in HER2-negative breast cancer with brain metastases (PHENOMENAL). This direct-drug evidence is more modest in volume than the ADC literature but supports biological plausibility.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
A Blocking data gap exists for TFDA/product safety labeling, which prevents completion of the S1 safety pre-screen, and the bulk of supporting evidence pertains to sacituzumab govitecan (a distinct antibody-drug conjugate entity) rather than free irinotecan. Combined with irinotecan's current unmarketed status in Canada, the evidence base does not yet support advancing beyond a research question.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/Health Canada product labeling — warnings, precautions, and contraindications (DG001, Blocking)
- Confirmed original indication and mechanism-of-action data for irinotecan (DG002)
- Direct free-irinotecan (non-ADC) efficacy and safety data specific to breast carcinoma — e.g., mature results from PHENOMENAL and NCT03562390
- Clarification of the regulatory pathway for a currently unmarketed product before further development
