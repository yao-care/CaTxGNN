## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the supplied record, and no original indications are listed. The reasoning below comes from general pharmacology, not from the supplied data.

Sulfamethoxazole inhibits bacterial dihydropteroate synthase and blocks folate synthesis. This is plausible against the bacteria that cause acute bacterial conjunctivitis, and topical sulfonamides have a historical role in eye infections. The prediction therefore looks like a drug-class and pathogen-susceptibility association, not a new mechanism.

There are two main limits. First, viral and allergic causes of "contagious" conjunctivitis would not respond to an antibacterial. Second, sulfamethoxazole is normally used systemically with trimethoprim, and no ocular formulation or ocular-penetration data are provided.
