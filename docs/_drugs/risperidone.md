## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the structured evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap, DG002). Based on information embedded in the trial evidence itself, risperidone is described as "a new-generation atypical antipsychotic agent with potent dopaminergic (D2) and serotonergic (5-HT2A) antagonist activity" (PMID 7545159), originally approved for schizophrenia and bipolar mania (as stated directly in trial NCT00277654's brief summary).

Bipolar mania and major affective disorder sit on the same clinical spectrum — mania, bipolar depression, and unipolar treatment-resistant depression (TRD) are all mood-regulation disorders. Risperidone's dual D2/5-HT2A blockade, already validated for controlling manic symptoms, has been extended clinically as (1) a maintenance therapy to prevent mood-episode recurrence in bipolar I disorder (e.g., long-acting injectable formulations), and (2) an augmentation agent added to SSRIs/antidepressants when patients fail to respond to first-line treatment for depression.

This is not merely a theoretical extrapolation — it is already an established, evidence-dense off-label/adjunctive practice pattern. Multiple completed Phase 3 RCTs (pediatric and adult bipolar populations, TRD augmentation trials) and several systematic reviews/network meta-analyses on second-generation antipsychotic augmentation in TRD directly support the mechanistic plausibility and clinical reasonableness of this TxGNN prediction.
