# Ripretinib: From Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumour (GIST) to Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on published literature, ripretinib is a switch-control tyrosine kinase inhibitor that blocks both primary and secondary activating mutations of KIT and PDGFRA. These mutations drive advanced gastrointestinal stromal tumours (GIST). Its clinical activity in GIST is supported by the INVICTUS and INTRIGUE trial analyses.

The link to multiple endocrine neoplasia (MEN) is weak. MEN syndromes are driven mainly by loss-of-function of the MEN1 gene (MEN1) or activating RET mutations (MEN2). No documented mechanistic connection was found between ripretinib's targets and these drivers. Ripretinib may inhibit some related kinase signalling to a limited degree, but this is speculative. The prediction should be treated as a hypothesis generated by the model, not a supported repurposing opportunity.

Other top-ranked predictions are also unsupported: HER2-positive and other breast cancer subtypes, cytomegalovirus infection and amenorrhea have no trials and no relevant literature. Two veterinary herpesvirus diseases (infectious bovine rhinotracheitis and malignant catarrhal fever) are likely knowledge-graph artefacts.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screening found 90 potential interactions. Two are Major among those listed: **clarithromycin** and **cobicistat**. Most others are Moderate, including:
  - Antidiabetic agents: pioglitazone, rosiglitazone, troglitazone, canagliflozin, repaglinide
  - Antiemetics: aprepitant, rolapitant
  - Azole antifungals: miconazole, clotrimazole
  - Others: dexamethasone, cimetidine, eliglustat, fidaxomicin, deferasirox, fostamatinib, selexipag, metreleptin, glycerol phenylbutyrate
- Published pharmacokinetic work suggests that CYP3A4 and P-glycoprotein inhibitors can change ripretinib exposure.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score alone (L5). There are no clinical trials or publications for multiple endocrine neoplasia, and no plausible mechanistic link between ripretinib's KIT/PDGFRA targets and the MEN1 or RET drivers of this condition.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (DrugBank) to allow a proper mechanistic analysis
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, which are needed before any safety screening
- Original indication and dosage form details from the Canadian licence record
- Preclinical evidence (for example, activity in MEN-relevant cell or animal models) before any clinical consideration
- Review of the other top-ranked predictions. All are also model-only, and the veterinary disease predictions are likely artefacts.
