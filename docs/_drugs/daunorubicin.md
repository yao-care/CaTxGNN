# Daunorubicin: From Acute Leukaemia to Hodgkin's Lymphoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Daunorubicin is an anthracycline chemotherapy drug, originally used to treat acute lymphoblastic and acute myeloid leukaemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Hodgkin's lymphoma**,
but the support is limited: **2 related clinical trials** (both multi-drug regimens in non-Hodgkin lymphoma) and **20 publications**, most of them indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, daunorubicin is an anthracycline that intercalates into DNA and inhibits topoisomerase II. This is a plausible mechanism against lymphoid malignancies, and its efficacy in acute leukaemia is well established.

Leukaemia and lymphoma are both haematological cancers, and anthracyclines are widely used across them. Two caveats weaken the prediction, however. First, the supporting trials are multi-drug regimens in non-Hodgkin lymphoma, so daunorubicin's own contribution cannot be isolated. Second, the anthracycline in the standard Hodgkin regimen (ABVD) is doxorubicin, not daunorubicin.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level contraindication signals (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: neutropenia and other severe neutropenic conditions, cardiomyopathy, heart conduction disease, ventricular tachycardia, sudden cardiac arrest, liver disease, kidney disease, gallbladder disease, and infectious disease, including fungal infections.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Examples:
  - **Major**: cisapride, dolasetron, papaverine, deferiprone, macimorelin.
  - **Moderate**: clarithromycin, levofloxacin, ondansetron, granisetron, palonosetron, famotidine, loperamide, and others.
  - **Minor**: metronidazole.
- Anthracycline cardiotoxicity is a well-recognised class risk, discussed in several of the cited reviews.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but the supporting evidence is indirect. The two trials are multi-drug regimens in non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and the standard Hodgkin regimen uses doxorubicin rather than daunorubicin. Daunorubicin is already marketed in Canada, which lowers access barriers, but this indication is best treated as a research question rather than an action candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data to support the mechanistic link
- Comparative evidence of daunorubicin versus doxorubicin in Hodgkin lymphoma regimens
- A cardiac and haematological safety monitoring plan, given the cumulative anthracycline exposure typical of Hodgkin regimens
