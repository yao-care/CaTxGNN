# Eltrombopag: From Autoimmune Thrombocytopenia to HIV Infectious Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Eltrombopag is a thrombopoietin receptor agonist, originally used to treat autoimmune thrombocytopenia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **HIV infectious disease**. The supporting evidence is limited: **1 clinical trial** (whose relevance to HIV is unconfirmed) and **8 publications**, mostly case reports, case series and reviews. Any benefit is likely to be supportive care for HIV-related low blood counts, not antiviral activity.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, eltrombopag is a thrombopoietin receptor agonist. Its efficacy in immune thrombocytopenia is established, and it may be applicable to HIV-related blood-count problems.

The most plausible link is symptomatic. Eltrombopag raises platelet counts in HIV-associated immune thrombocytopenia, including thrombocytopenia after immune reconstitution. It may also support blood-cell production in HIV-associated marrow failure such as aplastic anaemia. This is supportive-care benefit for HIV-related cytopenias, **not** antiviral activity.

The very high TxGNN score (0.99) probably reflects the shared thrombocytopenia biology, not an effect on HIV itself. One 2020 screen of FDA-approved compounds (PMID 32977702) reported modulation of HIV-1 proviral transcription. It is preclinical and only hypothesis-generating.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-related cautions (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: The TxGNN knowledge graph links eltrombopag to cautions in these conditions: kidney disease, liver disease (including acute liver failure and hepatitis), gallbladder disease, hepatic vein thrombosis and Budd-Chiari syndrome, anaemia, leukopenia, myelodysplastic syndrome, thrombocytosis, and hyperbilirubinaemia (including Gilbert syndrome). ## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence for HIV is limited to case reports, a case series, reviews and one preclinical screen. The single clinical trial is in chronic liver disease, was terminated early, and is not confirmed as HIV-specific. The high TxGNN score probably reflects shared thrombocytopenia biology, and any benefit is supportive care for HIV-related cytopenias, not antiviral activity.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data
- Confirmation of the population, endpoints and termination reason of NCT00678587
- Prospective or controlled studies of eltrombopag in HIV-associated thrombocytopenia or marrow failure
- Dosage form and approved indication details for the Canadian licences
- Review of safety in patients with liver disease and of interactions with commonly co-prescribed antiretrovirals and cation-containing products
