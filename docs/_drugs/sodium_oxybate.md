# Sodium Oxybate: From Narcolepsy with Cataplexy to Insomnia

## One-Sentence Summary

Sodium oxybate (the sodium salt of gamma-hydroxybutyrate, GHB) is a GABA-B receptor agonist established for treating narcolepsy with cataplexy by enhancing slow-wave sleep and nocturnal sleep consolidation. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Insomnia**, with **13 clinical trials** and **13 publications** currently supporting this direction, including a completed head-to-head Phase 2 RCT against zolpidem in patients with chronic insomnia.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Formal DrugBank mechanism-of-action data for sodium oxybate is not available in this evidence pack. Based on the clinical literature included in the evidence base, sodium oxybate is a GABA-B receptor agonist (and possibly acts at specific GHB receptors) that reliably increases slow-wave ("deep") sleep and improves sleep consolidation — effects repeatedly documented in narcolepsy studies (e.g., PMID 21476957, PMID 15586785).

Narcolepsy with cataplexy and insomnia are pharmacologically linked through the same sleep-architecture pathway: narcolepsy patients frequently experience fragmented nocturnal sleep alongside excessive daytime sleepiness, and sodium oxybate's core therapeutic action — consolidating and deepening nighttime sleep — maps directly onto the core deficit in insomnia (difficulty initiating or maintaining sleep). This mechanistic overlap is directly tested in a completed Phase 2 RCT (NCT00383643) comparing sodium oxybate to zolpidem in chronic insomnia, lending direct (not just inferential) support to the TxGNN prediction.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
While a completed Phase 2 RCT directly supports sodium oxybate's efficacy in chronic insomnia (L2 evidence), the drug is not currently marketed in Canada (0 DINs) and Health Canada warning/contraindication data is entirely unavailable — a **Blocking** gap that prevents any S1 safety pre-assessment. Sodium oxybate is also a controlled, abuse-liable substance (GHB), which the current evidence pack cannot yet characterize from a Canadian regulatory perspective.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada product monograph / warnings and contraindications (DG001, Blocking)
- Formal DrugBank/MOA data to confirm mechanistic rationale (DG002, High)
- Confirmation of controlled-substance scheduling and abuse-potential monitoring plan for insomnia use
- Assessment of the regulatory pathway required given the drug currently has no Canadian marketing authorization
