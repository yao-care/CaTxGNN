# Sodium Oxybate: From Narcolepsy to Insomnia

## One-Sentence Summary

Sodium oxybate (GHB) is marketed in Canada as Xyrem and Xywav. The published literature describes it as a treatment for narcolepsy, but the Health Canada indication text is not in the data provided. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **insomnia**, with **12 retrieved clinical trials** (only 1 directly on insomnia) and **13 publications** (mostly indirect). The evidence is thin, and abuse and respiratory risks weigh heavily against this use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Sodium oxybate acts on GABA-B and GHB receptors. It increases slow-wave sleep and sleep consolidation, which plausibly supports a role in sleep-maintenance insomnia. A narcolepsy drug that deepens and consolidates nocturnal sleep is mechanistically close to insomnia, where the problem is fragmented or insufficient sleep. Reviews of GABA-B agonists describe reduced sleep onset latency, increased sleep consolidation and increased slow-wave sleep.

The direct clinical support is limited to one small Phase 2 randomized trial against zolpidem in chronic insomnia (NCT00383643, n=48). The Phase 3 cluster headache trial (NCT02637648) has UNKNOWN status and no results, so it cannot support a higher evidence level.

The risks are substantial. Sodium oxybate carries abuse and dependence liability, respiratory depression and REMS requirements. Insomnia is highly prevalent, so these risks weigh heavily against use there. Other predicted indications (neurogenic bladder, cauda equina syndrome, Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome, restless legs syndrome) have no supporting evidence. Some of the retrieved records signal harm, including a GHB-abuse case of Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome and a case of restless legs syndrome as a possible side effect.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. No warning, contraindication or interaction data were available in the Evidence Pack.

The retrieved evidence and prediction rationale point to these concerns:
- **Abuse and dependence liability**, with REMS requirements.
- **Respiratory depression.**
- **GHB withdrawal syndrome** after discontinuing frequent use (PMID 11174231).
- **Possible restless legs syndrome** as a side effect (PMID 17275405).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only direct evidence is one small, completed Phase 2 trial in chronic insomnia (n=48). The Phase 3 trial has unknown status and no results. The abuse, dependence and respiratory risks are hard to justify in a high-prevalence condition like insomnia, and the safety review cannot proceed without package insert data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings and contraindications, so safety screening can begin.
- Results or publication of NCT00383643, with its efficacy and safety outcomes against zolpidem.
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- Approved indication text and dosage forms for DIN 02268272 and DIN 02538237.
- A risk-benefit and REMS feasibility assessment for a high-prevalence, non-narcolepsy population.
