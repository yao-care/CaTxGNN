# Sodium Oxybate: From Narcolepsy to Insomnia

## One-Sentence Summary

Sodium oxybate is a central nervous system depressant, originally used to treat narcolepsy (including narcolepsy with cataplexy).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **insomnia**, with **3 registered clinical trials** and **12 publications** currently linked to this direction. Only one of these trials directly tests insomnia, and it is a small Phase 2 study.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, sodium oxybate (gamma-hydroxybutyrate, GHB) acts as an agonist at GABA-B and GHB receptors. Its efficacy in narcolepsy has been established, and it may be mechanistically applicable to insomnia.

Sodium oxybate increases slow-wave sleep and consolidates sleep architecture. It is established in narcolepsy, where nighttime sleep is fragmented. Because insomnia involves difficulty initiating or maintaining sleep, this effect is plausibly relevant. A randomized Phase 2 trial has compared it directly with zolpidem in chronic insomnia.

There are important cautions. The drug carries CNS and respiratory depression risk, abuse and misuse potential, and many interactions with sedatives and alcohol. In a common, non-orphan condition such as insomnia, these risks are hard to justify without strong efficacy data.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (not label text): hypertension, epilepsy, anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder, liver disease, gallbladder disease, substance abuse/dependence, substance-related disorder, obstructive sleep apnea syndrome, sleep apnea syndrome, congenital central hypoventilation syndrome, respiratory failure, succinic semialdehyde dehydrogenase deficiency, and parasomnia.
- **Drug Interactions**: 48 interactions were identified. **Major** interactions include ethanol, opioids (morphine, codeine, hydrocodone, opium), cannabinoids (dronabinol, nabilone), sedating antihistamines (promethazine, chlorpheniramine, brompheniramine, carbinoxamine, acrivastine), bupropion, fenfluramine, metoclopramide, and sibutramine. **Moderate** interactions include diazoxide, amyl nitrite, and phentolamine.
- **Literature signals**: case reports describe sleep-related eating disorder, restless legs syndrome, and intolerance linked to sodium oxybate. Reviews also highlight abuse liability.

Please refer to the package insert for official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only direct evidence is one small Phase 2 trial (n=48) against zolpidem, with no results available, and the other literature is mostly indirect. Sodium oxybate's risks (CNS and respiratory depression, abuse potential, extensive sedative and alcohol interactions) are hard to justify in a common condition such as insomnia without stronger efficacy data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Published results of NCT00383643, and ideally larger Phase 3 trials in insomnia
- The Health Canada package insert, including approved indications, warnings, and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data
- A risk-benefit and abuse-mitigation assessment for a non-orphan population
- Other predicted indications (sleep disorder of initiating and maintaining sleep, restless legs syndrome, and others) have little or no supporting evidence. Restless legs syndrome even shows a possible harm signal, so these are not recommended for pursuit.
