# Tamoxifen: From Breast Cancer to Mammary Paget Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Tamoxifen is a selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM) internationally established for the treatment of estrogen-receptor-positive (ER+) breast cancer. The TxGNN model predicts it may also be effective for **Mammary Paget Disease**, but this signal is currently supported by only **1 clinical trial** and **12 publications**, most of which are case reports and only indirectly relevant to the specific diagnosis.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in this evidence pack (flagged as a High-severity data gap). Based on well-established pharmacological knowledge, Tamoxifen competitively antagonizes estrogen binding at the estrogen receptor (ER), and its efficacy in ER-positive breast cancer has been proven over decades of clinical use, including adjuvant, metastatic, and chemoprevention settings.

Mammary Paget disease is a rare form of breast malignancy that, in the majority of cases, is associated with an underlying ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) or invasive ductal carcinoma, a proportion of which are hormone-receptor positive. Because Paget's disease frequently represents an epidermal extension of an underlying ER-positive breast tumor, Tamoxifen's anti-estrogen mechanism could plausibly extend to hormone-receptor-positive Paget's disease, particularly when co-occurring with an ER+ ductal lesion.

However, the evidence pack's own mechanistic assessment notes an important caveat: Paget cells themselves often show *lower* ER expression than the associated ductal carcinoma, making the mechanistic link indirect rather than disease-specific. This is reflected in the assigned **L4 (mechanistic/preclinical)** evidence level and the **"Research Question"** designation from the scoring pipeline — the hypothesis is biologically plausible but not yet clinically validated for this specific diagnosis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Evidence specific to mammary Paget disease is limited to case reports/case series and one Phase 3 trial that addresses Tamoxifen's endometrial safety rather than its efficacy in Paget's disease itself. The evidence level (L4) and decision stage (S1, "Research Question") indicate this remains a mechanistic hypothesis, not a clinically validated repurposing candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action (MOA) documentation for Tamoxifen (currently a High-severity data gap)
- TFDA/Health Canada label warnings and contraindications, required before any safety pre-screening (Blocking data gap — cannot currently enter S1 safety review)
- Disease-specific studies evaluating Tamoxifen efficacy in mammary Paget disease specifically (not only in the associated underlying ductal carcinoma)
- ER expression data specific to Paget cells, to confirm the mechanistic rationale
- Confirmation of Canadian regulatory/market pathway, given the drug is not currently marketed in Canada
