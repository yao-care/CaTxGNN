# Tamoxifen: From Female Breast Carcinoma to Mammary Paget Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Tamoxifen is an estrogen receptor-modulating drug, originally used to treat female breast carcinoma (including invasive ductal carcinoma and ductal adenocarcinoma of the breast).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Mammary Paget Disease**, but only **1 clinical trial** (which does not test tamoxifen for this condition) and **10 publications** (mostly case reports and case series) are available.
The evidence for this specific prediction is weak, so this report also notes the stronger candidate among the ten predictions: **breast carcinoma in situ**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, tamoxifen is an estrogen receptor antagonist in breast tissue, and its efficacy in hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer is well established.

Mammary Paget disease is usually accompanied by an underlying ductal carcinoma in situ (DCIS) or invasive breast carcinoma, many of which are hormone-receptor-driven. Estrogen receptor blockade is therefore plausibly relevant. However, this is largely an extension of the existing breast cancer indication rather than true repurposing. No dedicated tamoxifen trial exists for Paget disease.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (for mammary Paget disease)

**Rationale:**
The lead prediction rests on case reports, case series and recurrence studies, with no tamoxifen-specific trial. It is essentially an extension of the existing breast cancer indication. Of the ten predictions, only two have Phase 3 support (L1): **breast carcinoma in situ** (TAM-01, NSABP B-24 and IBIS-II) and **ER-positive breast cancer**. Both are guideline-supported or core indications that overlap with the approved scope, so they are closer to label extension or dose optimisation than novel repurposing, and both merit Proceed with Guardrails. Hormone-resistant breast cancer (L3) is a research question only. Basal-like breast cancer, the murine Ehrlich tumour, sebaceous breast carcinoma and vaginal rhabdomyosarcoma have little or no clinical support, and some conflict with the drug's biology.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Canadian package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Hormone receptor (ER) status confirmation of Paget disease and its underlying carcinoma, ideally in a prospective or registry study
- For DCIS, a restriction to ER-positive disease, consideration of low-dose regimens, and monitoring for endometrial cancer and thromboembolism
- A CYP2D6 and drug interaction review before use
