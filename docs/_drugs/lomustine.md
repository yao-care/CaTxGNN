## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for lomustine are not available in the data compiled for this report. From general pharmacology, lomustine is a lipophilic nitrosourea alkylating agent. It damages DNA by cross-linking, which is most harmful to rapidly dividing cells, and it can reach the central nervous system.

These properties make lymphoid malignancies a plausible target, particularly lymphomas involving the brain and spinal cord. Lomustine is already used as part of combination regimens, including oral CCNU-based regimens for AIDS-related lymphoma, and in canine lymphoma protocols such as LOPP (lomustine, vincristine, procarbazine, prednisolone). Its activity in lymphoma has also been explored in primary CNS lymphoma regimens.

The caveat is that almost all of this work involves lomustine as one component of a multi-drug regimen, or involves animals, so the contribution of lomustine alone to lymphosarcoma cannot be separated out. The single study of lymphosarcoma itself (PMID 22888657) is a mouse model, which is why the evidence level is capped at L4.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (not label text):** the data compiled for this report flag kidney disease, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, hyperbilirubinemia (including Gilbert syndrome), drug-induced liver injury, pulmonary fibrosis, and fungal or other infectious diseases. These should be confirmed against the Health Canada product monograph.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found. Major-level examples are cimetidine, deferiprone, samarium (153Sm) lexidronam and adalimumab. Moderate-level examples include mercaptopurine, zidovudine, chloramphenicol, aldesleukin, alemtuzumab and asparaginase. Naltrexone and palifermin are also listed as moderate; levofloxacin is minor.

The package insert warnings and contraindications for the Canadian products have not yet been reviewed. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is high (0.9990), and lomustine is plausible mechanistically and is used within lymphoma combination regimens. However, the only study of lymphosarcoma itself is a mouse model, and the clinical data are small trials or retrospective series in which lomustine cannot be separated from its combination partners. This places the evidence at L4.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the Health Canada product monograph for warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism-of-action data for lomustine
- Clinical data in which lomustine's contribution to lymphoma outcomes is separable from companion drugs
- A safety and myelosuppression monitoring plan, given the high haematological toxicity of nitrosoureas
