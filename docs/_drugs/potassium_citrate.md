# Potassium Citrate: From Potassium Deficiency and Renal Tubular Acidosis to Nephrolithiasis

## One-Sentence Summary

Potassium citrate is an alkalinizing potassium salt originally used for potassium deficiency, renal tubular acidosis and acute urate nephropathy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **nephrolithiasis (kidney stones)**, the highest-ranked prediction with supporting clinical evidence, backed by **14 registered clinical trials** and **15 publications**.
The finding mostly confirms an established use rather than a novel repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, potassium citrate raises urinary pH and urinary citrate levels.

Citrate binds calcium and inhibits the crystallization of calcium salts, and alkalinization dissolves uric acid stones. This is closely related to the original indications (renal tubular acidosis, acute urate nephropathy), where urinary acidity and low citrate are central problems.

Potassium citrate is already a guideline-supported standard therapy for hypocitraturic calcium stones and uric acid stones. The prediction is therefore best read as confirmation of an established use. Guardrails apply: avoid excessive alkalinization, which may promote calcium phosphate stone formation.

**Other predictions.** Most lower-ranked predictions have no supporting data and a weak or absent mechanistic rationale:
- Familial visceral myopathy, mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation disorder, Pendred syndrome, hypermanganesemia with dystonia, autosomal recessive nonsyndromic deafness and leukocyte adhesion deficiency are model predictions only (L5), with no trials or literature. They should be held.
- Nephrolithiasis susceptibility caused by SLC26A1 is biologically coherent (a hyperoxaluria-related stone subtype), but has no subtype-specific data. It is a reasonable research question.
- Cystinosis (L4) and exocrine pancreatic insufficiency (L4) have only indirect evidence. Potassium citrate would act as supportive alkali/potassium replacement, or on a complication (oxalate kidney injury), not on the underlying disease.

## Safety Considerations

**Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph** (not label text; to be verified against the package insert). Conditions listed include:
- Kidney and urinary: kidney disease, impaired renal function, anuria, nephrocalcinosis, pyoureter
- Adrenal: adrenal insufficiency, Addison disease and related adrenal disorders
- Cardiovascular: hypertension, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction
- Metabolic: hypercalcemia, phosphorus metabolism disease, metabolic acidosis, rhabdomyolysis
- Other: peptic ulcer disease, pancreatitis, diarrheal disease, benign prostatic hyperplasia, hemoglobinopathy

**Drug interactions:** The interaction search returned 100 records. The first 20 reviewed include:
- **Major:** amiloride, benazepril, olmesartan, valsartan (hyperkalemia risk with potassium-sparing drugs, ACE inhibitors and ARBs), and anticholinergics/antihistamines such as atropine, hyoscyamine, homatropine, methscopolamine, chlorpheniramine, acrivastine, phenyltoloxamine and amitriptyline
- **Moderate:** aliskiren, ibuprofen, ketorolac, acetylsalicylic acid, doxycycline, pseudoephedrine, amphetamine, dextroamphetamine

Warnings and contraindications from the Canadian package insert were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails** (for nephrolithiasis). Hold the other predictions; treat SLC26A1-related nephrolithiasis as a research question.

**Rationale:**
- Nephrolithiasis has a plausible mechanism (urinary alkalinization and increased citrate), a Phase 2/3 trial, a Phase 3 comparison trial, a Phase 4 study, a meta-analysis and several reviews. It is best seen as confirmation of an established use rather than a new discovery.
- The remaining predictions have either no data or only indirect evidence.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Health Canada package insert warnings, contraindications and approved indication text (currently missing and blocking safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data
- Results of larger randomized trials with stone-recurrence endpoints
- Monitoring plan for serum potassium and renal function, particularly in chronic kidney disease and with potassium-sparing drugs, ACE inhibitors or ARBs
- Care to avoid over-alkalinization, which may promote calcium phosphate stones
- For SLC26A1-related nephrolithiasis, a subtype-specific study
