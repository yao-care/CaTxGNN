## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, lidocaine is a sodium channel blocker used as a local anesthetic. Its efficacy as an anesthetic is well established, and mechanistically it might relieve pain or discomfort on the ocular surface.

Any link to punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis is plausible but unsupported. Blocking sensory nerves could ease symptoms such as pain, but nothing retrieved suggests lidocaine treats the underlying corneal or conjunctival inflammation. Prolonged topical anesthetic use is also known to damage the corneal epithelium, which argues against use in a corneal surface disease.
